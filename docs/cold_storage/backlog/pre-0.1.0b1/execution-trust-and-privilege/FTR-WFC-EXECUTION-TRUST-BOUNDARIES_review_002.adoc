== 1. F-I1 -- authority provenance base case and resolution rule

*RESOLVED.*

All five required properties are stated.

*Authority roots mechanically distinguishable from agent output.* "Policy, a
WorkItem, execution configuration, or equivalent governed input carries
authority only when its provenance is mechanically distinguishable from
agent-produced output." This is the sentence review 001 asked for, and it
attaches the property to the carriers rather than leaving it as a background
assumption.

*Commit, tracking, pinning, and admission do not establish authority.* "Being
committed, tracked, pinned at `HEAD`, structurally valid, or admitted by
ordinary repository checks is not by itself proof that an input is an authority
root." This negates the conflation review 001 identified, and it negates it in
exactly the terms the repository makes true: admission proves tracking at the
pinned HEAD and structural coherence, and `WorkItem.authority_source` remains a
free string (`model.py:89`, `serialization.py:358,370`) that nothing checks. The
enumeration matches the mechanism rather than gesturing at it.

*Effective authority resolves only from independently admitted inputs fixed
before the requester could influence them.* "For a requesting Hop or component,
the effective envelope must be resolved only from authority-bearing inputs fixed
or admitted independently of and before that requester could author or influence
them." This is the resolution rule whose absence review 001 identified as the
missing closure, and the ordering constraint ("before") is explicit.

*Requester, peer, receiver, and generated artifacts cannot bootstrap.*
"Assertions by the requesting worker, a delegating peer, the receiving worker,
or their generated artifacts do not become authority merely by being referenced,
committed, or passed onward." The four roles are enumerated, which closes the
`A -> B -> C` case directly, and "merely by being ... committed" names the
specific pre-split F1 channel: agent output landed by the trusted commit path
and read back as a later Run's governing input.

*Autonomy inside an admitted envelope survives.* "Once authoritatively admitted
under this provenance rule, that envelope supports automation without a new
human act per Hop; widening it requires new authority from its protected root."
This is compatible with the present model, where per-Run maintainer
authorization admits an envelope and the Run then proceeds up to
`max_autonomous_hops`.

=== F-N1 (INFO) -- the delegation clause is now phrased as attenuation rather than as grant authority

The superseded text required that "the delegating component must already have
explicit human-derived authority to grant the specific bounded capability or
context". The revision reads "an agent may delegate only authority it was
explicitly granted". Holding a capability and being authorized to re-delegate it
are not the same property, so read alone this is marginally weaker.

It is not a regression in effect, because the resolution rule dominates: the
receiving component's effective envelope is resolved from independently admitted
inputs, so a peer's purported delegation can at most select within what the
envelope already admits for the receiver. Recorded for precision only.

=== F-N2 (INFO) -- the non-human root disjunct is admitted without its own provenance chain

The envelope may now be "rooted in human/maintainer-derived authority *or* in a
separately governed authority source whose provenance is mechanically protected
from the requesting and delegating components". The generalization is reasonable
and probably necessary outside a single-maintainer repository, and the guard
clause does the needed work for the attack this FTR cares about, since an
agent-produced source fails "mechanically distinguishable from agent-produced
output".

What the record does not say is what makes a source "separately governed", or
that this disjunct must not become the way an automated system terminates its
own chain. For a deferred backlog record that is acceptable; it is worth one
sentence whenever the envelope is next specified.

== 2. F-I2 -- ingress and egress information flow

*RESOLVED.*

The new `Information flow and derived output` section supplies all seven
required properties.

Ingress and egress are named as two predicates in the opening sentence.
Selecting a protected binding is explicitly bounded to input visibility:
"Selecting a protected, local, or self-hosted execution binding authorizes only
its bounded input visibility; it does not authorize arbitrary output to return
to a less-trusted or cloud boundary." Derived output retains protection: "Derived
output retains at least the combined protection requirements of the protected
sources provided to the execution until an explicit, independently authorized
declassification permits disclosure to a lower-trust sink." Laundering channels
are closed by name: "Summarizing, paraphrasing, transforming, regenerating,
logging, committing, or copying information is not itself declassification."
Self-declassification is denied outright, with the authority located: "the
producing agent cannot self-declassify", and "Declassification authority must
come from the admitted human/policy-owned envelope."

Two details are better than review 001 asked for.

The join is taken over "the protected sources *provided to the execution*", not
over sources the producing agent cites. That is the correction the pre-split T4
made, and taking it now avoids computing a label from untrusted `evidence_refs`.
"At least" makes the rule a floor rather than a ceiling, which is the fail-closed
direction.

The sink list is concrete rather than generic, and it matches the surfaces review
001 established as live: "model prompts and context, summaries and Findings,
generated files, review artifacts, logs, diagnostics and scratch space,
Run/checkpoint/evidence state, commit subjects and messages, and other
Coordinator-owned durable output". `Hop.summary` reaching `checkpoint.json`
(`persistence.py:201`), `run.json`, `execution-evidence.json`, commit subjects,
and publishable backlog review artifacts are all inside that enumeration.

Neutrality is preserved explicitly: "Future implementation may use labels and
authority comparison, but this rule defines no classification lattice, schema,
content scanner, DLP engine, or taint-tracking mechanism."

The weak clause in `Deployment implication` was replaced rather than left to
coexist: "Output may return to the ordinary workflow only under the
information-egress and declassification rule above." Scenario C is now decided by
the text.

== 3. F-I3 -- defensive-review trigger

*RESOLVED.*

Both records now carry the same trigger in near-identical words. Trust FTR: "If
a phase receives mutation or execution authority over a surface classified by the
admitted authority envelope as requiring elevated handling, the explicit
elevated-privilege workflow and mandatory defensive review ... apply."
Elevated-privilege FTR: "The mandatory trigger is a phase receiving mutation or
execution authority over a surface that the admitted authority envelope
classifies as requiring elevated handling." The elevated FTR's thesis and its
`Relationship to execution trust boundaries` section were re-keyed to the same
formulation, so the divergence is closed at all four places it appeared rather
than patched at one.

Self-assessment is denied in both records and in three places in the trust FTR:
"not the worker's claim about whether its change can influence something
trusted", "The worker cannot waive review; any exemption must come from the
governing authority that classified or granted the elevation", and, in the
candidate-design section, "the worker's consequence assessment does not".

The "influence a more trusted boundary" language survives exactly where it
belongs. It is gone as a trigger the worker evaluates, and retained unchanged as
the containment invariant the system must enforce: "Before defensive review
succeeds, modified privileged state must not influence execution in a more
trusted or more privileged boundary." Separating the invariant from the trigger
is the right split and is the substance of the fix.

Pure visibility elevation is now exempted in the elevated FTR too, closing the
cross-record ambiguity: "Pure visibility elevation is governed by the
pre-disclosure information-boundary rules in the execution-trust-boundaries FTR,
not by mandatory defensive review merely because protected data was read." The
combined case retains both control sets ("both controls apply"), so Scenario D
still resolves; the superseded emphasis clause "a compatible information boundary
does not remove the review requirement" is dropped, which slightly softens the
statement without changing the rule.

Aspects no longer determine topology: "An `aspect` describes what kind of
authority is elevated; it does not by itself determine which control topology
applies." The two aspects review 001 named as read-shaped are dispositioned
directly -- "Sensitive data or network capability may implicate visibility,
mutation, execution, or multiple dimensions" -- and the trust FTR adds the
matching clause, "Network capability is not intrinsically assigned to either
route".

=== F-M1 (MINOR) -- the classification-based trigger has a completeness precondition and no stated fail-closed default

The trigger now fires when the admitted envelope classifies a surface as
requiring elevated handling. That correctly removes the worker's judgement from
the control path. It also makes the control silent wherever the envelope
classifies nothing, and the record does not say what happens then.

The seam is narrow but concrete, and review 001 already recorded its present
instance. `pyproject.toml` carries `[tool.pytest.ini_options]`, sits inside
`work_item_scope_ceiling` in `governance/self-development/repository-policy.yaml`,
and the Coordinator executes `python -m pytest -q` in the agent-mutated checkout
after the Hop (`vertical.py:254-256`). Nothing in governance classifies that
surface as requiring elevated handling. Under the superseded consequence-shaped
trigger such an edit was caught, badly, by a predicate the worker assessed; under
the revised trigger it is caught only if the envelope says so.

Three things keep this MINOR rather than a reopening. The escalation requirement
is independent of the classification and is keyed to a category the record itself
enumerates: "When a task genuinely requires mutation of an execution-sensitive
surface, the agent must explicitly escalate", with `Indirect-execution threat
model` listing Git hooks, CI configuration, test discovery/configuration,
`conftest.py`, Makefiles, build hooks, packaging hooks, container configuration,
and scripts later run by a trusted service. "No automatic unlock" and the
distinguishable-run requirement are unchanged. And the elevated FTR's containment
invariant still binds anything that did escalate.

The fix is one clause whenever the envelope is specified: an envelope that does
not classify a surface the threat model names as execution-sensitive is a gap in
the envelope, not an exemption from review. Recorded so that the classification
dependency is visible to whoever implements it; it does not change the F-I3
disposition, and every alternative trigger considered in review 001 was worse.

== Final statement

Commit `b7adc7759aae35d151fe81df9335b27d18b09cd3` resolves all three IMPORTANT
findings of review 001. Authority provenance now has a base case and a resolution
rule, and states both in terms the repository makes true. Information authority
now has an egress half, with the join taken over provided sources, laundering
channels closed by name, self-declassification denied, and the Coordinator's own
durable state and commit metadata recognized as sinks. The defensive-review
trigger is identical in both records, is governed by admitted classification
rather than worker judgement, and leaves the containment invariant intact where
it belongs.

The strongest property of the revision is that it fixed the trigger by splitting
it: "can influence a more trusted boundary" was the wrong question to ask a
worker and the right invariant to impose on a system, and the revision now asks
it only of the system. The residual concern is the mirror of that choice -- a
classification-driven trigger is silent on surfaces nobody classified (F-M1) --
and it is a wording gap in a deferred record, not a broken invariant.

`FTR-WFC-EXECUTION-TRUST-BOUNDARIES` is accepted as preserved backlog
architecture and remains suitable to stay deferred. No implementation is
authorized or obligated by this record.
