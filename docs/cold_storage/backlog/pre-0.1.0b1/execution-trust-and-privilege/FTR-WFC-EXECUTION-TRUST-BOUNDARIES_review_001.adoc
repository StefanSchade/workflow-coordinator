== Verdict

*MATERIAL_REVISION_REQUIRED.*

The basic separation of visibility, mutation, and execution authority is sound
and is the right decomposition; nothing found argues for rejection. The
provenance invariant added by `cf68a1b` is the correct invariant and is stated
in the correct direction. Three security invariants nevertheless need bounded
conceptual correction before this record can be relied on as the preserved
design direction: the provenance chain has no stated base case and no stated
resolution rule (F-I1); the egress/return half of information authority is
present as one clause and its prior formulation survives only in an explicitly
non-authoritative directory (F-I2); and the trigger for mandatory defensive
review is stated two different ways across the two records, one of which is a
predicate the mutating agent could assess for itself (F-I3).

Each correction is a small number of sentences inside the existing sections. No
finding asks for IAM, capability tokens, DLP, a classification taxonomy, a
policy engine, a provider router, or a sandbox design, and none of them is
resolved by adding such a platform.

Finding counts: BLOCKING 0, IMPORTANT 3, MINOR 3, INFO 3.

== 1. Authority provenance

The `cf68a1b` paragraph is mechanically clear on five of the six required
propositions. "Agents may request additional visibility or capability, but they
do not create authority" states request/grant separation; "A higher loop may
evaluate a request and select or delegate to a compatible binding only within
that envelope; it must not invent or widen the envelope" denies the higher loop
manufacturing power; "agent-to-agent delegation cannot bootstrap authority: the
delegating component must already have explicit human-derived authority to grant
the specific bounded capability or context" denies peer-granted authority; and
the closing pair "Requesting authority is not granting it, and selecting within
authority is not widening it" names the select/widen distinction as a first-class
rule rather than leaving it to inference.

The envelope shape is also not speculative. `_admit_a1` already enforces the same
domination relation in two dimensions: `work_item.scope` must lie within
`policy.work_item_scope_ceiling`, and `work_item.max_autonomous_hops` must not
exceed `policy.max_autonomous_hops`, both fail-closed at admission
(`src/workflow_coordinator/operator.py:102-165`). `governance/self-development/repository-policy.yaml`
carries both fields at `schema_version: 3`. The FTR's envelope invariant is
therefore a generalization of an enforced relation to further dimensions
(visibility, execution boundary, network), not a new abstraction. That is the
strongest evidence that the invariant is implementable, and it is worth the FTR
saying so in one clause.

Scenario A resolves correctly: a peer agent saying "approved" is a delegation,
and the delegation clause requires the delegating component to already hold
explicit human-derived authority for that exact bounded context, so the peer's
assertion is inert. Scenario F resolves correctly: "I need network access" is a
request; `requires_network` is listed as a request event, not a grant, and the
envelope sentence covers "network capability" by name. Scenario B resolves
correctly: selection between two pre-admitted bindings is explicitly not
widening, so no new human act is required.

=== F-I1 (IMPORTANT) -- the provenance chain is stated as a prohibition, without a base case or a resolution rule

The invariant terminates in "an authority envelope ultimately derived from
previously admitted human/maintainer-authorized policy, WorkItem, execution
configuration, or equivalent governed input". Two things that the chain's
soundness depends on are absent.

First, the base case. "Previously admitted" and "human/maintainer-authorized"
are joined into one phrase as if they were the same property. In this repository
they are not. Admission proves that a governing input is tracked at the pinned
HEAD and structurally coherent; it does not prove authorship. `WorkItem.authority_source`
is a free string (`model.py:89`), is carried through serialization
(`serialization.py:358,370`), and is never checked -- the committed fixtures
read `authority_source: fixture` and `authority_source: accepted task`. The
pre-split review recorded this as F1 and it remains open: the Coordinator's own
trusted commit can satisfy the "committed at pinned HEAD" proof, and Git author
identity does not separate Coordinator commits from maintainer commits
(`docs/imported/core-incubator/agent_workflow_coordinator_concept_followup_outer_loop_authority_review_2026-09-14.adoc`,
F1). A chain whose every link forbids widening still yields agent-created
authority if its first link can be agent-authored.

Second, the resolution rule. The FTR says who may *not* widen; it does not say
what the evaluator reads when deciding what the envelope contains. This is the
missing closure for the circular case `A -> B -> C`. The delegation clause stops
B from granting, but nothing states that the entity evaluating C's claim must
resolve the envelope from an input that A, B, and C could not author. The
pre-split F7 already has the sentence in usable form -- "a recommendation becomes
authority only through an input the recommending Hop cannot author -- a human,
trusted policy pinned before the Hop, or a separately declared gate phase" --
and the pre-split T4 states the read-side analogue, that the label must not be
computed from agent-cited sources because `evidence_refs` are untrusted input.

Two sentences close both facets: state that an input carries admitted authority
only when its provenance is mechanically distinguishable from agent output, and
that the envelope must be resolved from inputs pinned before the requesting Hop
rather than from any assertion made by the requesting, delegating, or receiving
component. Neither sentence designs a schema, and the FTR can keep declining to
design one; "policy", "WorkItem", and "execution configuration" are adequate as
carrier names precisely because `governance/` already instantiates them.

Current exposure is procedural rather than open: `_admit_a1` rejects any phase
`authorized_scope` overlapping `policy.governance_paths`, the ceiling excludes
`governance/`, `git_facts.py:372` raises `pinned policy/profile mutation` as an
anomaly, and `docs/README.adoc` states that "Committed governance alone neither
starts nor authorizes a Run; each real Run additionally requires explicit
maintainer authorization." The gap is forward-looking, which is exactly the
horizon this record exists to serve.

== 2. Who defines the trust boundary

Adequately established for the read side. "The ordinary worker must not be able
to widen its own visibility, and prompt instructions are semantic guidance
rather than containment" denies the agent the boundary definition directly;
"Protected paths or data must instead be absent, sealed, or otherwise
technically inaccessible to an incompatible execution boundary" moves the
decision into the environment; and "it does not authorize the ordinary worker to
reveal or mount the protected context for itself" forecloses the specific
"I should run locally, therefore I may read X" move named in the brief.

"This path is safe to mount" and "this network destination is acceptable" are
covered by the envelope sentence, which names visibility, mutation, execution,
network capability, and execution-boundary trust as the dimensions that may not
be self-widened.

"This data is not confidential" is the one listed decision the record does not
reach. On the read side it is moot, because unreachable data cannot be
misclassified into reach. On the write side it is live, and it is handled below
as part of F-I2: an agent declaring its own derived output unprotected is a
declassification, and declassification authority is not addressed.

== 3. Visibility versus mutation/execution

The distinction is sound, and the non-obvious half of it is correct.

Mandatory defensive review is rightly not triggered by protected reading alone.
The FTR gives the right reason rather than a convenient one: "A later review
cannot undo disclosure to an unauthorized provider or execution boundary."
Control for pure visibility therefore has to be placed before disclosure, which
is what "selecting a compatible technical information boundary *before*
protected information is exposed" says. A review-shaped control after the fact
would be theatre.

The combined case is explicit and closes the escape the brief asked about:
"When work needs both protected visibility and execution-sensitive mutation,
both controls apply; a compatible information boundary does not remove the
review requirement." Scenario D resolves correctly and by direct quotation --
the local confidential worker that also edits `conftest.py` gets both control
sets, and the compatible information boundary buys it nothing on the mutation
side.

The escape that does remain is not through the read rule but through the trigger
predicate; see F-I3.

== 4. Technical containment

Sufficient for a backlog concept, and correctly ordered. The candidate list
covers filesystem visibility, readable/writable separation
("constraining writable paths separately from readable paths"), Git metadata,
indirect-execution-sensitive surfaces, ambient credentials, network/egress,
separate execution environments for agent-authored code, and trusted test/build
services with fixed entry points and least authority. `bwrap` is named as "one
candidate Linux mechanism, not a required implementation" and appears again in
the non-goals, so no mechanism is mandated.

Prompt instructions are treated as semantic guidance rather than containment in
both records, and the elevated-privilege FTR states the migration direction
explicitly: the future mechanism "must replace *reliance on prompt-only
enforcement* for a privileged boundary crossing", while prompts continue to
describe semantic authority. The preference for "technically unavailable" over
"instructed not to access" is stated as a rule, not an aspiration.

=== F-N3 (INFO) -- reachable-surface identity is the honest disclosure bound and the containment list stops just short of it

"Making protected or unneeded paths unavailable rather than merely prohibited"
is the right instinct, but the surface a boundary actually provides is not
identified by the tracked tree. Ignored content is invisible to
`_observe_repository` by construction (`status --porcelain --untracked-files=all`
respects ignore rules), and `.git` metadata carries other branches, reflog,
remote URLs, and stashes. The pre-split T1/T3 recorded the consequence:
disclosure may be claimed only for a surface the Coordinator constructed or
verified, and a manifest of the form "checkout at HEAD X" is provably incomplete
because two Runs at the same HEAD have different surfaces depending on local
residue. The FTR names Git metadata but not ignored or out-of-tree residue.
Adding "ignored and untracked residue" to the same clause costs one phrase and
preserves the distinction between an exported tree and a live checkout for
whoever implements this. Nothing to correct in the concept.

== 5. Confused deputy and indirect execution

The strongest section of the record. The threat model is stated as its own
section rather than folded into containment, the surface list is complete for
the purpose (Git hooks, CI configuration, test discovery/configuration,
`conftest.py`, Makefiles, build hooks, packaging hooks, Docker build and runtime
configuration, scripts later run by a trusted maintainer or service), the
mechanism is named correctly ("A later trusted test/build service executes that
artifact with protected data, credentials, filesystem access, or network
access"), and the conclusion is the one that matters: "Path scope alone does not
express this trust transition."

Scenario E composes correctly across the two records. The trust FTR forbids
automatic unlock and requires escalation plus a run distinguishable from
ordinary sandboxed runs; the elevated-privilege FTR supplies the invariant that
closes the maintainer-side hole by name -- "a newly written Git hook running
inside the agent sandbox but not through a more privileged host-side Git
operation before review" -- and generalizes it as "Before defensive review
succeeds, modified privileged state must not influence execution in a more
trusted or more privileged boundary." A restricted agent conceptually cannot
cause a more privileged process to act for it before privileged review.

=== F-M3 (MINOR) -- the threat is already instantiated in this Runtime, and the revisit trigger implies it is still hypothetical

"Trusted test-service boundary" describes a future service, and "Deferred
priority and revisit trigger" lists "trusted test services, CI mutation" among
the conditions that would make the record concrete. One instance is concrete
now. After the Hop and its trusted commit, `vertical.py:254-256` executes the
policy-admitted evidence commands in the agent-mutated checkout under
`CWD_POLICY = "canonical-repository-root-v1"` and
`ENVIRONMENT_POLICY = "coordinator-inherited-environment-v1"`
(`evidence.py:23-24`). The self-development policy's evidence command is
`python -m pytest -q`, and `pyproject.toml` -- which carries
`[tool.pytest.ini_options]` with `testpaths` and `addopts` -- is inside
`work_item_scope_ceiling`. Argv digest and executable stat fingerprint are
pinned, so the *command* is pinned; what pytest loads is not, and `addopts`
alone can load a plugin.

This does not change the concept, which already covers "test
discovery/configuration" and services. It changes the deferral premise: the
record defers partly on the ground that these requirements are not yet
concrete. Current mitigation is genuine -- path scope plus maintainer-supervised
self-development -- and is the same posture prior reviews accepted. Naming the
in-Run evidence execution as the nearest existing instance of the modelled
transition would sharpen the revisit trigger and cost one sentence.

== 6. Information egress and the return path

The record has one clause: "Only results permitted by policy return to the
ordinary workflow." Its shape is fail-closed and its placement is inside
"Deployment implication", where it reads as a remark about a delegation topology
rather than as an invariant of the architecture. It is not enough, and this is
the finding the brief was right to press.

=== F-I2 (IMPORTANT) -- information authority is stated as an ingress predicate; the egress half is one clause and its prior formulation survives only where it is explicitly non-authoritative

Three specific gaps sit behind the single clause.

*Derived output has no stated label.* The FTR does not say what relation holds
between a protected input and an artifact derived from it. Without that
relation, "results permitted by policy" has no subject: a summary of proprietary
source is not obviously a "result" carrying protection. The pre-split F4 stated
the rule in its minimal form -- derived items inherit the join of their sources,
a lower label may be assigned only by an explicit human/policy-owned
declassification recorded as provenance, and dropping an item from a disclosure
set is not declassification -- and T4 added the necessary qualification that the
join must be taken over the provided surface, not over agent-cited sources.
Scenario C is decided by that rule and by nothing currently in the FTR.

*Sinks are not enumerated, and several are already live.* The return path is not
only the next phase's prompt. `Hop.summary`, `Finding` prose, `RoutingResult`
summaries, and the CommitIntent subject are agent-authored prose that reaches
`run.json`, `checkpoint.json` (`persistence.py:201`), `execution-evidence.json`,
and commit subjects; `docs/README.adoc` classifies backlog review artifacts as
preserved planning evidence and the incubator review instructions assume review
artifacts may be committed to a public repository. The pre-split T2 named the
consequence precisely: "the exfiltration path is the sanctioned effect, not a
bypass", because `trusted_commit` validates paths, subject, and worktree facts
and has no notion of content or audience. An FTR that lists the laundering
channels the brief names -- summaries, generated files, logs, prompts, scratch,
model output -- and states that Coordinator-owned durable state and commit
subjects are themselves sinks, preserves the direction; one that does not,
leaves an implementer to rediscover it.

*The prior formulation is not preserved anywhere authoritative.* T2's egress
rule is expressed in a form that explicitly avoids the platform this review is
told to resist -- every sink carries an audience label, derived output may land
in a sink only if its label is dominated by the sink's audience, "No content
scanner or DLP is implied; the check compares two labels". That text lives in
`docs/imported/core-incubator/`, which `docs/README.adoc` classifies as
"pre-split historical evidence, not current standalone authority", and a grep
across `docs/` outside `imported/` finds no reference to it; `project_memory.adoc`
does not carry it either. This backlog FTR is in practice the only
forward-carrying record of the direction, which makes the omission a
preservation failure rather than a redundancy.

The correction is two or three sentences and a cross-reference: state that
information authority has an ingress and an egress predicate; state that derived
output carries its source's protection until an authority inside the same
envelope declassifies it, and that summarizing, regenerating, or logging is not
declassification; state that the Coordinator's own durable state and commits are
sinks. Selecting a protected binding then authorizes input visibility only, which
is the proposition the brief asked to be made explicit and which the current text
implies but never says.

== 7. Local or self-hosted execution

Treated as a binding, not as a trust decision. "Local/self-hosted execution is
one possible boundary, not a provider-specific rule" is explicit; the delegation
target is "a bounded execution binding compatible with the information, such as
a local or self-hosted model", where the load-bearing word is "bounded" and
local appears as an example; the non-goals include "require all development to
be self-hosted"; and the compatible worker is required to have "network, egress,
and credentials bounded consistently with the information's sensitivity"
regardless of where it runs. The record does not say `local == trusted`.

=== F-M2 (MINOR) -- compatibility is never said to be a property of the enforced boundary rather than of the hosting location, and the retention dimension is missing

The proposition is carried by example and by adjective; it is never stated. One
clause -- compatibility is a property of what the boundary enforces, not of where
it runs -- would make it unmissable, and is cheap because the record already has
the bounding list to point at.

That list is also short by one dimension. It covers filesystem visibility,
credentials, network, and egress, which is most of what distinguishes a
compatible boundary; it does not cover retention -- logging, telemetry, scratch,
and unintended persistence. For a provider choice retention is implicit in
"disclosure to an unauthorized provider", which the record does name. For a local
process it is the dimension most likely to be assumed away, and it is where a
local model with a verbose log or a cached session quietly stops being
compatible. Adding "retention, logging, and persistence" to the existing bounding
clause is a phrase, not a secret-management architecture.

== 8. Model and provider binding

Compatible with the later separation, and prematurely designs none of it. The
record speaks of an "execution binding" and a "compatible execution boundary"
without fixing how a binding is named, selected, or scored; the non-goals
exclude "delegation/router architecture"; and the deployment section disclaims
"an outer-loop planner, automatic model router, provider selection algorithm, or
new workflow profile". The higher loop's power is confined to selection inside
the envelope by the provenance paragraph, which is the one constraint that has
to survive into whatever binding architecture arrives.

=== F-N2 (INFO) -- the binding concept now partially answers an open question in the companion record

The elevated-privilege FTR's open questions include "Do elevated phases require a
distinct provider or model policy?". The trust FTR's binding-compatibility
direction supplies the shape of that answer -- a phase-declared requirement
checked fail-closed against binding facts -- without answering it. This is
healthy composition, not a conflict. Worth knowing when either record is next
revised; nothing to correct.

== 9. Delegation

The topology preserves least authority, and the one sentence that makes it work
is present: the delegation "does not authorize the ordinary worker to reveal or
mount the protected context for itself". The requesting agent therefore does not
acquire the protected context by requesting it -- it reports a requirement and
the work moves, which is the property that distinguishes this topology from an
escalation ladder. "The compatible worker receives only the visibility needed for
its task" bounds the delegate. Widening is denied by the envelope sentence, and
nested delegation is denied by the bootstrap clause, which binds the delegating
component at every level rather than only the first, so recursion gains nothing.

The remaining leg is the return path, which F-I2 covers: nothing currently stops
return data from exceeding the ordinary workflow's visibility authority except
the unelaborated clause "Only results permitted by policy return to the ordinary
workflow".

== 10. Human authorization semantics

The envelope model is expressed, and per-Hop human approval is not required. The
deployment section says "a future higher loop *or* human operator may make the
authority/policy decision", and the provenance paragraph permits the higher loop
to "evaluate a request and select or delegate to a compatible binding" inside the
envelope. Human involvement returns only for widening: "it must not invent or
widen the envelope or independently authorize new disclosure".

This also matches the system as it stands. `docs/README.adoc` records that
governance is "current authority only within their explicit declared ceilings"
and that each Run additionally requires explicit maintainer authorization, after
which the Run proceeds autonomously up to `max_autonomous_hops`. The admitted
envelope is already the unit of human authorization at Run granularity; the FTR
extends the same granularity to visibility and execution-boundary dimensions
rather than introducing a per-delegation gate. Scenario B is decided by the text
alone and needs no new human act.

== 11. Relationship to the defensive-review record

The intended division holds in outline. The trust FTR defines containment,
indirect execution, and trust transitions; the elevated-privilege FTR defines the
escalation/work/mandatory-review topology and the invariant that privileged state
must not influence a more trusted boundary before review. Each points at the
other by path, the elevated FTR states the complementarity in a dedicated
section, and its non-goals include "duplicate or supersede the technical-containment
work in `FTR-WFC-EXECUTION-TRUST-BOUNDARIES`". Neither silently supersedes the
other, and their vocabularies do not collide: envelope, higher loop, binding, and
compatible boundary are the trust FTR's terms; escalation, elevated work,
privilege payload, and aspect are the elevated FTR's.

The rules do collide in one place.

=== F-I3 (IMPORTANT) -- the trigger for mandatory defensive review is stated two different ways, and one of them is self-assessable

The trust FTR keys the mandatory control to a consequence: "Mutation or
execution-authority elevation *that can influence a more trusted boundary* is
additionally subject to the explicit elevated-privilege workflow and mandatory
defensive review". The elevated-privilege FTR keys it to the crossing itself:
"Some work legitimately crosses an ordinary-agent authority boundary. The generic
workflow pattern for such work is explicit privilege escalation, bounded
elevated-privilege work, and mechanically mandatory defensive review", with "no
ordinary route from elevated-privilege work that bypasses the defensive review".

Two consequences follow.

*The consequence-shaped trigger is a predicate someone must evaluate, and the
record does not say who may not.* "Can influence a more trusted boundary" is
precisely the judgement the indirect-execution section spends a page showing is
hard -- the whole point of that section is that a surface's downstream influence
is non-obvious. Left as written, an implementer or an agent may reason that a
particular execution-surface edit cannot influence anything more trusted and
thereby skip a mandatory control, which is the same shape of escape the read rule
was carefully drafted to avoid. The trigger should be the class of surface
mutated, with "cannot influence a more trusted boundary" available only as a
fail-closed determination made by the authority that granted the elevation, never
by the mutating agent.

*The elevated FTR's aspect list contains read-shaped aspects that the trust FTR
routes away from mandatory review.* Its illustrative aspects include "sensitive or
realistic test data" and "network capability". The trust FTR names "realistic
protected test or staging data" as a subject of pure visibility elevation, for
which it explicitly states that mandatory defensive review is not required, and
lists `requires_network` beside `requires_protected_read` in the same candidate
vocabulary. A reader routing "the agent must read realistic protected test data"
gets mandatory review from one record and no mandatory review from the other.

Both records are reconcilable, because the elevated FTR calls its list
"illustrative" and "not a closed taxonomy" and the trust FTR supplies the real
discriminator. Reconciling them should not be left to the reader: one sentence in
the elevated FTR stating that the aspect names what was elevated while the
mutation/execution criterion decides whether its workflow applies, and one
sentence in the trust FTR making the influence predicate an authority
determination rather than a property to be argued, removes the divergence.

=== F-M1 (MINOR) -- "an authority must authorize" is the one phrase that, read alone, admits agent self-authorization

`b59ba54` replaced "a maintainer/operator must authorize the widened context"
with "an authority must authorize the widened capability". The genericization is
correct and deliberate -- it is what makes autonomous selection inside an
envelope possible, per section 10 -- but the replacement term is undefined at its
point of use. "An authority" is repaired two paragraphs earlier by the provenance
sentence, and a reader who arrives at "Candidate design direction" through the
table of contents does not have it. Binding the phrase to the envelope where it
occurs, for example "an authority within the admitted envelope", costs four words
and removes the only sentence in the document a motivated reader could quote in
support of a peer-granted unlock.

=== F-N1 (INFO) -- an accepted backlog record already depends on this one's containment, and this record does not acknowledge the dependency

`FTR-WFC-ATOMIC-HOP-RECOVERY.adoc`, accepted at rereview 002, gates its R2
same-Hop retry on containment work defined here: its residual-risk section states
that current primitives cannot prove "no unresolved side effect" and names
escaped descendant processes, writes outside Git or the repository, network and
external-service mutation, credentials, and mutable caches, then makes R2 depend
on the direction in `FTR-WFC-EXECUTION-TRUST-BOUNDARIES.adoc`. This record's
"Relationship to existing WFC boundaries" lists declarative phase authority,
mutation scope, Coordinator-owned Git mutation, trusted evidence, and independent
review, but not the recovery record. A backlog record that another backlog record
blocks on should say so, so that revisiting this one surfaces what unblocks.
Observation only.

== 12. Minimality and backlog fitness

Appropriately bounded, and the boundary is defended in the text rather than left
to good intentions. The non-goals exclude mandating `bwrap`, implementing
sandboxing, a complete information-classification taxonomy, a delegation/router
architecture, replacing repository policy or prompt/contract authority or review,
guaranteeing protection against all malicious code, a complete secret-management
system, and requiring self-hosted development. The deployment section separately
disclaims an outer-loop planner, model router, provider selection algorithm, and
new workflow profile. The event vocabulary is marked "Candidate, non-final" and
followed by "This record neither implements nor schema-commits that vocabulary."
The priority line and revisit trigger keep the record deferred and name the
conditions that would reopen it.

Nothing in the document drifts toward IAM, capability tokens, DLP, a policy
engine, or a sandbox implementation. Length is 168 lines, which is proportionate
to a record whose purpose is to keep a direction from being lost.

The three IMPORTANT findings do not reverse this judgement, and none of them is
answered by building a platform. F-I1 asks for a base case and a resolution rule
-- two sentences, both already drafted in prior repository text. F-I2 asks for an
egress predicate whose prior formulation was explicitly constructed to avoid DLP.
F-I3 asks for a trigger restatement across two records. Adding all three leaves
the record under two hundred lines and still deferred.

This FTR is suitable to remain deferred backlog architecture, and is useful for
future implementation planning in its current shape; the revisions asked for here
make it reliable as the thing later planning is built on, which is the standard
the verdict applies.

== Final statement

`FTR-WFC-EXECUTION-TRUST-BOUNDARIES` at `cf68a1b` is a coherent and
appropriately bounded backlog architecture concept whose central decomposition
survives attack. Its strongest contribution is the pairing of the three-dimension
separation with the non-obvious routing call that follows from it: protected
reading alone does not earn a mandatory defensive review, because the only
effective control for disclosure precedes disclosure, while any work that also
touches an execution-sensitive surface earns both control sets. That is the
correct call and the record states the correct reason for it. The provenance
invariant added by `cf68a1b` is the right invariant, stated in the right
direction, and shaped like a domination relation `_admit_a1` already enforces.

The strongest remaining security concern is egress. Selecting a compatible
protected binding is a decision about what a worker may *receive*; the record
