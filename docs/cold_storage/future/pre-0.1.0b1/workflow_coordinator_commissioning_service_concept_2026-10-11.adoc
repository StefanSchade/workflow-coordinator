== 2. Repository evidence

=== 2.1 The tier already exists and a human occupies it

`docs/wfc_operator_manual.adoc` §18 already draws four tiers, not three:

[source,text]
----
higher orchestration / planning / curation   (not designed here)
    -> Commission / execution choice          (maintainer-owned)
    -> Harness v0 deterministic mechanics      (this manual)
    -> WFC Run                                 (WFC-owned semantic execution)
----

The second tier is the hypothesized Commissioning Service. It is not missing; the
maintainer plus an interactive assistant occupy it. §18 lists what it does and
Harness v0 deliberately does not: estimate task difficulty, choose semantic
execution strategy, decompose tasks, author Commissions, and "decide which model
to use semantically (it only records the maintainer's explicit `--role-binding`)".
`successor_continuation_runbook_2026-10-10.adoc` §7 lists the same tier as "what
remains manual today". The friction record prices it.

=== 2.2 The complaint about leaked execution detail is true -- and the refusal is deliberate

`scripts/wfc_op.py:1056-1059` requires, per profile role,
`--role-binding ROLE=ADAPTER:MODEL:REASONING`, documented "repeatable; one per
profile role. No automatic selection."; `ROLE_BINDING_RE` (`wfc_op.py:324`) parses
exactly the `codex_cli:<model>:<reasoning>` shape the hypothesis objects to. It is
a flag an operator types, not even a committed artifact.

The refusal of automatic selection is a decision, not an oversight:
`write_execution_bindings` (`wfc_op.py:338-341`) states "from explicit maintainer
inputs only. No automatic model/provider selection, no inferred fallback", and
`FTR-WFC-ROLE-PERSONA-EXECUTION-BINDING` states "There is no registry, fallback
chain, score, price, or automatic router", naming "automatic model/provider
routing, fallback, escalation, scoring, or cost optimization" a non-goal. A
proposal to resolve models from semantic need must argue against that decision,
not around it.

=== 2.3 Execution binding is already separated -- and deliberately outside governed inputs

Implemented: `ExecutionBinding(adapter, model, reasoning)` and
`RunExecutionConfiguration` (`model.py:170-184`), "Immutable role-keyed MVP
projection prepared once for one Run"; parsed once by
`run_execution_configuration_from_yaml` (`serialization.py:973`, `adapter` must
equal `codex_cli`); binding roles must equal profile roles exactly
(`operator.py:341-353`).

One fact carries much of this analysis. The binding source is read from the
filesystem (`operator.py:304-310`), whereas the five governing inputs -- WorkItem,
profile, policy, components, task -- are read pinned at `expected_head` and
carried in the `governing` digest map re-verified between Hops
(`operator.py:311-314`, `441-456`). The binding is deliberately *not* a governing
input, on the stated ground that it "grants no scope, action, route, information,
or Finding authority". That premise is conditional; see §10.3.

=== 2.4 The three axes already exist, with selector seams reserved

`FTR-WFC-ROLE-PERSONA-EXECUTION-BINDING` decided three independent concepts --
workflow role, persona, execution binding -- and reserved, without implementing,
selector seams illustrated as `persona selector = frontend_developer` and
`execution binding selector = elevated_local`. Two of its rules are treated as
binding here: "subject expertise must not select a provider", and "an eligible

=== 2.6 Thirty-six profiles: five raw signatures, reducing to two repeating units

Independently reproduced over `governance/self-development/*-profile.yaml` at
the amendment baseline (36 files, clean worktree), in raw YAML phase order,
signing each phase `WORK` or `GATE:<breadth>`: 124 phases; 12 distinct
*phase-name* sets; five raw WORK/GATE *signatures* -- 20
`WORK, GATE:focused, WORK, GATE:focused`; 7 `WORK, GATE:focused`; 5
`WORK, GATE:broad, WORK, GATE:focused`; 2 `GATE:focused, WORK, GATE:focused`;
2 `GATE:focused, WORK`. An earlier draft of this record reported eleven
phase-name sets and four signatures and omitted the two gate-first,
two-phase profiles; independent review 001 (§2.3, F-M5) caught this, and the
figures above are a fresh independent reproduction against the repository,
not a copy of the review's numbers.

The four gate-first profiles are real, currently admitted continuation
Commissions: each has a `WorkItem.initial_phase` entering directly at
`review`, `independent_review`, or `rereview`, resuming after a predecessor
Run's work phase already executed
(`correction-convergence-escape-implementation-s3-002`,
`projected-result-contracts-p1-assurance-closure`, and two of
`correction-convergence-escape-implementation-s{3,4}-003`).

Normalizing by phase *position* and relative route target, rather than by
phase *name*, goes further than the raw signature count, but the target is a
logical graph, not the raw materialized order, and the two must be kept
distinct. Verified directly against every phase's `routes`: *every* gate's
`accept` route completes -- first gate and final gate alike, not only the
final one -- and every gate's `correct`/`correction` route targets the same
single correction-WORK phase of its cycle. Whether that target sits before or
after the gate in *raw YAML order* depends on where the profile starts: in a
profile that materializes WORK before its first gate (the common case), the
first gate's route to the correction phase is backward/preceding, exactly as
the second gate's loop back to it also is; in the four gate-first
continuation profiles, no WORK phase is materialized before the first (or,
for the two two-phase cases, only) gate at all -- the predecessor Run already
executed it -- so that gate's `correct`/`correction` route is necessarily
*forward*, to a WORK phase that follows it in raw order, never to a preceding
one. All five raw signatures reduce, on this corrected basis, to one logical
cycle occurring once (9 profiles, including the two gate-first two-phase
truncations) or twice (27 profiles, including the two gate-first four-phase
truncations). `breadth` (`focused`/`broad`) is a property of the gate's
review field, not of the graph shape -- both values occur inside the
otherwise-identical twice-occurring cycle. One profile
(`standalone-developer-ergonomics`) adds a route this reduction does not
cover: its first gate can advance directly to the second gate, bypassing the
correction phase. That is one additional edge in one profile out of 36, not a
third base unit, but §9's grammar does not yet name it, and normalization to
"one base pattern" is demonstrated for 35 of 36 profiles plus legitimate
entry/direction offsets, not for the full corpus.

Phase names (`implement`, `design`, `investigate`, `integration`, `revise`;
`review` vs `independent_review`; `correct` vs `correction`) carry no engine
meaning -- "the reducer does not branch on phase names" (product-boundary §5
item 2). This is the decisive, now-verified input to §9.

== 4. Analysis: the hypothesis straddles the trust boundary

=== 4.1 The fatal ambiguity

The proposed component is given two responsibilities that the repository's own
invariants place on *opposite sides* of a trust boundary.

*Authoring* -- producing the governing inputs (WorkItem, profile, policy, task,
personas). Per §2.8(7) these are authority-bearing artifacts whose provenance must
be distinguishable from agent output. A component that authors them sits *above*
the boundary and must hold no authority; its output is a *request*.

*Deriving* -- resolving the capability envelope, assurance floor, execution
boundary and eligible model set. Per §2.8(2)-(4) this is explicitly assigned to
trusted Level 0 and must *admit or reject* what is requested. A component that
does it sits *inside* the boundary.

One service doing both would decide what authority to request *and* whether that
request is admissible: precisely §2.8(5), an escalation path terminating inside
its own beneficiary, and §2.8(8), policy becoming an output of the pipeline it
governs. This is the strongest reason against the hypothesis as stated, and it is
structural rather than stylistic.

=== 4.2 A real boundary, but not a new one

The seam is real (§2.1) -- a tier exists, a human occupies it, its cost is
measured -- but mechanizing it needs no new runtime layer. It needs one new
*artifact* (a canonical semantic `CommissionIntent`), one new *deterministic
projection component above the boundary* (already named "Commission Compiler" in
`workflow_coordinator_successor_commission_pipeline_target_2026-10-10.adoc` §5),
and *completion of a derivation duty already assigned to L0* and already recorded
as unbuilt (§2.8, final paragraph). A third addressable layer would move the
authorship precondition from "unsolved for L1" to "unsolved for a new component"
and add an interface.

=== 4.3 What becomes hidden, and why it is worth hiding

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Hidden below the boundary | Why that is valuable

| `adapter`/`model`/`reasoning` literals | A provider-catalogue change would otherwise edit planning documents; binding is already kept out of profile and policy for this reason (§2.4)
| Gate evidence placement and `failed_evidence_route` wiring | Exactly what P2 (three non-accepting Runs) and P3 (second PREPARE refusal) got wrong; derivable from "this gate carries acceptance evidence"
| Durable-artifact path declarations | Already identified as derivable (`run_event_and_review_artifact_concept` §12)
| Phase-scope / policy-ceiling / WorkItem-scope duplication | In the P3 Commission the same eleven implementation paths are written in four places (`WorkItem.scope`, policy ceiling, `implement`, `correction`), and the one review-artifact path in six (two scopes, both gate phases, two `durable_artifacts` entries)
| Persona byte limits, prompt-component ordering | Caused a provider-free refusal after a multi-minute authoring cycle (friction §3.3)
| Attempt-id and OPS lifecycle | Consumed an identifier on a provider-free failure (friction §3.4)
|===

What must *not* be hidden is §7.

== 5. Recommended boundary

Replace the single proposed component with a split at the *existing* trust
boundary, plus one canonical artifact. "CI" below means the
CommissionIntent/Compiler layer.

[source,text]
----
            L1 : semantic planning / disposition / authority
                   |  WorkIntent, disposition, AuthorityDelta
                   v
  +--------------------------------------------------+
  |  above the trust boundary: NO AUTHORITY          |
  |    CommissionIntent    (canonical authoring      |
  |                          source, not authority)  |
  |    CommissionCompiler       (deterministic,      |
  |         |                    provider-free)      |
  |         v                                        |
  |    rendered governing-input projection           |
  |         = an ordinary dirty worktree             |
  +--------------------------------------------------+
                   |
   maintainer reviews/approves the exact bundle ;
   git records/transports/pins/audits it ; a separate
   maintainer act authorizes PREPARE/LAUNCH against
   the pinned commit -- the commit alone is not proof
                   v
  +--------------------------------------------------+
  |  the trust boundary: Level 0                     |
  |    admission : pin, classify, derive floors,     |
  |                admit or reject, fail closed      |
  |    execution : provider invocation, capability   |
  |                enforcement, trusted evidence,    |
  |                trusted Git, typed stops          |
  +--------------------------------------------------+
----

Two canonicalities, not one. `CommissionIntent` is the canonical *authoring*
source for a render transaction -- what the compiler reads to produce the
projection. The exact committed and admitted governing inputs, which Level 0
actually pins and re-verifies, are the canonical *execution* authority. Today
Level 0 knows no `CommissionIntent`; it admits only the rendered files. A
future architecture may mechanically bind an intent to its projection and
elevate the intent's authority role, but that is not designed here. Default
rule until it is: a generated governing file manually changed after rendering
invalidates the intent/projection equivalence, and the changed file -- not
the stale intent -- is what L0 admits.

=== 5.1 Why `git diff` is the right acceptance seam

Not an invention. `DEC-WFC-010` (active) retired a custom staging protocol --
tracked staging directory, approval marker, baseline and proposal fingerprints,
one-shot consumption, a bespoke `memory-promote` acceptance operation -- for
canonical JSON plus a generated workbook with "Git as the review, acceptance, and
rollback boundary". `README.md`: "Git is the review boundary ... There is no
separate Project Memory promotion protocol."

Applying that pattern means the compiler creates *no new authority mechanism at
all*. Its output is working-tree text, reviewable the same way any dirty
worktree is reviewed today. `git diff` remains the review seam; admission still
pins committed governing inputs at `expected_head` with digest re-verification
between Hops, unchanged. The authorship precondition (§2.8(7)) stays exactly as
procedurally satisfied as it is now -- neither strengthened nor weakened --
which is the correct ambition for a change meant to remove transcription, not
to invent provenance.

=== 5.1.1 The current authorizing act, stated precisely

Repository state or a commit alone is not proof of execution authority. The
authority index is explicit that committed governance alone neither starts nor
authorizes a Run, and that each real Run additionally requires explicit
maintainer authorization (`docs/README.adoc`). Current supervised practice is:

* a separately trusted maintainer/operator reviews or approves the exact
  bundle;
* Git records, transports, pins, and audits that bundle;
* the maintainer separately authorizes PREPARE/LAUNCH against the pinned
  commit.

The abstract invariant this preserves is narrower than "commit = authority":
authority must originate in a separately protected authority source, never in
the requester or the beneficiary of the work. Today that source is the
maintainer's explicit PREPARE/LAUNCH act, operationally coupled to a prior
review/commit step; it is not the commit by itself.

=== 5.2 The one asymmetry that must hold

[quote]
____
Above the boundary a requester may *add* assurance and *narrow* capability. Below
it, derivation may *add* assurance and *narrow* capability, and its additions are
not removable from above.
____

Composition is monotone. This generalizes
`FTR-WFC-ROLE-PERSONA-EXECUTION-BINDING`'s one-directional eligibility rule
(§2.4) and product-boundary §11.2.

=== 5.3 Where the compiler lives

Outside the trusted core; plausibly inside the WFC distribution as an explicitly
non-authoritative tool, in the relationship `scripts/wfc_op.py` and
`make memory-export` already have. It must not link into
`src/workflow_coordinator/` admission or reducer paths, and it must not be
required: admission must not depend on this one convenience authoring tool, so
a hand-authored Commission must remain admissible. Any future path that did
become the exclusive route to a valid Commission would still have to preserve
a separately protected authority source, an inspectable admitted projection,
and fail-closed provenance (§5.4) -- it is the dependency on a single
convenience tool that is rejected here, not every possible future exclusivity.

=== 5.4 The authorizing act is current practice, not a frozen invariant

§5.1.1 describes today's supervised workflow, not a permanent architecture.
The repository's accepted invariant is narrower and more durable: authority
must originate in a separately protected authority source, fixed before the
requester can influence it -- never in the requester or the beneficiary of the
work. The present maintainer-commit-plus-PREPARE/LAUNCH sequence is one way to
satisfy that invariant, not the only way it could ever be satisfied.

A plausible long-term direction, recorded here as direction only and not
designed:

[source,text]
----
today    explicit maintainer authorization of each individual Run
later    a Level-1-or-higher mechanism operates inside a separately
         pre-authorized envelope covering a bounded family of work
eventual the human resembles a project stakeholder/sponsor, participating
         at defined decision, visibility, approval, or checkpoint
         boundaries rather than dispatching routine Commissions
----

This is not implementation authority and nothing in this record authorizes
moving along it. It exists so that the concept does not accidentally rule out
a future separately protected authority source merely because today's source
happens to be a human commit. Two preconditions this record leaves open (§17)
gate any such move: a caller-mediation boundary for above-L0 actors (§10.1),
and a mechanical -- not merely procedural -- distinction between
maintainer-authorized and agent-produced governing input (§2.8(7)).

== 7. What must never be hidden behind the compiler

Hiding these would hide semantic authority, not mechanics.

. *`authority_source`* -- why this work is authorized and by which record. It is
  what a reviewer and a fresh agent read first. Templated prose here would be the
  most damaging possible outcome of this concept.
. *The authority delta* -- what the successor may touch that its predecessor could
  not, and what remains withheld (handoff §5.9).
. *Acceptance semantics* -- an evidence profile name may select which commands
  run; it must never silently decide what the project accepts.
. *Scope as a decision* -- the compiler may project one authored path list into the
  places that need it, never widen one to make a template fit (pipeline target §7:
  "The renderer must not invent semantic authority to make the template fit").
. *Withheld authority and the escalation default* -- what the Run must do when its
  authority proves insufficient.
. *Any derived assurance control* -- a derived mandatory defensive review must
  appear in the rendered projection *and* the admission report, never as an
  invisible consequence of a capability name.

== 8. Task type, semantic role, persona: three concepts, not one

Genuinely distinct, but the strawman's axes are mis-cut: the repository already
separates *phase function* from *subject perspective*, and the strawman's "role"
straddles both.

[cols="2,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Concept | Status today | Correct owner

| `WorkflowRole` -- phase function | implemented, closed: `worker`, `independent_reviewer` (`model.py:154-160`); grants no authority | L0. Closed. Must not grow.
| Task type | *absent*; leaks as phase names (§2.6) and prose | L1 vocabulary; a CI selector key; never in the L0 core (§2.8(6))
| Semantic role | *absent* | A consumer/L1 catalog key; maps to persona + components, never to a provider (§2.4)
| Persona -- bounded prose | implemented; 512 B; `authority=none` | WorkItem-owned, CI-selected, L0-admitted
|===

* *Task taxonomy* is owned by L1/the consumer -- it is project semantics; L0 may
  carry it only as an inert label.
* *Task type/context -> role* is an L1 judgement about what expertise the work
  needs.
* *Role -> persona* is the compiler's, from a catalog authored above, mechanically
  validated below.
* *Personas must be provider-independent* -- "subject expertise must not select a
  provider" (§2.4). A persona naming a model has crossed axes.
* *May a role imply reference material?* Split the question. The *prose* (an idiom
  guide, a style reference) is a prompt component the compiler may attach by role.
  *Read access* to a path, dataset or network resource is a visibility capability:
  requested above, derived and enforced below, never implied by a role string.
  `FTR-WFC-EXECUTION-TRUST-BOUNDARIES` is explicit that "the ordinary worker must
  not be able to widen its own visibility" and that prompt text is "semantic
  guidance rather than containment".
* *Is that role semantics, execution policy, or capability policy?* All three --
  which is exactly why one word must not own it.

== 9. Workflow topology: grammar with a floor, not yet an accepted grammar

Recommendation: *option C, base pattern plus orthogonal modifiers*, bounded by
§5.2's monotonicity rule -- as a hypothesis to be falsified against the full
corpus, not as an accepted grammar (§19).

The evidence is unusually direct, now that §2.6 has been independently
corrected and route-normalized: 36 authored profiles, five raw WORK/GATE
signatures, reduce by position-normalized route graph to one repeating
WORK/GATE unit occurring once or twice, plus legitimate entry offsets into
that unit for four gate-first continuation profiles, plus one bypass edge in
one profile that the reduction does not yet cover. Option A (families) is what
the repository already has, and it produced 36 near-duplicate files whose
component selectors are uniformly empty; stale-pattern copying between them
caused P2's three non-accepting Runs and P3's second PREPARE refusal. Option B
(one parameterized mega-topology) is rejected by the brief and by
`FTR-WFC-ELEVATED-PRIVILEGE-DEFENSIVE-REVIEW`'s refusal to "design one workflow
stage per privilege aspect", which cuts equally against one stage per
conditional.

A minimal grammar consistent with the *corrected* corpus:

[source,text]
----
base pattern (small closed set, pre-authorized):
    work_only       WORK                                 (degenerate case;
                                                            not present today)
    single_cycle    WORK -> GATE                          ( 9 of 36, incl.
                                                             2 gate-first)
    reviewed_work   WORK -> GATE -> WORK -> GATE          (27 of 36, incl.
                                                             2 gate-first)
entry offset:
    continuation    enter at the cycle's GATE phase       ( 4 of 36;
                                                             initial_phase =
                                                             review |
                                                             independent_review |
                                                             rereview)
modifiers:
    first_review_breadth = focused | broad   requestable ( 5 of 27 broad)
    final_review   = none | peer | stronger  requestable
    defensive_review                         DERIVED, not removable
    convergence_escape = on | off            requestable
    max_corrections = n                      requestable, capped by policy
    evidence_profile                         L1-owned meaning

    one profile routes its first gate directly to its second, bypassing
    `correct` -- a bypass/skip edge this grammar does not yet express
----

Two constraints keep this from becoming a configuration language. Every modifier
must be either derivable or monotone-requestable -- one that can reduce assurance
from above is inadmissible by §2.8(1). And the base-pattern set is pre-authorized
and *selected*, never supplied (§2.8(4)): a compiler emitting an arbitrary phase
graph has authored a topology, the escalation product-boundary §11.2 forbids.

What this would buy concretely, if accepted: the evidence placement and
`failed_evidence_route` wiring that P2 and P3 both got wrong stops being
authored at all. It is a property of "this phase is an acceptance gate",
derivable from the base pattern.

*Future acceptance condition, not satisfied here.* Before any profile
grammar or renderer replaces current authored topology, it must demonstrate
lossless or semantically equivalent round-trip coverage of the complete
current profile corpus, including non-default initial entry points and the
one bypass edge identified above. Short of that, this grammar remains the
decisive-looking but unverified hypothesis the corpus evidence supports
investigating, not a result.

== 10. Model execution, model selection, and information flow

=== 10.1 "All model execution through Level 0" is unsound as stated

Rejected as written. It is false on its face here: every concept document cited in
this record, including this one, was authored by an interactive LLM session above
L0 (handoff §5.8 says so explicitly). An invariant the project violates
continuously while writing its own architecture is not an invariant.

The sound narrowing has two clauses:

[quote]
____
(a) Every model invocation whose output is cited as trusted evidence, mutates the
repository, or consumes protected context passes through Level 0.

(b) A model invocation outside Level 0 produces only an untrusted *request*, and
holds neither repository write authority nor access to protected context.
____

Clause (b) is what makes an authoring assistant admissible rather than a shadow
channel, and it is the reasoning product-boundary §11.1 Case A applies to an
orchestrator: being polite about calling WFC does not make an actor governed --
having no other effect path does. So the requirement is not "route the call
through L0" but "ensure the above-L0 caller has nothing to escalate with". That
requirement is currently *unmet* (product-boundary §5.1: no caller mediation
boundary; a one-shot CLI under ambient OS identity), which is why this record's
§5.1.1 keeps explicit maintainer authorization in the loop rather than relying
on repository state alone.

=== 10.2 Model selection: trusted ceiling below, preference above

The recorded open question "who owns the execution-class resolver" is answerable
without picking a side, because the reasons for constraining a model are not one
kind of fact.

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Reason a model is constrained | Side

| Information-flow compatibility: this work needs protected context, so only these execution boundaries may receive it | *Below.* Product-boundary §17 already notes "the choice of execution boundary is a *security* decision and cannot be a Level-1 convenience."
| Capability/privilege class of the surface being mutated | *Below.* Same derivation as the assurance floor.
| Minimum model capability/assurance class, where an accepted control genuinely depends on competence (e.g. a high-assurance architecture review) | *Below, as a floor only.* Trusted policy may impose a minimum eligible capability class; see below.
| Quality beyond that floor: cost, latency, preferred strength, or a stronger-than-minimum model | *Above, within the eligible set.* A judgement about the work, expressed as a declared requirement -- the thing that must stop naming `codex_cli:<model>:high` directly.
| Cost/latency budget | *Above.* Keeping it below would "import economic policy into the governance engine" (product-boundary §17).
|===

Mechanism, following §2.4's reserved seam exactly: a phase or Run declares
*requirements*; trusted derivation enumerates the *eligible* bindings;
request-side preference selects *within* that set; an empty eligible set is a
fail-closed refusal before dispatch, never a downgrade.

Quality is not uniformly a requester preference. Trusted Level-0 policy
derives the minimum eligible security/execution boundary *and*, where the
accepted work class genuinely requires it, a minimum model capability or
assurance class -- for example, a high-assurance architecture review may
require a minimum eligible model capability class before any binding is
offered as eligible. Request-side preference then chooses among the eligible
candidates by cost, latency, or preferred quality, and may request a
stronger-than-minimum model, but never one below the trusted floor. This adds
no universal model score, benchmark threshold, model router, provider policy
schema, or concrete model class -- only the architectural allowance that a
floor may exist and must be derived below, never supplied above. The original
motivation for WFC's own use of this is engineering quality and deterministic,
understandable execution; the rule becomes more directly load-bearing if WFC
is later used to support audit/compliance or assurance claims, which this
record does not attempt to design.

=== 10.3 The consequence the hypothesis must not skip

The binding is kept out of the governed, digest-pinned input set (§2.3) on the
stated ground that it grants no authority. That premise is *conditional on there
being no information-flow classification*. The moment a binding choice can
determine whether protected context may be disclosed, the binding becomes
authority-bearing, and its current position -- an un-pinned file written from a CLI
flag, outside `governing` -- becomes wrong.

[quote]
____
Introducing data-sensitivity-driven model constraints converts the execution
binding from an authority-neutral preference into an authority-bearing control,
and therefore requires moving the constrained part of it inside the pinned,
trusted input set.
____

This is a falsifiable prediction and the most important sequencing consequence in
this record: information-flow policy must not be layered on top of today's binding
transport.

=== 10.4 Data zones: reject the enum, keep the compatibility predicate

`data_zone: normal | restricted | secret` should *not* be adopted.
Product-boundary §23 lists "define a classification lattice, taxonomy, DLP engine,
or taint-tracking mechanism" as an explicit non-goal, and
`FTR-WFC-EXECUTION-TRUST-BOUNDARIES` says its own rule "does not imply that all
such information has one classification or define a complete classification
scheme".

What survives is weaker and more useful: a *compatibility predicate* between a
declared information requirement and an execution boundary, with an ingress clause
(who may receive protected context) and an egress clause (where derived output may
flow). The egress clause is the one most easily forgotten, and the FTR already
enumerates the sinks that make it non-trivial -- "model prompts and context,
summaries and Findings, generated files, review artifacts, logs, diagnostics and
scratch space, Run/checkpoint/evidence state, commit subjects and messages". A
summary is not a declassification.

So yes: information-flow policy belongs inside this architecture -- at the
admission boundary, derived, fail-closed, before dispatch.

=== 10.5 Capability escalation and information escalation differ in control *timing*

Already settled in the repository, and the strawman loses it.

* *Capability / execution-surface escalation* -- the control acts *after* the
  mutation: `FTR-WFC-ELEVATED-PRIVILEGE-DEFENSIVE-REVIEW`'s mandatory defensive
  review, with no ordinary route bypassing it.
* *Information-access escalation* -- the control must act *before* disclosure.
  `FTR-WFC-EXECUTION-TRUST-BOUNDARIES`: "A later review cannot undo disclosure to
  an unauthorized provider or execution boundary", and "A pure protected-read
  elevation does not by itself require the mandatory defensive-review workflow."

That is a real difference in authority owner and in compilation output, so the
distinction earns its keep. The naive unification -- grant more, then review harder
-- is unsound for the information case.

=== 10.6 Does privilege change compiled review policy? Yes, but not keyed on the capability name

The hypothesis offers `git_write -> implies defensive_git_review`. Too
name-coupled, and already refined here: the accepted trigger is a phase receiving
mutation or execution authority over a surface that *the admitted authority
envelope classifies* as requiring elevated handling; the `aspect` is
"intentionally open-ended ... does not by itself determine which control topology
applies"; and "the worker cannot waive review ... any exemption must come from the
governing authority that classified or granted the elevation".

So the compilation rule is:

[source,text]
----
granted capability  x  resource class of the authorized scope
      -> envelope classification        (trusted, below)
      -> mandatory assurance modifier   (derived, not removable from above)
----

`git_write` over ordinary source and `git_write` over `.github/workflows/` are not
the same request -- product-boundary §11.4's point about deferred effect and the
confused deputy. Keying the modifier on the capability name would get the common
case right and the dangerous case wrong.

=== 10.7 Escalation algebra: the proposed ontology is net-negative

Mapping the strawman onto the six existing kinds (§2.7):

[cols="2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Proposed variant | Existing kind | Verdict

| `ScopeEscalation` | `scope` | rename
| `SemanticAuthorityEscalation` | `semantic_authority` | rename
| `OperationalEscalation` | `operational` | rename; and it is a failure class, not an authority escalation
| `CapabilityEscalation` | `trust_boundary` | rename
| `ArchitectureEscalation` | `semantic_authority` + ladder | *not a kind*; an orthogonal axis (handoff §5.6)
| `InformationAccessEscalation` | -- | *the one genuine gap* (§10.5)
| -- | `public_surface` | lost by the proposal
| -- | `prerequisite` | lost by the proposal
|===

The proposed algebra renames four existing kinds, mis-models one as a kind when it
is an orthogonal axis, silently deletes two, and adds one real distinction.
Adopting it would be ontology bloat *and* a regression.

The evidence-supported change is minimal: keep the existing closed set; consider
distinguishing a protected-read need from a mutation/execution-capability need,
plausibly as a *need predicate* in the escalation payload rather than a new kind --
`FTR-WFC-EXECUTION-TRUST-BOUNDARIES` already drafts exactly that vocabulary
(`requires_protected_read`, `requires_execution_surface_mutation`,
`requires_network`, `requires_trusted_test_context`) while schema-committing none
of it. Keep the ladder (Commission / Slice concept / Epic / programme) as L1 prose
vocabulary, as the handoff concept recommends, not as schema.

== 11. Task assessment

=== 11.1 Assessment is advice, made structurally incapable of granting authority

`DEC-WFC-005` already states the general rule; pipeline target §8.3 the specific
one: "A worker's own statement such as 'I need path X' is evidence/recommendation,
not authority."

What makes this mechanical rather than aspirational is *not* a rule about how
assessment output is used. It is that the output type contains no
authority-bearing field. An assessment may carry `suggests_protected_read: true`;
it must not be able to carry an authorized path list, a capability grant, or a
declassification.

Stated as an invariant: assessment/advice may *recommend* that complexity is
high, that a Git write is likely needed, that architecture impact is likely
cross-cutting, or that protected information may be required. It may *never
grant* scope, capability, information access, architecture authority, or
provider eligibility. An assessment reading as justification next to a
request for the thing it just recommended is assessment laundering (§14.2);
`authority_source` must cite a governing record, never an assessment.

=== 11.2 Where it belongs, and when it must be an L0 Run

Assessment belongs *above* the boundary, as an L1 input to `CommissionIntent`.
Whether it must execute as an L0 Run is determined by §10.1(a), not by preference:

* *Ordinary repository, no protected context* -- an above-L0 assistant may assess;
  its output is an untrusted request, and no governance is needed because no
  authority attaches.
* *Protected context required to judge the work* -- it must be an L0 Run, because
  reading the repository into a model prompt is itself a disclosure to a sink
  (§10.4). Here the invariant is *forced by information flow*, not adopted for
  neatness.

Governance is not the only reason model-assisted assessment might later be
routed through a common Level-0 execution mechanism even where no protected
context is involved. A single provider/model execution seam offers DRY,
telemetry, provenance, reproducibility, budget/timeout controls, consistent
adapter handling, and operational clarity that a scattered set of ad hoc
above-L0 calls does not. Those are real benefits, but they are not promoted to
an invariant here -- whether and how to capture them remains an
implementation/design question for later, independent of whether authority is
at stake.

=== 11.3 The recursion objection: open, not dissolved

"Assessment needs a Commission; a Commission needs assessment" is a real
objection to a per-task assessment Run. A *standing fixture* -- one
pre-authorized, committed, read-only Commission per repository, reused across
tasks -- is a plausible future answer, but it does not yet dissolve the
recursion: today's concrete Task is one of the five pinned governing inputs
(§2.3), fixed at authoring time, so a standing fixture authored with one such
Task cannot represent whatever different concrete task actually needs
assessing next without a new input surface, and `authorized_scope: []` bounds
mutation, not what the provider process can read.

Before a standing assessment envelope can be called sufficient, it must
define:

* the dynamic task-input envelope -- how the concrete task being assessed
  enters a fixture whose Task input is otherwise fixed;
* the readable/context envelope -- what repository content the assessment
  may read, not merely what it may mutate;
* provider/execution eligibility for the assessment call itself;
* ingress/egress constraints where protected context is in scope (§10.4);
* provenance binding between the assessed input and the resulting advice.

None of this is designed here. Until it is, a standing assessment fixture is a
possible future mechanism, not a completed architectural solution, and the
recursion is better described as *relocated* -- from "generate a Commission"
to "authorize a dynamic task and read envelope" -- than resolved. Until that
envelope exists, protected-context assessment remains ordinary, separately
admitted Level-0 work (§11.2), and unprotected-context assessment remains an
above-L0 advisory call under §10.1(b). No such Commission, standing or
per-task, is authorized here.

== 12. The four-file Commission structure

Recommendation: *`CommissionIntent` becomes the canonical authoring source for
a render transaction; the four governing files become its generated
projection, still committed, still pinned, still the thing L0 admits as
execution authority.* The two are not simultaneously the same canonical
truth (§5): the intent is authored once, above the boundary; the projection,
once rendered and committed, is what Level 0 actually pins, re-verifies, and
executes against.

The four files must *not* become internal L0 artifacts and must *not* be hidden
from review. They are the admitted authority; admission reads them at
`expected_head` and re-verifies their digests between Hops (§2.3). Hiding them
would move authority into a generator. *Generating* them changes nothing about
admission -- which is exactly why this is the cheap option, and why §5.1's
precedent applies directly. If a maintainer hand-edits a rendered file before
commit, the edited file -- not the intent that produced it -- is what gets
admitted; per §5 that divergence invalidates the intent/projection
equivalence for this render transaction, and a future re-render should not
silently claim to represent the edited state unless the edit is folded back
into the intent first.

Canonical in the intent, authored once: `authority_source` prose (never templated
-- §7); the authorized path list (projected into WorkItem scope, phase scopes and
policy ceiling); base pattern and modifier selection; persona/component selection
by semantic role; acceptance semantics; withheld authority and escalation default;
predecessor/salvage provenance for a successor.

Derived: phase-name boilerplate, gate evidence placement, `failed_evidence_route`
wiring, durable-artifact paths, filename/ID conventions, the standard
withheld-authority and fresh-successor clauses, policy-ceiling projection, hop
ceiling within policy.

Honest accounting of what this does *not* fix: it does not close the authorship
precondition (§2.8(7)); it does not reduce the semantic judgement in
`authority_source`; and it would not have prevented the P3 stop, whose
withheld-but-required `adapter.py` was a *semantic* boundary error no renderer
would catch (handoff §2). It removes transcription and the two deterministic P3
refusal classes, nothing more. That is still worth having, and claiming more would
be the failure mode the friction record warns about.

== 14. Risks and rejected alternatives

=== 14.1 Strongest counterargument: this may be renaming, not reducing

The friction record §5.1 warns that correctness "often depends on *cross-file
relationships*". A compiler does not remove those relationships; it relocates them
into a renderer and an intent schema. If the intent ends up as verbose as the four
files -- plausible, since the brief's own candidate list names eight catalogs -- the
result is a fifth artifact and a new synchronization problem.

The honest reply is measurable rather than rhetorical. Friction record §8 already
specifies the baseline. This concept is justified only if it reduces: Commission
lines authored per WorkItem, duplicated scope declarations across files,
provider-free preflight refusals, and consumed attempt identifiers before first
launch. If those four numbers do not move, it is renaming.

Against the objection, §2.5-§2.6 are hard counter-evidence: 36 files reducing
to one repeating WORK/GATE unit plus entry offsets, 124 phases with uniformly
empty component selectors, one path list written in four places per
Commission, and an empty 64 KiB reusable slot beside an overflowing 512-byte
field is duplication a renderer provably removes. The reduction is not yet
proven lossless over the full corpus (§9), so this remains counter-evidence
for *some* renderer being worthwhile, not yet proof that the specific grammar
in §9 is the right one.

=== 14.2 Other risks

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Risk | Mitigation

| Generic-platform drift: the brief's eight catalogs are most of an agent platform | Admit no catalog without a populated, repeated, real need. The honest count today is two -- base patterns, and personas/components, which exists and is empty.
| Premature freezing of Level 1 | The interface is a `CommissionIntent`, saying what work is authorized and how it executes. It says nothing about how L1 decided, plans, tracks an Epic, or curates a ledger; L1 can grow goal custody, lineage and closure judgement without it changing.
| The compiler becomes load-bearing, so a convenience tool becomes authority infrastructure | §5.3: hand-authored Commissions stay admissible; admission never learns the compiler exists.
| Derived controls become invisible and cannot be audited at review time | §7: derived controls appear in the rendered projection *and* the admission report.
| Assessment laundering -- "high complexity, needs git write" reads as justification next to a request for git write | §11.1 type-level separation, plus §7: `authority_source` cites a governing record, never an assessment.
|===

=== 14.3 Rejected alternatives

. *A third addressable layer between L1 and L0* -- §4.1 trust-boundary straddle;
  §4.2, no evidence requires it.
. *A semantic model resolver mapping `role + difficulty -> model`* -- contradicts
  "subject expertise must not select a provider" and the explicit no-router
  decision (§2.2, §2.4). Replaced by eligibility plus preference (§10.2).
. *`data_zone` as a three-level enum* -- §10.4.
. *A six-variant escalation sum type* -- §10.7, net-negative.
. *`git_write -> defensive_git_review` keyed on capability name* -- §10.6.
. *Moving the four governing files inside L0 as internal artifacts* -- §12; it
  would relocate authority into a generator.
. *An LLM inside the compiler* -- deterministic rendering only, with at most one
  bounded repair call driven by validator diagnostics, per pipeline target §5-§6.
. *More workflow families*, and *one parameterized mega-topology* -- §9; the
  corpus falsifies the first and the brief rejects the second.
. *Making the compiler the only route to a valid Commission* -- §5.3.

== 15. Worked examples

Each distinguishes semantic decision / authority decision / CI projection / L0
enforcement.

=== A. Documentation or archive task
*Semantic* L1: documentation task, no independent review needed. *Authority*
maintainer authorizes a doc path, no code scope. *CI* base `work_only`; one
persona; evidence profile `documentation_validation`; no gate; no durable review
artifact. *L0* admits, classifies `docs/**` ordinary, derives no mandatory review,
runs one Hop. The cheap path exists precisely because nothing derived a floor
above it.

=== B. Ordinary implementation task
*Semantic* L1: implementation, Python, ordinary risk. *Authority* maintainer
authorizes the path list and the acceptance meaning. *CI* base `reviewed_work`,
`breadth=focused`, personas from catalog, evidence profile `python_focused`; gate
evidence and `failed_evidence_route` *derived, not authored*. *L0* admits, derives
the ordinary floor, runs the two-Hop happy path -- the
`RUN-b419abdd174143219ed8d9b6b42d92b5` shape, already low-friction once the
Commission is coherent.

=== C. Difficult implementation task
*Semantic* L1: high complexity, cross-cutting. *Authority* scope unchanged; L1
*adds* assurance (`final_review = stronger`), which §5.2 permits. *CI* same base
pattern, `breadth=broad`, a declared quality requirement instead of
`codex_cli:<model>:high`. *L0* derives the eligible binding set -- narrowed to a
minimum capability/assurance class if trusted policy requires one for this
review strength (§10.2); the stronger preference is admitted only if eligible
within that set; an empty set is a refusal, never a downgrade.

=== D. Git / capability escalation
*Semantic* a Hop discovers it needs privileged Git metadata mutation and returns a
typed `trust_boundary` escalation without widening its own authority. *Authority*
L1 classifies the ladder level; the maintainer or a protected envelope root grants
or denies -- the worker's own request is not authority (`DEC-WFC-005`). *CI* a
fresh successor intent, explicitly not resume/retry/replay (handoff §5.9),
rendering the expanded capability request and the authority delta. *L0* classifies
the granted capability against the resource class of the authorized scope,
*derives* the mandatory defensive-review modifier, renders it visibly (§7), admits
no ordinary route around it, enforces the envelope. If the resource class is
ordinary, no defensive review is derived: the capability name alone decides
nothing (§10.6).

=== E. Restricted-information escalation
*Semantic* a cloud-capable Run discovers it must read restricted material; it stops
and escalates, and no unauthorized read occurs because the material is absent or
sealed rather than merely prohibited. *Authority* the envelope root decides, and
must decide *before* execution, because no review can undo a disclosure (§10.5).
*CI* the successor intent declares a protected-read requirement; it does *not* name
an execution environment, which would be a caller selecting a mechanism (§2.8(3)).
*L0* evaluates the compatibility predicate, enumerates eligible execution
boundaries, fails closed before dispatch on an empty set, and enforces the egress
clause over every sink -- Findings, summaries, commit subjects, Run evidence
(§10.4). Derived output stays protected absent independent declassification. Per
§10.3 this case cannot be built on today's un-pinned binding transport.

=== F. Architecture / design task
*Semantic* L1: design task, adversarial architecture review wanted. *Authority*
authorizes producing a concept document only. *CI* base `reviewed_work`; personas
`architect` / `adversarial architecture reviewer`; authorized scope limited to the
concept path. *L0* admits; the Run can produce only its concept artifact. The
concept output does not self-authorize implementation -- that needs a separate
Commission, the pattern every design record here already follows, including this
one. A concept-revision Run may not accept its own output (handoff §5.8).

=== G. Assessment requiring model judgement
*Semantic* L1 wants a complexity/risk estimate. *Authority* none granted; the
output type carries no authority-bearing field (§11.1). *CI* consumes the
`TaskAssessment` as an input to `CommissionIntent`; if the repository holds no
protected content, an above-L0 assistant may produce that assessment directly,
and its output is simply an untrusted request (§10.1(b)) -- no L0 execution is
involved in this case at all. *L0* only if protected content is involved: the
assessment is today ordinary, separately admitted Level-0 work, because no
standing read-only assessment envelope is yet defined (§11.3) -- a future
standing fixture remains a possible, not an available, option until its
dynamic task-input, read, eligibility, and provenance envelope is specified.
There is no side channel either way, not because a rule forbids one, but
because the above-L0 path has no repository write authority and no
protected-context access (§10.1(b)) -- a property currently *procedural*, not
mechanical (§2.8(7)).

== 16. Minimal viable concept

Smallest version worth building, in dependency order. Each step is independently
useful and separately authorizable; none is authorized here.

. *Populate the existing component mechanism.* Move reusable role/review material
  out of 512-byte personas and 23 KiB single-use task files into `components.yaml`
  prompt components. No code, no schema, no new mechanism -- the 64 KiB
  digest-pinned slot already exists and is empty (§2.5). The cheapest available
  response to the P3 persona overflow.
. *An authoring-time validator.* Run the existing provider-free admission checks
  against an uncommitted Commission, before `PREPARE` reserves an attempt
  identifier. Addresses friction §9.2-§9.3 and both P3 refusals directly. No new
  artifact, no intent schema.
. *A deterministic renderer over a maintainer-authored `CommissionIntent`.* Zero
  LLM calls -- pipeline target "Stage 1". Output is a dirty worktree; `git diff`
  is review; the maintainer's separate authorization, not the commit alone,
  remains what permits execution (§5.1.1).
. *Name the base-pattern set.* Two patterns plus entry offsets, derived from
  §2.6's corrected corpus rather than designed, and gated on the round-trip
  acceptance condition in §9 -- including the one bypass edge §2.6 found
  uncovered; derive gate evidence placement and `failed_evidence_route` from
  the pattern only once that is demonstrated.

Steps 1-2 require no new concept at all and capture a large share of the measured
friction. If they are taken and §14.1's metrics do not improve, steps 3-4 should be
reconsidered rather than continued.

== 21. Conclusion in one paragraph

The Commissioning hypothesis identifies a real boundary and mis-draws it. The tier
exists and a human occupies it, but it is not a layer *between* Level 1 and Level
0: it is one authority-free deterministic projection component on the requesting
side of the existing trust boundary, plus a derivation duty already assigned to
Level 0 and already recorded as unbuilt. Splitting it that way preserves every
accepted invariant -- envelope derived below, topology selected rather than
supplied, no escalation terminating inside its own beneficiary, assurance
composable only upward -- and keeps the maintainer's explicit review-and-launch
authorization as the current authorizing act (§5.1.1), which is the only thing
presently closing the authorship precondition; that act is today's supervised
practice, not a frozen invariant, and a separately protected authority source
could satisfy the same invariant differently in the future (§5.4). Most of the
measured friction, meanwhile, is reachable without any of this: a 64 KiB
digest-pinned reusable prompt-component slot sits empty while role material
overflows a 512-byte field, and 36 authored profiles reduce to one repeating
WORK/GATE unit plus entry offsets, with one open edge still uncovered (§2.6).
Build the component and validator steps first and measure; the rest of this
concept, including the base-pattern grammar, should be judged by whether the
numbers move and by round-trip coverage of the full corpus (§9).
