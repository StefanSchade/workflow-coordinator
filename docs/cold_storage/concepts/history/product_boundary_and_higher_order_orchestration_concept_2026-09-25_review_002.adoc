PRODUCT-SCOPE REALIGNMENT.*

All eight material findings from review 001 are *CLOSED*. Each was checked
against the corrected text and, where it made a claim about executable truth,

The verdict is B rather than A because the correction introduces one new
IMPORTANT defect at precisely the boundary the later README must state. §11.1
pairs an absolute admissibility invariant ("a design that also equips the
orchestrator with its own direct effect mechanisms over the same governed
resources is inadmissible, whether or not it intends to use them") with a
hypothesis that makes the removal of exactly that capability *optional* ("a

because any orchestrator can claim it was not *given* a capability it merely
happens to possess.

The remaining correction is roughly one paragraph. It does not reopen any
closed finding, does not touch the product direction, and does not require
re-litigating the maintainer's threat-model decision.

== 4. Finding-by-finding closure

=== F-B1 (BLOCKING) -- protected caller/executor boundary -- *CLOSED*

Review 001 required, as the smallest correction, a hard precondition beside F1
stating that an untrusted programmatic orchestrator must not possess ambient
authority to bypass Level 0, explicitly distinguished from containment of the
worker invoked by WFC, plus recasting the BYO sentence as a future direction for
untrusted callers.

The correction does all three. §11.1 adds the threat model, splits Case A
(agentic alternate effect path -- in scope, INVARIANT) from Case B (authorized
human or administrator bypass -- explicitly out of scope with an INVARIANT
denying any technical prevention claim). §11.6 adds "the effect-path
precondition" and states that the two preconditions are independent: provenance
answers who authorized the work, effect path answers whether the work could have
happened without asking. §1 and §21.2 qualify the product sentence to a trusted
or cooperative caller under external operator control, with untrusted
programmatic orchestration gated on both preconditions. §5.1 records "no
protected caller identity and no programmatic Higher-Loop authority path" as a
current limit.

Critically, the correction does *not* answer the bypass by appealing to human
trust. §11.6 states that a Higher Loop "does not become governed by being polite
about calling WFC", which is the exact failure mode the maintainer's brief warned
against.

The finding is closed. The residual ambiguity about what "equipped" means is a
*new* defect created by the correction's own pairing of Case A with the optional
hardening hypothesis, recorded as D-I1 below rather than as a failure to close
F-B1.

=== F-I2 (IMPORTANT) -- Level-1 authority, semantics, continuity -- *CLOSED*

§12.1 defines the three classes review 001 asked for and, more importantly,
terminates authority.

The separation is real and not merely nominal. Protected Epic authority holds
identity, goal, maximum envelope, closure criteria, *and the rules naming who
may revise each*; it may be changed "only by a protected authority root, through
an explicit, recorded revision. Not the orchestration engine, and not a model
call." The ledger permits the engine only to *propose* events, with a kernel
validating them and each event carrying transition authority and evidence. The
checkpoint is disposable.

The attack review 001 constructed -- ordinary transition code widening the goal,
replacing the envelope, deleting an obligation, marking a child accepted without
a WFC result, superseding work, or revising closure criteria -- is addressed
head-on. The opening sentence states the necessary distinction explicitly:
"Determinism and schema validity are not authority: a small validator can prove
that a mutation is well-formed while accepting an entirely unauthorized semantic
change." The INVARIANT then enumerates exactly that list of events and requires
each to root in an admitted authority source or mechanically verified evidence
under a pre-authorized rule, with `CHILD_ACCEPTED` singled out as needing to bind
to a verified WFC result "rather than to the engine's own assertion that the
child went well".

Restart is handled: "Restart reconstructs and reconciles from the protected
specification and the semantic ledger. It never regenerates authority or
obligations from a model call." `resume != replan` and "process restart must be
semantically invisible" are both preserved (§12, §12.2). Append-only event
sourcing is left OPEN, as required.

The added "where each class lives" paragraph was checked for the specific hazard
of requiring WFC to understand Epic semantics. It does not: it routes *ledger
edits* through bounded Level-0 work as ordinary path mutations, and §6 and §8.6
independently state that governing an opaque artifact is not understanding it,
with §8.6's "WFC sees a path set, an action set, a profile and an anchor; it does
not see an Epic." No Epic vocabulary is proposed for WFC.

=== F-I3 (IMPORTANT) -- procedural versus mechanical provenance -- *CLOSED*

Both propositions review 001 required are present and neither is hedged.

Procedural, not mechanical: §11.6 states that the interim arrangement "is a
*procedural* control, not mechanical closure of the precondition", gives the
reason ("Nothing in current admission can distinguish a maintainer-prepared
bundle from an agent-written bundle whose `authority_source` says
'maintainer-prepared'"), and adds "This concept must not be read as claiming the
precondition is met." §19 repeats it as an INVARIANT on the MVP, and §23 adds
"claim that the governing-input authorship precondition is mechanically closed"
to the explicit non-goals. §5.1 records the missing authorship root as a current
limit.

Not one human act per item: §11.6 carries an INVARIANT that "One human act per
WorkItem is *not* an architectural invariant", permits a separately protected
authority source to pre-authorize a bounded family or envelope, and states what

the actor that requests work within it". This matches the accepted
execution-trust-boundaries rule, which permits a human/maintainer root *or* a
separately governed authority source and explicitly supports automation without a
new human act per Hop once the envelope is admitted.

The `authority_source`-is-prose claim was verified: `model.py` declares
`authority_source: str` on `WorkItem` with no validation, and `operator.py`
pins and digests inputs without any provenance check.

== 5. New findings

=== D-I1 -- IMPORTANT -- "Equipped with" is undefined, so Case A cannot be applied to the concept's own first adoption path

Location:: §11.1, "Two cases that must not be conflated" (Case A INVARIANT and
the deployment-hardening HYPOTHESIS), read against §19.

Claim under test:: Case A states that "a design that also equips the
orchestrator with its own direct effect mechanisms over the same governed
resources is inadmissible, whether or not it intends to use them." Four
paragraphs later, the HYPOTHESIS lists "a target repository not writable by the
orchestrator" and "a distinct OS identity for mutation" as *optional deployment
hardening*, explicitly "not a universal prerequisite for the basic WFC product".

Evidence of tension:: If ambient OS write access counts as being "equipped with
direct effect mechanisms", then the baseline product configuration -- an
orchestrator process that can write the repository -- is inadmissible under the
concept's own INVARIANT, and the optional-hardening hypothesis contradicts it. If
ambient access does *not* count, Case A does not cover the attack review 001
actually constructed: a LangGraph node running as the same user calling
`open("src/foo.py", "w")`. That node was never "given" a write tool; it simply
inherits the process identity. The concept supplies both readings and selects
neither.

Failure scenario:: The concrete case is the concept's own recommendation. §19
proposes a supervised continuation assistant that reconciles state, tracks
obligations, prepares a candidate request, and stops for an operator. That
process runs under the operator's identity and can therefore write the
repository, invoke Git, and run commands. An implementer asking "is my first MVP
admissible under §11.1 Case A?" gets no answer. Worse, the permissive reading is
self-certifying: any orchestrator may assert that it was not *equipped* with a
capability it merely possesses, which reduces Case A to the "polite Higher Loop"
posture the correction elsewhere rejects in §11.6.

Why this is new rather than a reopened F-B1:: Review 001's requested correction
was to add the missing precondition and to stop treating a call convention as a
trust boundary. The correction does both. The defect is created by the new
pairing of an absolute invariant with an optional mitigation of the same
capability, which did not exist in the reviewed version.

Smallest correction:: One paragraph in §11.1 naming the discriminator and the
residual. A defensible form: Case A concerns the *effect surface deliberately
exposed to the agentic decision path* -- tools, capabilities, credentials, and
network reachable by the model-driven component -- which must not include a route
to governed resources that bypasses Level 0. Ambient capability inherited from
the host identity of a *supervised* orchestrator is a distinct, accepted residual
at the current maturity level, accepted precisely because a human authorizes each
governed effect; as supervision is removed, that residual is what the optional
hardening exists to remove, which is why autonomous operation makes the hardening
a requirement rather than an option. Stating the supervision/isolation trade
resolves both readings and makes the first MVP's admissibility determinate.

Severity rationale:: IMPORTANT, not BLOCKING. The product direction is
unaffected and no other finding depends on it. It is nevertheless the sentence a
README would have to get right, because it is the boundary between "WFC governs
your orchestration" and "WFC is called by your orchestration".

=== D-M1 -- MINOR -- The vocabulary carrying the only present-tense product claim is undefined and drifts

Location:: §1 (two occurrences), §5.1 limits table, §21.2.

Observation:: The single present-tense product claim in the corrected document
is that the BYO formulation "holds today for a trusted or cooperative caller
under external operator control". "Trusted or cooperative caller" is never
defined. A reader cannot determine whether an autonomous agentic orchestrator
that happens to be well-behaved qualifies -- which is precisely the population
most likely to read a future README and conclude that it does.

Separately, one concept carries three labels: "protected caller boundary" (§1),
"protected deployment boundary" (§11.1 OPEN and §24), and "caller mediation"
(§21.1 heading). §1 directs the reader to §11.1 for "protected caller boundary",
but §11.1 never uses that phrase.

Risk:: Low in the concept, which is internally readable. Material for the stated
downstream purpose: a README inherits the terms without the surrounding
paragraphs.

Smallest correction:: Define "cooperative caller" once, in §11.1, in terms of
what makes it cooperative -- most plausibly that a human authorizes each governed
effect and the caller is operated rather than autonomous -- and use one label for
the caller boundary throughout.

=== D-M2 -- MINOR -- "Durable" is overloaded, and Principle 12 sweeps in the disposable checkpoint

Location:: Design Principle 12 versus §12.1.

Observation:: §12.1 is titled "Three classes of durable state, not one blob" and
includes the operational checkpoint among those classes, where it may be changed
"Freely, by the engine". Principle 12 states "Level 1 owns the semantics; Level 0
owns the effects -- including bounded effects on Level-1's own durable
artifacts." Read literally against §12.1's own use of "durable", the principle
requires checkpoint writes to flow through WFC, which §12.1 explicitly does not
want.

The body resolves this in the "where each class lives" paragraph, which states
that the checkpoint "needs no governance, because losing or corrupting it costs
time rather than truth". The principles list -- the part a README writer is most
likely to lift verbatim -- does not carry the carve-out.

Smallest correction:: Either qualify Principle 12 ("...on Level-1's own
*authority-bearing or audit-preserving* artifacts"), or reserve "durable" in
§12.1 for the two governed classes and call the third "operational" or
"rebuildable" state.

== 7. Threat-model assessment

The maintainer's product-scope decision is expressed clearly and is, in itself,
architecturally defensible. Splitting by *actor kind* rather than by *capability*
is the right cut: agentic actors are the population WFC can meaningfully
constrain, because their authority is mediated by artifacts WFC admits, while
authorized administrators hold authority that predates and defines WFC.

The corrected text meets four of the five tests in the brief. It preserves a
meaningful agentic governance boundary (§11.1 in-scope list, Case A INVARIANT,
§11.6 effect-path precondition). It does not pretend to prevent deliberate
authorized-human bypass (explicit INVARIANT). It does not require a Zero-Trust
platform as a universal prerequisite (hardening is a HYPOTHESIS, explicitly
optional). It forecloses "the Higher Loop is polite and promises not to use its
shell" as sufficient architecture (§11.6, in those terms).

It fails the fifth test only partially: optional hardening *is* clearly
distinguished from baseline scope, but the distinction between what baseline
scope tolerates and what Case A forbids is not, which is D-I1.

One further observation in favour of the correction: the out-of-scope list is
specific rather than a general disclaimer. Naming host root, deliberate
governance edits, two-maintainer collusion, and out-of-band Git use makes the
assumption auditable by a deploying organization, which is exactly what allows
organizational controls to be placed against those specific cases.

== 8. Security-astronautics assessment

The guardrail holds and is well-placed. §11.1's closing subsection denies that
signed policy, hardware-backed identity, remote attestation, immutable external
audit storage, cryptographic approval provenance, separate administrative
security domains, or HSM-backed Git provenance are product prerequisites, while
preserving each as a possible future deployment option. The framing sentence --
"adopting them speculatively would trade a buildable governance layer for an
unbuildable platform" -- states the cost correctly.

The test required by the brief is whether this guardrail weakens any control
against the *agentic* actors inside the threat model. It does not. Every
mechanism disclaimed is a control against human or host-level adversaries, which
are Case B. The controls that bear on agentic actors -- admission, scope
domination, governance-path exclusion, digest re-verification, trusted evidence,
the trusted-commit path, review gates, fail-closed stops, and the future
derivation and containment work -- are untouched by the disclaimer, and §5.1
keeps their present status honest.

§12.1's protected Epic authority was checked for the same hazard, since "rooted
outside the engine" could be read as demanding an external trust infrastructure.
It does not: the representation is left OPEN and the requirement is relational
(outside the engine that operates under it), which an ordinary maintainer-owned
file or a separately governed repository surface can satisfy.

== 9. Current-versus-future truthfulness assessment

This is the strongest part of the correction. §5.1's three registers are
specific, each limit is paired with its consequence rather than stated as a
disclaimer, and the closing INVARIANT supplies a default reading rule for the

== 10. Level-1 authority and state assessment

worth naming: it separates *what may change* from *who may change it* and
attaches evidence to the transition rather than to the record. That is the same
shape as Level 0's own rule that persisted projections never become authority,
applied one level up, which is a genuine architectural continuity rather than a
borrowed slogan.

The specific mutations review 001 attacked are each blocked by the INVARIANT, and
`CHILD_ACCEPTED` is correctly singled out, since it is the one event where the
engine has both motive and a plausible-looking basis to self-certify.

Restart semantics are preserved and sharpened. The anti-pattern paragraph in
§12.2 -- a loop that re-derives its plan from a model call on every start, which
"has no memory, only a habit" -- states the failure concretely enough to be
testable in a later design review.

The added caveat that the existing prose record (WorkItem `authority_source`
fields, disposition notes, roadmap) is "good semantic material and a plausible
seed ... but not by itself a mechanically protected authority root" is a
correct and useful qualification that prevents the concept's own evidence from
being mistaken for an authority mechanism.

The strengthened thesis is coherent, orchestration-neutral, and compatible with
