= Semantic Backtracking Versus Lifecycle Forward Motion -- Concept Note
:toc:
:toclevels: 2

Status:: *NON-NORMATIVE CONCEPT NOTE. AUTHORIZES NOTHING. NOT IMPLEMENTATION
AUTHORITY.*

Date:: 2026-10-02.

Scope:: Names and preserves one architectural insight that became clearer
while designing correction-strategy convergence escape and while thinking
about future Level-1 orchestration: *semantic reconsideration may revisit or
completely reject an earlier assumption, design, plan, or strategy, but
lifecycle/history should still move only forward.* Knowledge may be revised;
history is not rewritten. Backtracking is semantic, not historical. This note
records the distinction, its generic shape, and where it already appears in
accepted and proposed WFC material. It proposes no mechanism, schema, or
record format.

== 1. The distinction

*[OBSERVED CURRENT FACT]* Two different kinds of "going backward" already
appear, under different names, in accepted and proposed WFC material:

* *Semantic backtracking* -- an actor, having seen new evidence, concludes
  that an earlier assumption, design, plan, interpretation, or strategy was
  incomplete, insufficient, or simply wrong, and adopts a different one going
  forward.
* *Lifecycle/history backtracking* -- erasing, mutating, or reinterpreting a
  past authoritative record (a Finding's disposition, an accepted design's
  acceptance, a Run's outcome, a commit) so that the record no longer
  truthfully reflects what was decided and when.

*[INVARIANT]* WFC's existing accepted mechanics already choose semantic
backtracking and refuse lifecycle backtracking, consistently, every time the
two could be confused. This note's only claim is that the choice is one
recurring principle, not several unrelated local rules, and that naming it
helps a future designer recognize the same shape instead of re-deriving it
under time pressure.

A later design may supersede, correct, or completely reject an earlier
accepted design. That does not make the earlier acceptance historically
false: the earlier design *was* accepted, under the authority and evidence
available at the time, and the record of that acceptance remains true. New
evidence causes a new forward lifecycle state, revision, or successor; it
does not rewrite the old authority's history. `FTR-WFC-CORRECTION-CONVERGENCE-ESCAPE_review_001.adoc`
already embodies this at small scale: the H002 rereview found two blocking
design requirements (`D001`, `D002`) in candidate `bef6211`; the H003
correction addressed them; the H004 rereview accepted the corrected
candidate. The H002 verdict was not deleted, edited, or reclassified as
having-never-happened once H004 accepted -- the review artifact *grew*, with
"Carried identifier disposition" sections closing `D001` and `D002` against
the H002 record, and the file still states "This section preserves the H002
review history above" immediately before recording the H004 verdict.

== 2. The generic forward-only shape

*[HYPOTHESIS]* Independent of WFC-specific vocabulary, the recurring shape is:

[source,text]
----
D1 accepted
  -> implementation / research evidence
  -> evidence shows D1 insufficient or wrong
  -> D2 / design revision / successor activity
  -> D2 independently accepted
  -> later work proceeds under D2
----

D2 may semantically return all the way to first principles -- it may reject
the problem framing D1 used, not merely patch D1's mechanism. What does not
reset is the process history: D1's acceptance, the evidence that followed it,
and the activity that produced D2 all remain inspectable facts, in the order
they actually happened. "D2 supersedes D1" is a true forward-dated statement
about two authoritative records; it is not a statement that D1 was never
accepted, or that the history between them can be collapsed away.

*[OPEN]* Whether this generic shape should ever be lifted into a reusable WFC
primitive -- shared vocabulary, a shared record shape, a shared validation
rule -- or whether it should simply keep recurring as a locally-justified
design choice at each scope where it applies, is unresolved. §7 and §8 record
why this note declines to choose.

== 3. Convergence-escape scope: the same principle, already present

The accepted convergence-escape design gives this note its most concrete
evidence, at the smallest scope.

*[OBSERVED CURRENT FACT]* `FTR-WFC-CORRECTION-CONVERGENCE-ESCAPE.adoc`'s
convergence mechanism is explicitly built so that:

* a qualified local-correction cycle "is retained even when it does not
  qualify" -- i.e. every correction/evaluation cycle remains in history
  whether or not it advances the goalpost count;
* an accepted strategy revision "returns to correction with the original
  Findings still open and a new strategy generation. One persisted escape
  event binds the complete triggering ID/generation set... Only a later
  independent evaluation with ordinary Finding disposition authority may
  close or supersede an original obligation";
* "the prior counts and all events remain historical, while each qualified
  count in the new generation starts at zero. Findings that did not trigger
  the event retain their existing generations and histories"; and
* there is deliberately "no persisted `goalpost_reached` boolean or mutable
  strategy counter" -- goalposts, generations, and counts are *derived*
  projections over an ordered, immutable Hop/Finding event history, chosen
  explicitly because "a second mutable value could drift."

A strategy reframe is semantic backtracking: the corrector's strategy for
satisfying an invariant is abandoned and replaced, potentially returning to a
completely different approach. Strategy generation `g+1` is semantic, not
historical, revision. What does not move backward is the record: the original
Finding's identity, every prior qualified or non-qualified cycle, the
diagnosis, the revision, and the acceptance all remain, in order, under their
original identities and generation numbers. "Diagnosis does not resolve
Findings, accept the subject, reset a count, or select a new profile" is the
same refusal stated from the authority side (§5 below); "generation `g + 1`...
exactly once for every `(Finding ID, g)` trigger pair" while "the prior counts
and all events remain historical" is the refusal stated from the persistence
side (§6 below).

*[INVARIANT]* Convergence escape does not erase failed correction cycles. A
strategy reframe creates a new strategy generation while prior generations
and their triggering events remain historical. Diagnosis and reframe must not
silently rewrite the authority under which earlier correction work occurred
-- they may conclude that strategy was wrong, but they cannot make it
retroactively never-attempted.

This is cited as existing accepted evidence for the generic shape, not as a
claim that the accepted design needs anything added. The accepted design
already closed exactly this gap (`D001`/`D002` in the H002 review, above);
this note observes the pattern, it does not correct the record.

== 4. Level-1 scope: the same principle, not yet designed

*[HYPOTHESIS]* The same shape is expected to recur at the much larger scope
`product_boundary_and_higher_order_orchestration_concept_2026-09-25.adoc`
names Level 1, where it is not yet designed and this note does not design it.

That concept already states the forward-only consequence for its own scope,
under different names:

* *"The last step is reconsider, not resume."* (§13.2) When a suspended
  parent's child work completes, the parent does not blindly continue a
  stale plan; it reconsiders itself against new repository truth. That is
  semantic backtracking at the plan level -- the parent's "next action" may
  be completely different afterward -- performed without mutating the
  Run/Hop history of either the parent or the child.
* *The `supersedes` edge* (§10.1) is explicitly "not decorative": the FK
  Cycle-3 `"nan"` obligation was absorbed by a later, unrelated correction,
  and a separate hand-written commit reconciled the stale roadmap statements
  that resulted -- additively, by recording the supersession, not by editing
  away the Cycle-3 record.
* *"Closure is evidence-based... not... exhaustion"* (§14.2): a goal may be
  judged closed, reopened on "residual work discovered," or closed on a
  legitimate negative result. Every one of those is a semantic judgment about
  the work; none of them is licence to make an earlier accepted plan revision
  or closure criterion never have existed.

*[OPEN]* Whether this note's principle becomes a named, generic WFC
invariant that both Level 0 and a future Level 1 must satisfy, a Level-1-only
rule (because Level 0's Finding/Hop/Run ledger already satisfies it
mechanically), or both independently, is unresolved and is explicitly left
to whichever later record actually designs Level-1 persistence
(`product_boundary...`'s §12.1, §24 "Level-1 design" open questions). This
note does not choose, and does not add a new open question to that concept's
own list -- it only observes that if and when that design happens, it should
recognize this shape rather than re-derive it.

A later orchestration layer may discover that the current decomposition,
design, prerequisite model, or lifecycle choice for a body of work was wrong
-- not merely that one child task failed. `product_boundary...`'s own
evidence basis already contains an example at Epic scope: the Relation-Key
cross-owner assessment "redirected the cross-owner key question away from a
one-sided FK Cycle-3 decision" (§13.1). *[HYPOTHESIS]* Such a discovery should
normally produce a new explicit activity, successor, or plan revision --
analogous to D2 in §2 -- rather than mutating a historical Run, silently
reinterpreting a closed Finding, or pretending an earlier lifecycle stage
never happened. `product_boundary...`'s own §12.1 already separates
"protected/admitted Epic authority" (revisable "only... through an explicit,
recorded revision") from the "audit-preserving semantic work state (ledger)"
for exactly this reason; this note adds no mechanism to that separation, only
the observation that it is the Level-1-scale instance of §2's shape.

== 5. Authority consequences

*[INVARIANT]* Discovery is not authority. An implementation, correction,
diagnosis, or review activity may discover that the design or plan governing
it is insufficient, ambiguous, or wrong. That discovery is evidence; it is
not itself permission to rewrite the governing design. This is already how
the accepted convergence-escape design treats diagnosis: "Diagnosis does not
resolve Findings, accept the subject, reset a count, or select a new
profile," and "a diagnosis, strategy-revision author, strategy gate, counter,
or route substitution also has no Finding disposition authority."

*[INVARIANT]* Crossing an authority or design boundary -- an activity
realizing it needs a different design, not just a different local fix --
may require a stop and a separately authorized successor or design-revision
activity, exactly as the accepted convergence-escape design requires an
`authority_scope_gap` diagnosis to "use the existing matching escalation and
human stop," because "merely choosing `authority_scope_gap` does not make an
otherwise unauthorized reframe route valid." `product_boundary...`'s §11.6
states the same rule at Level-1 scope: "no escalation path may terminate
inside the component that benefits from it."

The point of both citations is the same: semantic backtracking -- "this
strategy/design/plan is wrong" -- must route through whatever authority
already governs changing that strategy/design/plan. It must not become its
own authority merely by virtue of having discovered the problem.

== 6. Persistence and audit consequence

*[INVARIANT]* Historical facts and the authority that was effective at the
time remain inspectable after a later semantic reconsideration. Supersession
is additive: a new record referencing and superseding an old one, not a
rewritten old one. This note deliberately avoids proposing rollback semantics
that would require deciding which old Findings, reviews, budgets, Runs,
commits, or authority facts cease to have happened -- because no accepted or
proposed WFC record needs such a decision procedure, and inventing one is
exactly the kind of premature schema commitment this note's hard constraints
forbid.

The accepted convergence-escape design already supplies the concrete current
example: avoiding a persisted mutable convergence counter or goalpost flag
"prevents replay drift and makes the event source inspectable," and "the
escape event is not a mutable counter: it is an immutable routing decision
and correlation anchor." Replay over the same Run and pinned profile "must
derive byte-equivalent convergence projections and the same next route."
That is forward-only persistence for a single Run's correction strategy.
Whether the same additive, event-sourced shape is the right fit for Level-1's
much longer-lived ledger (`product_boundary...` §12.1's "audit-preserving
semantic work state") is exactly the kind of question that concept's own
§24 leaves open ("What is the durable Level-1 persistence representation...
append-only event sourcing left OPEN" per its §21.7 review-disposition
table) -- this note does not answer it, only notes that the answer, whatever
it is, should preserve the same property.

== 7. Relationship to existing concepts

This note does not duplicate, restate, or re-decide
`product_boundary_and_higher_order_orchestration_concept_2026-09-25.adoc`.
That concept already settled the Level-0/Level-1 product boundary, the
non-self-similarity argument (its §8), and the parent/child
suspend-reconsider-return mechanics (its §13); this note cites those sections
as evidence for a narrower, cross-cutting observation, and changes none of
their classification or disposition.

What this note adds, if anything, is sharpening rather than new ground: it
names one principle that is currently expressed independently in at least two
places -- the accepted convergence-escape design's escape/diagnosis/reframe
chain (a Level-0, single-Run mechanism) and `product_boundary...`'s
reconsider/supersede/closure language (a Level-1, multi-Run, as-yet-unbuilt
mechanism) -- and observes that a future Level-1 design should expect to
need the same forward-only property that Level 0 already has, rather than
treating it as a novel problem when it is encountered there. It constrains
nothing about *how* a future Level-1 design satisfies that property; it only
asserts that a design which fails to satisfy it (one that lets semantic
reconsideration mutate or erase prior authoritative history) should be
treated as a design error in the same way `product_boundary...` already
treats a design that collapses Level 0 and Level 1 into one self-similar
abstraction -- a failure mode that document exists specifically to prevent
(its §8, closing invariant).

This note is explicitly not a second Level-1 concept. It makes no claim about
work-graph shape, persistence representation, caller authority, containment,
or any of the twenty-five sections of `product_boundary...`; it touches only
the single cross-cutting property named in §1.

== 8. Open questions

Recorded, not answered. Several of these are narrower restatements of
questions `product_boundary...`'s own §24 already leaves open; they are
repeated here only where this note's framing adds a distinct angle.

* What is the exact representation of cross-Run supersession or design
  revision -- a typed event analogous to `ConvergenceEscape` but scoped above
  a single Run, a durable ledger entry, a commit-level convention, or
  something not yet imagined? This note takes no position.
* Does "semantic reconsideration moves only forward in lifecycle" become a
  named generic WFC invariant (applicable wherever WFC or a future Level 1
  keeps authoritative history), a Level-1-specific rule, or both,
  independently justified at each scope? §4 leaves this open deliberately.
* How should a future design revision be linked to the implementation
  activity it interrupts or redirects -- by the same kind of exact,
  immutable correlation set the accepted convergence-escape design uses for
  `ConvergenceEscape`/diagnosis/revision/assessment, by a looser reference,
  or by a mechanism specific to whatever Level-1 persistence is eventually
  chosen?
* How should "return addresses" and successor relationships be represented
  across a boundary that -- unlike a single Run's Finding ledger -- may span
  many Runs, many days, and (per `product_boundary...`'s §13.3) possibly a
  graph rather than a stack?
* Which transitions along this forward-only history require human semantic
  authority specifically (as opposed to an independent reviewer, an
  evaluator gate, or a protected non-human authority source per
  `product_boundary...`'s §11.6)? The accepted convergence-escape design
  answers this question for its own single-Run scope (gate/human Finding
  disposition authority only); whether the same answer, a stricter one, or a
  looser one is right at Level-1 scope is unresolved and is not decided here.
