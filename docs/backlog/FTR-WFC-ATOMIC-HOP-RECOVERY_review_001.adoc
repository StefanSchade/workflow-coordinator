== 2. Findings

=== F-B1 (BLOCKING) -- "accepted Hops are the only semantic history" contradicts the uncertain-dispatch charge the FTR claims to preserve

Recovery invariant 1 states that "Accepted Hops remain the only semantic
history that can update Findings, route/phase, accepted HEAD, or the semantic
Hop budget", and the budget section states that "`max_autonomous_hops` counts
accepted Hops only in the target model".

That is false of the behavior the FTR elsewhere promises to keep.
`recover_uncertain_dispatch` appends a Hop whose `outcome` is `uncertain` and
whose `invocation_status` is `acceptance_not_disproved` into `run.hops`,
increments `hop_used` through `record_hop`, and advances `current_head` to the
independently observed head (`persistence.py:112-160`). That Hop is by the
FTR's own taxonomy *not* accepted -- no candidate was accepted, no Attempt was
accepted -- yet it is semantic history and it consumes semantic budget. Slice R2
simultaneously promises to "Keep conservative charged/reconciliation behavior
for every uncertain or mutated case."

The document therefore contains two mutually exclusive rules, and the one
stated as an *invariant* is the wrong one. An implementer reading invariant 1
literally would conclude that an uncertain dispatch must stop charging
`max_autonomous_hops`, which is precisely the fail-closed weakening the review
brief's section 6 asks to be prevented. The confusion is not hypothetical
vocabulary drift: the repository already uses `invocation_status="accepted"`
for a semantically *failed* Hop (`vertical.py:286`), so "accepted Hop" is an
actively ambiguous term against existing code.

Required correction: replace the single word "accepted" with an explicit
three-way charging rule. A defensible formulation, consistent with both current
code and the FTR's intent:

* a *charged Hop* is any Hop appended to `run.hops`; it always consumes
  `max_autonomous_hops`;
* an Attempt whose candidate was validated and correlated produces a charged
  Hop *regardless of its semantic `Outcome`*, including `Outcome.FAILED`;
* an Attempt that cannot be disproved as having been accepted produces a
  charged *uncertain* Hop and `reconcile_required`;
* only a *proven-transient, candidate-free, fully quiescent* discarded Attempt
  produces no Hop at all.

Then restate invariant 1 in terms of reduction authority rather than history:
only a Hop carrying a validated correlated candidate may reach
`reduce_result`. That is both true today and true in the target model.

=== F-I1 (IMPORTANT) -- `Outcome.FAILED` budget treatment is never stated, although the brief marks it the decisive terminology case

The FTR says a valid semantic `Outcome.FAILED` "is not made transient merely
because another model invocation might produce a better answer", which settles
*retry eligibility*. It never states whether such an invocation is an accepted
Hop that consumes semantic budget. Under `record_hop` it does
(`model.py:405-430`, `vertical.py:286`), and under `WFC-AUTO-02` it must
("whether it succeeds, fails, or yields malformed output").

Because the target model is described as counting "accepted Hops only", a
reader can reach the opposite conclusion by reading "accepted" as "succeeded".
The revision must say, in one sentence and in the budget section rather than
the taxonomy table, that a validated correlated candidate consumes one semantic
Hop irrespective of `Outcome`, and that this is unchanged from today.

=== F-I2 (IMPORTANT) -- the retry predicate over-claims "no unresolved side effect"; ignored paths are invisible to the exact primitive it relies on

The retry predicate requires "clean tracked/untracked state and resolved
submodules" plus "no unresolved external side effect". The first is a real
observable; the second is not derivable from it, and the FTR's general caveat
("Repository cleanliness proves only repository cleanliness") is stated once in
prose and then not reflected in the conjunctive list, which reads as if the
list is sufficient.

The concrete gap is repository-grounded and specific:

* `_observe_repository` runs `git status --porcelain=v1 --untracked-files=all`
  (`git_facts.py:222`), which by construction excludes *ignored* paths.
* This checkout ignores `__pycache__/`, `*.py[cod]`, `.pytest_cache/`,
  `.venv/`, `build/`, `dist/`, `*.egg-info/`, `.coverage`, and `htmlcov/`
  (`.gitignore`).
* The agent runs under `--sandbox workspace-write` (`codex_adapter.py:146-153`),
  which authorizes writes anywhere in the workspace, ignored paths included.
* The Coordinator then executes trusted `pytest` as evidence with
  `cwd=command.repository` and the Coordinator's inherited environment
  (`evidence.py:259-261`, `governance/self-development/repository-policy.yaml`).

A discarded Attempt can therefore leave ignored-path content that a later
trusted evidence execution imports and runs with Coordinator authority, while
`observe_repository` truthfully reports the worktree clean. That is exactly the
confused-deputy transition described in
`docs/backlog/FTR-WFC-EXECUTION-TRUST-BOUNDARIES.adoc`.

Honesty requires two qualifications, and the revision should make both:

. This hazard is *pre-existing*, not created by retry. It already spans Hop N to
  Hop N+1 within one Run, because `run_serial_hops` re-enters with the same
  repository and the same evidence commands (`operator.py:539-594`). Retry does
  not introduce a new class of exposure; it adds one more crossing of an
  already-unguarded boundary.
. It nevertheless falsifies the predicate's *claim*. The revision should either
  drop "no unresolved side effect" from the machine-derived conjunction and
  demote it to a named residual risk, or add an explicit dependency on the
  deferred containment work in `FTR-WFC-EXECUTION-TRUST-BOUNDARIES` before R2
  may be implemented.

The related review brief items are disposed as follows: submodules are covered
(`unresolved_submodules`, `git_facts.py:230-232`); untracked non-ignored files
are covered (`git_facts.py:222-229`); *ignored* files, writes outside the
repository, subprocess descendants that escape the killed process group,
network and credential effects, and mutable caches are *not* covered by any
current primitive and must be named as such.

=== F-I3 (IMPORTANT) -- `allowed next action` is outer-loop policy leaking inward, and the repository already contains the correct pattern

The watchdog seam proposes exposing "allowed next action such as
`continue_session`, `retry_same_hop`, `fresh_successor`, `human_reconcile`, or
`none`, with required authority", and then a decision table mapping classes to
actions.

Mixing a classification fact with a chosen action in one field is the same
coupling the FTR's own non-goals reject ("solving the outer
coordinator/watchdog"). It also duplicates authority: the inner WFC would be
asserting what a future outer coordinator is permitted to do, while having no
knowledge of that coordinator's budget, task selection, or human policy.

The repository already demonstrates the right shape.
`reducer.permitted_route_keys` (`reducer.py:579`) derives the *finite permitted
set* from profile and status, is published into the checkpoint as
`next_permitted_route_keys` (`persistence.py:218`), and is documented as
deriving "finite choices; route authority is never read from Run state". Route
*selection* remains the agent's request and the reducer's validation, not a
value read back from state.

Recommended correction: rename the field to something like
`permitted_recovery_actions`, define it as the mechanically derived set of
actions whose predicates currently hold (possibly empty), and state explicitly
that WFC never selects among them and never initiates a successor Run. Delete
the decision table, or move it into an explicitly non-normative "an outer loop
might apply" note. This is answer *B* to the brief's question 11, with the
addition that a *permitted set* is still a useful fail-closed contract -- an
empty set is a genuine inner-WFC fact -- whereas a single chosen action is not.

=== F-I4 (IMPORTANT) -- the cheapest competing design is never evaluated: the reconcile/resume seam already exists and is merely unwired

The FTR's comparison of same-Hop retry against fresh successor Runs is a false
dichotomy. There is a third option, strictly cheaper than R2, that the document
does not mention:

`reducer.resume_after_reconcile` (`reducer.py:559`) already transitions a
`RECONCILE_REQUIRED` Run back to `READY` after verifying the phase resolves,
`run.anomalies` is empty, `current_head` is unchanged, and `hop_used` is within
budget. `serialization.load_run` and `persistence.load_dispatch_marker` already
exist. What is missing is only an operator entrypoint, because
`operator.main` refuses an existing state root (`operator.py:224-225`).

The consequence for the FTR's decisive case is material. Its argument for R2
rests almost entirely on Run-local Finding preservation: "if H001 review created
F001 and H002 correction hit quota before an accepted result, a clean same-Hop
H002 retry preserves F001. A fresh Run cannot treat F001 as authoritative
without repeating review." That is true of a *fresh Run*. It is not true of
reconcile-then-resume: the Run object, its `findings` tuple, its phase, and its
pinned governing identity all survive in `run.json`, and resume continues at
H003 with F001 intact.

The real residual value of R2 over a wired resume command is therefore
narrower than the FTR presents: *one charged Hop of budget, and one human
touch*. On this repository's actual work items that is one Hop out of three
(`governance/work-items/developer-ergonomics-review-005.yaml`,
`max_autonomous_hops: 3`).

The revision must add this third option to the comparison table and re-argue
R2 against it, not only against fresh successor Runs. This review's own
assessment of the three brief cases, with that option included:

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Case |Simplest correct operation |Reason

|A -- H001 review creates F001, H002 correction hits quota
|Reconcile + resume (wired), not same-Hop retry
|Findings are preserved either way. Retry saves exactly one charged Hop over
resume. That saving does not justify adapter classification, Attempt lifecycle,
crash-safe multi-marker ordering, and caps as a *first* implementation.

|B -- a bad Agent Operating Contract is exposed and corrected
|Fresh successor Run
|Governing truth changed; the old Run must stay frozen. The FTR is correct and
this case needs no new mechanism. It is already operationally routine here: a
new WorkItem YAML with an explicit `initial_phase` plus one operator
invocation, as `developer-ergonomics-review-00{2,3,4,5}.yaml` demonstrate.

|C -- a clean review Hop hits quota before producing any result
|Fresh successor Run
|No Run-local Findings exist to preserve and `initial_phase` is already
selectable. Retry buys only the avoidance of a new state root.
|===

The FTR's conclusion that fresh successor Runs are "not an adequate substitute
for ALL transient retries" is literally true but load-bearing beyond its
strength: it is carried entirely by Case A, and Case A is served by a cheaper
mechanism the FTR does not consider.

=== F-I5 (IMPORTANT) -- the motivating event has no recorded occurrence in this repository, and the FTR does not say so

The document is "Recorded now" because "Provider quota exhaustion and other
transient failures expose a gap". No repository evidence of such an event is
cited, and this review found none. Across seven dogfood attempts
(`docs/backlog/first_wfc_on_wfc_dogfood_attempt_{2..7}*.adoc` and the closure
records), every recorded failure is semantic, governance, or implementation
defect. The strings `uncertain_dispatch` and `acceptance_not_disproved` appear
nowhere in `docs/` outside this FTR and the imported P5 record, i.e. the
conservative recovery path has been exercised only by tests
(`tests/unit/test_operator.py:288-303`), never in operation. Runs in this
repository are 1--3 Hops.

This does not invalidate the concept, and the FTR's staging is the right
instinct. But an architecture-convergence document that proposes an Attempt
lifecycle, scratch sealing infrastructure, process re-entry, provider
continuation, and a watchdog seam should state plainly that its motivating
frequency is currently *unmeasured*, and should make R2 conditional on measured
occurrences rather than on R0/R1 merely being possible. The revision should add
an explicit evidence gate, for example: R2 is not opened until the R1 ledger
records transient, candidate-free, quiescent Attempts at a rate that makes the
one-Hop resume cost material.

== 3. Minor findings

=== F-M1 (MINOR) -- attempt-specific result paths are a liveness and observability need, not a correctness precondition

The trace section states that a retry design "cannot simply overwrite the
current single marker or fixed result file". The marker half is right. The
result-file half over-states the risk, and the over-statement matters because it
inflates R2's apparent cost.

`CodexCliAdapter.execute` truncates `result-message.json` before launch
(`codex_adapter.py:163`) and reads it after
(`codex_adapter.py:181`), and `validated_result` rejects any envelope whose
`(run_id, hop_id, invocation_id)` triple does not match the dispatched
`Invocation` (`adapter.py:112-115`). Because every Attempt allocates a fresh
`invocation_id` (`operator.py:560`), a stale or orphan-written result from a
predecessor Attempt *cannot* be mistaken for a successor's result; it fails
correlation and falls into the existing conservative uncertain path.

The genuine cost is loss of progress, not loss of safety: an orphaned
descendant that writes the fixed path after the successor's own write converts
a valid successor result into a charged uncertain Hop. The revision should
re-characterize attempt-specific paths accordingly, and should credit
invocation correlation as an already-present safety property that R2 inherits
rather than must build.

=== F-M2 (MINOR) -- the crash-safe marker ordering is named as a requirement but never stated, although it is the one ordering rule that actually matters

The FTR asks for "an ordering that cannot lose the fact that an earlier
invocation may have run" and lists the precise transition as an open question.
The rule is derivable today and should simply be written down, because getting
it backwards is the only way R2 can produce an unsafe replay.

Today `dispatch.json` is a single fixed path (`slice4.py:174`), written before
execution (`slice4.py:176`) and unlinked only after the post-Hop Run and
checkpoint are durable (`slice4.py:220-222`). `recover_uncertain_dispatch`
is already idempotent against a marker whose `hop_id` is present in `run.hops`
(`persistence.py:128-161`), so a crash between post-Hop publication and marker
retirement cannot double-charge.

The rule R2 needs is therefore: *no window may exist in which no marker
covers a possibly-live Attempt.* The successor Attempt's marker must be durable
before the predecessor's is retired, never the reverse; and since both markers
share `run_id`, `hop_id`, `sequence`, and `base_head`, and differ only in
`invocation_id`, a crash inside that overlap still recovers to exactly one
charged uncertain Hop under the existing logic. Stating this converts the open
question into a bounded design constraint and removes the temptation to build a
transaction system.

=== F-M3 (MINOR) -- the rendered Codex argv omits authority-relevant flags

The FTR renders the adapter invocation as
`codex exec --ephemeral --json --sandbox workspace-write --output-schema ... --output-last-message ... -`
and marks it "approximately". The actual argv (`codex_adapter.py:146-153`) also
carries `--ask-for-approval never`, `--color never`, `--model` (default
`gpt-5.6-terra`), `-c model_reasoning_effort="medium"`, and `--cd <repository>`.

`--ask-for-approval never` and the model/reasoning binding are not cosmetic in
a retry document: the binding is part of what a retry would have to hold
constant or deliberately vary, and the FTR itself lists "binding/capability
facts" in the Attempt record while its own argv rendering hides them.

=== F-M4 (MINOR) -- the FTR does not name the accepted normative rules its R2 would revise

The FTR correctly says "A future same-Hop retry must explicitly revise that
narrow contract rather than pretend it was already supported", but references
`FTR-AGENT-WORKFLOW-COORDINATOR-P5` only as a document. The rules concerned are
identified there by ID:

* `WFC-AUTO-02` -- "Every invocation accepted by the agent adapter consumes one
  Hop whether it succeeds, fails, or yields malformed output... once the atomic
  dispatch marker exists, acceptance that cannot be disproved is conservatively
  charged as one Hop and never replayed."
  (`docs/imported/core-incubator/FTR-AGENT-WORKFLOW-COORDINATOR-P5.adoc:505-512`)
* `WFC-AUTO-03` -- "V1 has no hidden retry."
  (`...P5.adoc:514-517`)
* `WFC-AUTO-05` -- resume requires explicit maintainer action.

A convergence FTR that proposes narrowing an accepted normative rule should
name the rule IDs and the exact narrowing, so the later supersession is
reviewable as a delta rather than as a re-reading.

== 4. Informational observations

=== F-N1 (INFO) -- Run -> Hop -> Attempt is justified, but the minimal form is a record extension, not a new lifecycle entity

Answering the brief's section 2 adversarially: a separate Attempt *lifecycle
entity* is not needed, and a durable Attempt *record* is.

Against a new entity: three of the four facts an Attempt needs are already
durable and already correlated. `Invocation` carries
`(run_id, hop_id, invocation_id)` (`adapter.py:32-35`); `DispatchMarker`
persists those plus `sequence`, `base_head`, and `budget_reserved`
(`model.py:363`); `execution-evidence.json` is already `schema_version: 2` with
a row array literally named `attempts` (`operator.py:525-528`) recording
binding, timeout, return code, timeout flag, candidate presence, HEAD range,
commits, and trusted observations.

What is genuinely missing is small and is exactly three things: rows are keyed
off `run.hops[-1]` instead of off the invocation (`operator.py:493-495`); the
exception path `recover_uncertain(None)` (`slice4.py:218-219`) produces no row
at all; and there is no Attempt ordinal or structured disposition. R1 can be
implemented as decoupling the evidence row from `run.hops`, always emitting a
row, and adding `attempt_ordinal` plus
`disposition ∈ {not_started, discarded, accepted, uncertain}`. That is an
extension of an existing published artifact, not a second state machine.

For R2 the durable record becomes necessary rather than merely useful, because
several `invocation_id` values then map to one `hop_id` and the marker must
distinguish them. The FTR's conclusion is therefore correct; its framing as a
new lifecycle tier is heavier than the evidence requires and should be softened
to "Attempt is a durable record subordinate to a Hop, carried by the existing
evidence and marker surfaces".

Answering the brief's remaining sub-questions: the ledger is necessary for
retry *correctness* only from R2 onward; for R1 it is observability, which is
its stated purpose and is legitimate. `HopSummary` should gain an accepted
`invocation_id` reference, because otherwise the accepted Attempt is
identifiable only by position in a separately published file, which is exactly
the correlation weakness the rest of the system avoids.

=== F-N2 (INFO) -- provider continuation is already decidable from static local facts; R0 should shrink accordingly

The FTR treats provider continuation as an open investigation and lists it
first in its decision summary. Two static facts, observed locally without
invoking a provider, make most of that branch decidable now
(`codex-cli 0.147.0`):

* `codex exec --ephemeral` is documented as "Run without persisting session
  files to disk";
* `codex exec resume` is documented as "Resume a previous session by id or pick
  the most recent with --last", operating on *recorded* sessions.

These are mutually exclusive by definition. Provider-session resume is not
merely unwired in WFC; it is structurally unavailable for any invocation that
passes `--ephemeral`. Enabling it would require persisting provider-owned
session state, which is untrusted execution state living outside the external
state root that `slice4._require_external_state_root` deliberately keeps
disjoint from the agent workspace, and which the imported governance review
already treats as an information-classification surface.

This review's position: WFC should keep `--ephemeral` and decline provider
session continuation, recording that as a deliberate architectural property
rather than an open question. The FTR's own R0 text already asks the right
question ("Decide explicitly whether WFC wants a persistent provider session at
all. A provider session is untrusted execution state, not authoritative Run
state") -- but its decision summary and taxonomy give continuation first
position and full taxonomy weight, which is not neutral. The revision should
either resolve it as a stated non-goal or demote it below retry.

What remains genuinely unknown, and is the only thing R0 should investigate, is
whether the `--json` JSONL event stream or the exit code carries a stable
structured quota/transient signal. Today that stream is captured only as a
16 KiB tail (`codex_adapter._bounded`, `codex_adapter.py:84`), is reduced to
free-text `diagnostics` (`codex_adapter.py:182-184`), and is dropped entirely
from `execution-evidence.json` (`operator.py:493-504`). The FTR's refusal to
classify quota by matching prose such as "5 hour limit" is correct and should be
retained verbatim.

R0 so narrowed is cheap: persist the JSONL stream and exit code for real
invocations and read them afterwards. It requires no new semantics and can be
merged into R1.

=== F-N3 (INFO) -- the scratch section is the FTR's least evidence-backed material and should be compressed, not deleted

Answering the brief's section 10: option *B*, premature overdesign, with the
core semantics kept.

The intended semantics ("salvage, not resume") are right, and the security
posture is mostly right: the FTR explicitly says that "Labels and delimiters do
not contain prompt injection" and that least-authority sandboxing, no automatic
execution, no authority derivation, and independent verification "remain the
real controls". That is the correct statement of the brief's warning -- the
prompt warning is not itself a security boundary -- and it is stated strongly
enough.

Three problems remain. First, proportionality: roughly seventy lines specify a
directory layout, a manifest, eligibility predicates, and exact prompt wording
for a mechanism the FTR itself defers past R2, for a failure whose frequency is
unmeasured (F-I5). Second, one eligibility gate does not exist: "compatible
information classification and successor reachable surface" presumes a
classification mechanism, and `prompt.ContextItem` (`prompt.py:89-95`) carries
only `reference` and `content`, with every item rendered into an undifferentiated
`[context]` block (`prompt.py:172-177`). There is no label to compare. Third,
the sandbox exposure claim is directional only: a "narrow per-Attempt scratch
exchange, bounded by the sandbox" is asserted without reference to any existing
containment, and `FTR-WFC-EXECUTION-TRUST-BOUNDARIES` explicitly lists
sandboxing as *candidate* direction, not an implemented guarantee.

Recommended: compress to a named deferred option carrying only the
non-negotiables -- never authoritative, never a Finding or disposition, never
governing input, never restored session state, Coordinator-sealed after process
termination, no authority widening to make it readable, no automatic execution
-- plus an explicit prerequisite on a prompt trust-label mechanism that does not
yet exist. Move the layout, manifest schema, and prompt wording into a note
marked as illustration pending evidence.

== 5. Disposition of the remaining brief questions

Current conservative contract (brief section 6):: R2 can revise the contract for
a machine-proven subset *provided* F-B1 is corrected. As written, invariant 1
would weaken the fail-closed path for uncertain dispatch, which is the one case
that must not change. The FTR is otherwise correct and careful here: its
eligibility is conjunctive and positive, semantic failure and malformed output
are explicitly excluded from transient classification, governing/authority
change forces a fresh successor Run, and Finding import between Runs is a stated
non-goal. `_governing_inputs_unchanged` and the inter-Hop
`stop_continuation("governing_input_changed", ...)` path
(`operator.py:555-559`) already enforce the governing-truth half of this at Hop
granularity, which the revision should cite as the precedent it extends.

Budget model (brief section 7):: The separation of semantic Hop budget from
technical Attempt allowance is correct and is the FTR's cleanest idea. Leaving
the configuration location open is appropriate and should not be forced. Two
gaps: the FTR does not say that Attempt allowance must be bounded by something
*durable* across a Coordinator crash -- an in-memory retry counter would silently
reset and produce the unbounded loop the section is meant to prevent -- and it
does not state that exhausting Attempt allowance must fall back to the existing
charged/reconcile behavior rather than to a new terminal state. Both belong in
the revision. The claim that continuation consumes no Attempt is consistent but
becomes moot if F-N2 is accepted.

Process-loss scope (brief section 12):: R1 and R2 can and should be implemented
for an intact Coordinator process only. Durable Attempt identity does not imply
process-level re-entry: the marker already survives a crash and
`recover_uncertain_dispatch` already handles re-entry idempotently at the Hop
level; an Attempt ordinal adds a field to artifacts that are already atomically
published (`persistence.atomic_write_json`, `persistence.py:88`). The FTR's own
open question ("Should process-loss recovery be introduced with R2 or only
after safe in-process retry has produced evidence?") should be closed in the
revision as *after*, with one exception: the resume entrypoint argued in F-I4 is
itself a process-level re-entry command and is worth building first, because it
serves Case A at a fraction of R2's cost.

Relationship to other security work (brief section 14):: No conflict found. The
FTR does not attempt generic elevated privilege, correctly names
`FTR-WFC-ELEVATED-PRIVILEGE-DEFENSIVE-REVIEW` as the separate deferred
mechanism, and does not weaken the trust-boundary direction. Two cross-reference
corrections are needed and are covered above: the scratch section asserts
sandbox bounding that `FTR-WFC-EXECUTION-TRUST-BOUNDARIES` lists only as
candidate direction (F-N3), and the retry predicate's side-effect claim depends
on containment that does not yet exist (F-I2). The FTR's statement that scratch
"respects the trust-boundary principles" should be weakened to "depends on"
them.

FTR quality and decision readiness (brief section 16):: Structure is good. The
document does separate current repository truth, target semantics,
recommendations, non-goals, and open questions, and its "Current implementation
facts" section is the most accurate part of it. Speculative material worded as
established fact is confined to the budget invariant (F-B1), the predicate's
side-effect sufficiency (F-I2), and the necessity framing of attempt-specific
result paths (F-M1). The internal contradiction is F-B1 against the R2 text;
terminology drift is the overloading of "accepted" across invocation status,
candidate validation, Attempt selection, reducer route acceptance, and semantic
history.

== 7. Architectural assessment summary

Strongest positive:: The separation of semantic Hop budget from technical
Attempt allowance, combined with the refusal to relabel `Outcome.FAILED`,
malformed output, reducer preemption, or authority defects as transient. That
boundary is the load-bearing idea, it is correct, and it keeps the reducer and
Finding layers entirely out of the retry mechanism -- which is why the concept
is genuinely small where it matters.

Strongest concern:: The document commits to the more expensive of two
mechanisms for its own decisive case without evaluating the cheaper one that
already exists in the repository in unwired form, while the motivating failure
has never been observed in operation. Combined with the budget-vocabulary
contradiction, that is enough to require revision before implementation.
