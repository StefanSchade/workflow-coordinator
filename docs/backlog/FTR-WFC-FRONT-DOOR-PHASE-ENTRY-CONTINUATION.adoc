[[design]]
== Design and evaluation (2026-11-27, slice O9, `WI-WFC-BETA2-O9-001`)

Written under the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-26 and the Beta rule --
the existence of AD-6 is no argument by itself, the change is judged on
benefit, effort, risk and dependencies, and what must survive are the
*intended* safety and governance properties rather than their present
realisation. Re-derived from the repository at base HEAD `44ff936`; no
acceptance credit is taken from AD-6's own reasoning.

[[design-decision]]
=== Decision

*Do not ship a non-default entry phase now. No code changes in this slice.*

The capability stays desired. What this evaluation adds to AD-6's deferral is
that the deferral no longer rests on "the substitute works", but on three
findings that are stronger and were not previously recorded:

. On the current product surface the *only* entry that saves anything is an
  entry at `broad_review`, and it saves exactly one Hop
  (<<design-benefit>>).
. That one saved Hop *is* the independence of the assurance. The Hop the
  substitute spends is the Hop that produces the object of review; a
  `broad_review` entry has none, and one invocation would both perform and
  accept the verification (<<design-vacuity>>). This Commission's withheld
  authority forbids exactly that form, and no cheap variant avoids it.
. Every other declared entry is *no better* than the default: a `correct`
  entry needs three Hops to acceptance where `implement` needs two, and a
  `focused_review` entry needs the same two while opening on an empty Finding
  ledger (<<design-benefit>>). A field limited to the entries that are safe
  would therefore ship a new public surface with no beneficial instance at
  all.

[[design-benefit]]
=== What the capability is actually worth

`robust@v1` declares four phases and exactly one terminal route
(`broad_review.accept`, `effect: complete`). Minimum Hops from each declared
entry to acceptance, computed from the accepted program literals
(`commissioning.py:134-160`):

[cols="1,1,1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Entry phase |Role |Min Hops |What it means

|`implement` (today's only rendered entry)
|worker
|2
|Worker candidate, then the program's own independent review.

|`broad_review`
|independent_reviewer
|1
|One reviewer invocation accepts and completes the Run. Saves one Hop; see
<<design-vacuity>>.

|`focused_review`
|independent_reviewer
|2
|No saving, and it opens by disposing of prior Findings that a fresh Run does
not have.

|`correct`
|worker
|3
|`correct` -> `focused_review` -> `renew_broad_review` -> `broad_review`.
*One Hop more expensive* than the default entry.
|===

`simple@v1` declares one phase, so the field is inexpressible there.

The benefit is therefore one Hop per final-assurance or re-review Commission.
Measured frequency: the three (in fact four, the P1 verification ran twice)
final-assurance Commissions AD-6 cites, across the whole of Beta-1 and
post-Beta; and *none* of the 21 friction entries of
link:../warm_storage/post-beta-planning/wfc_beta2_operator_efficiency_analysis.adoc[the
Beta-2 operator-efficiency analysis] (candidate (c)) records a Run that needed
a non-default entry. One Hop, about four times so far.

[[design-vacuity]]
=== Why a review-phase entry cannot be made safe cheaply

A review phase in `robust@v1` judges a Worker candidate produced earlier in
the *same* Run. Entering at `broad_review` removes that candidate, and nothing
mechanical replaces it:

* *No object of review is projected.* The Coordinator's reconstruction context
  emits `prior_hop`, `prior_range`, `prior_commits` and
  `prior_changed_paths` only under `if run.hops:` (`operator.py:707-723`). At
  `H001` there are no Hops, so a reviewer entering at `broad_review` receives
  no range, no commit and no changed path -- only `base_head` equal to the
  baseline and the Commission's task prose.
* *Nothing binds the verdict to a candidate.* The review-verdict contract
  carries `verdict`, Findings, evidence references and a summary
  (`structured_result_schema.py:209-239`); no field names what was reviewed,
  and the reducer has nothing to check a verdict against. An `accept` at
  `H001` is therefore an acceptance whose object exists only in prose, and it
  carries the same `completed` classification, the same accepted route and the
  same archived independent verdict as any other accepted `robust@v1` Run.
  Only the missing Worker Hop in the Hop list distinguishes it, and nothing in
  the admission chain, the reducer or the archive refuses it.
* *It is self-acceptance, not a saved Hop.* The substitute is not a
  workaround for the missing field; it is the mechanism that makes the
  assurance two-party. In each cited precedent the Worker Hop *wrote the
  verification record* that the independent review then judged -- see
  link:../warm_storage/post-beta-planning/wfc_postbeta_plan_p1_final_assurance_verification.adoc[the
  P1 final-assurance verification record] ("Sections 1-6 were written by
  `WI-WFC-POSTBETA-PLAN-P1-FINAL-ASSURANCE-001` H001 (Worker) ... The
  independent reviewer re-verifies the whole final plan from scratch after
  this Hop"). A `broad_review` entry collapses those two invocations into one
  that verifies and accepts its own verification.

The only form that is *not* vacuous pins the object of review in the
Commission itself (a reviewed commit range, verified at admission to be
non-empty and an ancestor of the baseline, projected to the reviewer as
trusted context and shown in the preview and the digest). That is new trusted
Run and request state, not AD-6's "one request field".

[[design-mechanics]]
=== The four mechanics the slice asked about

*Durable-artifact admission* (`operator.py:530-558` for the mapped form,
`:559-583` for the legacy form). In the *mapped* form -- the only form
`commission render` produces -- admission is entry-independent: every phase
declaring `durable_artifact: required` must carry mapped paths inside the
WorkItem and phase scope, whichever phase is the entry. A `broad_review` entry
is admitted unchanged, and this qualifies the analysis's note that review
entry "interacts with durable-artifact admission": the interaction is confined
to the *legacy* `--durable-artifact` path, which is entry-dependent and which
*already refuses* a review entry, because `broad_review` is both
required-artifact and a `renew_broad_review` target
(`operator.py:575-582`, tested at `tests/unit/test_slice5b.py:384-396`).

*The phase's `authorized_scope`.* Unchanged and correct: the review phase's
scope is the artifact slot `A`, so a reviewer entering at `H001` may write
only its own review artifact and could not produce a candidate even if asked
to. This is a reason the entry cannot be made meaningful by widening the
reviewer's authority -- doing so would merge the Worker and reviewer roles.

*Finding state.* Empty at entry, which is harmless: `broad_review` holds
`finding_authority: [resolve]`, may propose new Findings with
`finding_id: null`, and may route `correction`, so the correction loop behaves
normally. A `focused_review` entry is the degenerate case -- its declared
purpose is the disposition of prior broad/focused Findings, of which a fresh
Run has none.

*The post-Hop scope check.* Unaffected by the entry phase: the trusted-commit
boundary and `observe_hop` both take `allowed_paths=phase.authorized_scope`
against `run.current_head` (`vertical.py:405-427`), which is already exercised
for review phases at every re-review and reads nothing about which phase
started the Run.

*Execution bindings* (not asked, but load-bearing). Both the compiler and the
preflight derive the expected phase-binding set from
`profile.executing_phase_roles(work_item.initial_phase)`
(`commissioning.py:441`, `operator.py:481`), so a `broad_review` entry would
*refuse* a config that binds `implement`, and `wfc plan`'s "effective
execution by phase" would change shape with the entry. This part needs no new
code and is evidence that the kernel is genuinely entry-generic.

[[design-successor]]
=== Interaction with successor preparation

The capability already exists where it is safe, and is missing only where it
is not. `wfc prepare-successor` does not render: it consumes a *hand-authored*
WorkItem, profile and policy at pinned paths and admits them through the same
`_admit_a1`/`_preflight` chain (`operator.py:1753-1771`), so a prepared
successor can already carry any declared `initial_phase` today. The gap is
only in `commission render` / `wfc plan` / `wfc request`
(`commissioning.py:374`, the literal `"initial_phase": "implement"`).

That channel cannot supply the missing object of review either. The successor
descriptor is `trust_label: untrusted_context_only` by design and records only
the predecessor's `final_head` (`operator.py:1814-1826`); the successor's own
baseline is a freshly integrated head, so the predecessor's candidate range is
not derivable from trusted state. `SuccessorProvenance` carries
`predecessor_final_head` but not the predecessor's baseline.

And this record's own motivating Run -- a mechanical transport stop between a
trusted-committed `implement` Hop and its `broad_review` Hop,
`RUN-6ebce1ac10df4e099e93848d6c292f8b` -- is not an entry-phase case at all.
It is a *same-Run* continuation: there the candidate, its range and the Run's
Finding state all exist. `_predecessor_for_successor()` is categorically
ineligible for it (`operator.py:1706-1727`: `AWAITING_HUMAN` plus a non-null
`semantic_stop`), which is correct. link:beta2_operator_efficiency_plan.adoc#o10[Slice
O10] is the remedy for it. Shipping an entry field would address the
motivating case only by re-expressing a same-Run recovery as a fresh Run whose
review has nothing to review.

[[design-closure]]
=== The second negative constraint has no instance today

AD-6 requires that a phase unreachable from a terminal route be refused.
Computed from both accepted program literals, *every* declared phase of
`robust@v1` and of `simple@v1` reaches `effect: complete`, so the refusal has
no positive instance on the product surface and would be testable only against
a synthetic profile. It is still genuinely needed for any future program --
and note that neither `new_run()` (`model.py:1115-1116`) nor the preflight
(`operator.py:478-479`) checks reachability today; both admit any *declared*
phase. Shipping the field therefore also means adding a new fail-closed
refusal to the kernel's admission boundary, not only a renderer field.

[[design-effort]]
=== Effort, honestly

AD-6 estimates "one request field, the two negative tests, and the per-program
reachability check". The true scope of a shippable, non-vacuous form is
larger: a terminal-reachability predicate on `WorkflowProfile` plus the new
refusal in `new_run()` and `_preflight()`; the request field itself, which
would be the *first optional field of the Consumer Request* -- the parser
supports an `optional` set, and the Consumer Config uses it, but
`frontdoor/request.py` declares none -- in a grammar whose closure property is
documented: "exactly twelve named fields and no optional ones", and "it does
not supply phases, routes, Finding authorities, result schemas or an entry
phase", in
link:../technical_model/ch04_target_and_alternatives/03_adr_closed_versioned_workflow_programs.adoc#adr-statements[the
ADR's statements section], which a shipped field would also have to amend --
or, if kept required, a field that touches the 77
rendered bundles under `.wfc/governance/`, the request templates in the
shipped skill and the tutorials, and the twelve test modules that build a
Consumer Request, all for a value with one sane answer; the entry in the
preview, the
manifest digest, `wfc status` and `wfc report`; the pinned object of review
with its admission check and trusted projection (<<design-vacuity>>); tests
for each accepted entry and each refusal; and the Front Door docs-sync across
Operator Manual chapters 2, 3, 6 and 10 and the shipped skill sources. That is
disproportionate to one Hop saved roughly four times.

[[design-alternatives]]
=== Alternatives considered and rejected

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Alternative |Why it is rejected

|*A -- AD-6's full shape*: any declared phase that reaches a terminal route
|Admits the `broad_review` entry, which can obtain an acceptance with no
object of review (<<design-vacuity>>). Preserving the *intended* property --
an independent judgment of a candidate someone else produced -- is exactly
what forbids it, and the Beta rule does not licence dropping it.

|*B -- worker-phase entries only* (`implement`, `correct`)
|Safe and useless: `correct` costs one Hop *more* than the default and starts
on an empty Finding ledger. A new public field with no beneficial instance is
cost without benefit.

|*C -- review entry with a Commission-pinned reviewed range*
|The only safe shape, and kept as *the* shape for a later re-evaluation. Not
now: it needs new trusted request and Run state plus its projection and
display, for one Hop. Deferred on proportion, not on principle.

|*D -- derive the entry from the request's shape*
|Already rejected by AD-6 and by link:_open.adoc[the active-work manifest]:
it makes the entry an inference rather than an authorization. Nothing in this
evaluation revives it.

|*E -- close the question with `implement`-only as the permanent contract*
|Rejected for AD-6's reason: the capability is genuinely wanted for
independent re-review of an existing candidate. This is a deferral with named
triggers, not a closure.
|===

[[design-preserved]]
=== Properties preserved by this decision

Trivially, since nothing changes: the closed-program model, the independent
reviewer's sole Finding authority, the trusted-commit boundary, fail-closed
admission, the one-Run-per-checkout lifecycle, and the admitted Commission and
its digest as the Run's governing truth. The decision's substance is that the
*first* of those -- an acceptance that means a second party judged someone
else's candidate -- is what the cheap forms of this capability would spend.

== ADR decision for this record (2026-11-21)

link:../technical_model/ch04_target_and_alternatives/03_adr_closed_versioned_workflow_programs.adoc#adr-ad6[AD-6
of the ADR on closed, versioned workflow programs] records a *validated start

== Decision summary

`src/workflow_coordinator/model.py::new_run()` and the operator's own
preflight (`operator.py::_preflight()`) already admit a WorkItem whose
`initial_phase` names *any* phase declared by the supplied profile -- the
only check is `work_item.initial_phase in profile.phases`
(`model.py:891-892`; re-checked at `operator.py:395-396`). Nothing in the
Coordinator, the reducer, or the admission chain restricts a Run to starting
at a program's nominal first phase. This is current, already-accepted
mechanics, not a proposal.

The Commission Intent renderer does not expose this. `commissioning.py:450`
hardcodes the rendered WorkItem's `initial_phase` to the literal string
`"implement"` on every render, for both `direct@v1` and `robust@v1`. An
Intent author has no field to request a different, already-declared phase
as the Run's entry point.

This is a real capability gap against the Front Door's own stated purpose:
the closed-program model exists precisely so that ordinary Commission
authoring never needs a hand-authored WorkItem/Profile/Policy bundle outside
`commission render` / `commission check`. Entering an existing closed
workflow at an authorized continuation phase -- for example, resuming
independent review of an already-committed, not-yet-reviewed candidate after
a Hop was lost to a mechanical/transport interruption -- currently has no
Intent-level expression and forces exactly the hand-authored,
render-bypassing bundle the Front Door was built to eliminate.

== Motivating case

Commission `wfc-claude-adapter-write-permissions-004` (`robust@v1`,
`RUN-6ebce1ac10df4e099e93848d6c292f8b`): Hop 1 (`implement`) completed and
was trusted-committed (`498c331c25a65198a849a228de495127cee3bb5b`). Hop 2
(`broad_review`) was charged as an uncertain dispatch after the reviewer's
Codex CLI invocation failed at the transport level (a transient
provider-capacity condition, confirmed from the invocation's untrusted
local diagnostics; unrelated to the Commission or the candidate). The Run
correctly stopped `reconcile_required` with `semantic_stop: null`.

Neither currently implemented recovery primitive covers this exact shape:

* same-Run reconcile/resume (`reducer.resume_after_reconcile()`) is a
  tested library function with no operator-CLI wiring at all
  (`docs/backlog/FTR-WFC-ATOMIC-HOP-RECOVERY.adoc` already names this gap);
* `prepare-successor` is implemented and wired, but
  `operator.py::_predecessor_for_successor()` admits only a predecessor
  Run whose `status is RunStatus.AWAITING_HUMAN` and which carries a
  non-null `semantic_stop` (`operator.py:1463-1464`) -- this Run's stop is
  mechanical, not semantic, so it is categorically ineligible.

The only available continuation was an ordinary fresh WorkItem reusing the
existing, already-committed `robust@v1` profile.yaml unchanged (same routes,
scopes, role bindings, evidence gates, Finding authority, and correction
path), hand-authored outside `commission render` purely because the
renderer cannot express `initial_phase: broad_review`. The hand-authored
bundle still passed the same regular, provider-free operator preflight
(`_admit_a1`, `_preflight`) that a rendered bundle would -- the renderer
limitation is additive friction, not a missing safety check.

== What this is not

This is not a defect in `_admit_a1`/`_preflight`'s admission logic, in the
`robust@v1` program's phase graph, or in the reconcile/resume or
successor-preparation mechanics themselves -- all of those already behave
exactly as designed. This record is narrowly about the Intent *renderer*
lacking a field to use a capability the rest of the system already has.

This is also not a request to make `reconcile_required` Runs eligible for
`prepare-successor`, and not a request to implement same-Run resume -- both
remain `FTR-WFC-ATOMIC-HOP-RECOVERY.adoc`'s separate, already-tracked scope.

== Possible shape (recorded for later evaluation only)

*Superseded in two respects by <<design>> (2026-11-27).* The evaluation this
section asked for has been performed and did not ship the shape. Two of its
claims do not survive it: the change is *not* confined to the renderer -- a
terminal-reachability refusal belongs in `new_run()` and `_preflight()`, which
check only declaration today (<<design-closure>>) -- and `durable_artifacts`
needs no per-entry population, because the mapped admission the renderer
produces is entry-independent (<<design-mechanics>>). The shape kept for a
later re-evaluation is alternative *C* of <<design-alternatives>>, not the one
below.

An Intent field such as `initial_phase: <phase-key>` (defaulting to the
program's existing nominal first phase when omitted, preserving current
behavior for every ordinary Commission), validated by the renderer against
the resolved program's own `phases` mapping before rendering, with the
rendered WorkItem's `durable_artifacts` populated accordingly for whichever
phase is selected. No change to `_admit_a1`/`_preflight`, the program
catalogue, or any phase's routes/scope/evidence would be required -- the
renderer would simply stop forcing one hardcoded value onto a field the
rest of the system already treats generically.

This shape is recorded as a starting point for a future design, not as an
accepted design. A separately authorized Commission must still evaluate
alternatives (for example, whether this belongs on the Intent at all versus
a distinct "continuation Intent" concept) before implementing anything.
