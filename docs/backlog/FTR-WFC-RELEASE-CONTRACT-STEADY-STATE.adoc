[[problem]]
== Problem

`docs/developer_guide/ch04_release_management.adoc` is 615 lines. Verified
against the file at `0f50ad2`:

* *Roughly 340 of those lines (`:277-615`) are the record of one release* --
  the Q1 result record, the G7 acceptance, the MD-5 investigation, the
  hosted-run record of the seven attempts, the run-#9 primary evidence, the
  artifact verification, the MD-5 acceptance and a superseded acceptance
  template that says so about itself. Every release task loads the chapter as
  context.
* *The recovery text is wrong for one real state.* `:388-391` says that if a
  step before `publish-pypi` fails, "delete the remote and local tag, fix the
  problem, and re-tag from a corrected commit -- no artifact has reached an
  index yet". After a successful `publish-testpypi` that is false, and the
  same chapter says so 14 lines earlier (`:374-375`: "a pushed tag spends a
  TestPyPI version even if the publish then fails"). TestPyPI refuses a
  second upload of the version and `publish-pypi` needs `publish-testpypi`,
  so the corrected tag cannot pass. The first real failure of this kind
  would start at a dead end.
* *One gate's wording is stale after its own acceptance.* `:268` still reads
  "Operator-run now; CI once MD-5 is settled feasible", while `:14-16` and
  `:574-589` record that the maintainer accepted MD-5 for `0.1.0b1` on
  2026-11-23. The chapter itself notes that the acceptance "does not by
  itself rewrite" the gate wording (`:33-34`).
* *One stated invariant is easy to misread, though the chapter is
  consistent.* `:243` ("Every gate is release-blocking; there is no advisory
  class") is scoped to the enumerated gates G1-G8 (`:267-274`), and
  `python-compat` is not among them: the same chapter records it at
  `:164-168` as "not a prerequisite of any publishing job ... the
  maintainer's small compatibility job of 2026-11-23" and at `:566` as "the
  informational `python-compat` matrix", which matches
  `release.yml:188-195` and `:293-295`. The informational job is therefore a
  *decision*, not a defect, and this slice does not touch it; it only makes
  the scoping of `:243` explicit so the sentence cannot be read as covering
  the compat job. Whether that job should become a gate -- which would
  reverse the 2026-11-23 decision -- is maintainer decision D6 in
  link:beta2_release_streamlining_plan.adoc#d6[the plan]; no slice of the plan
  executes it, because the change would also outdate `:165` and `:566` and
  reach the gate list, so it stays with the maintainer who takes it
  (link:beta2_release_streamlining_plan.adoc#d6-slices[why]).
* *The same release facts are recorded in five places inside this
  repository* -- the chapter's hosted-run and Q1 records, the P3 plan, the
  release-notes qualification paragraph
  (`docs/release_notes/release_notes.adoc:59-72`), the quoted acceptance
  statements, and three cards in `_open.adoc` ("Release qualification
  preparation", "Q1 consumer smoke qualification executed" and "Q1 check
  correction", all dated 2026-11-23). Each copy needed its own commit; the
  gate wording above shows a copy going stale.
* *Four label namespaces for one release* -- C, G, MD, ES. MD and ES are
  decided or done; carrying them in the active chapter makes every later
  reader decode them.

== Chosen direction

Keep the chapter as the decided contract and make it the chapter a release
*operator* reads: contract, job description, one-page runbook, the corrected
recovery table, the gate list. Move the records of `0.1.0b1` to cold
storage, *verbatim*, and link them.

* *Move, never delete.* The maintainer's two acceptance statements are
  recorded evidence; link:README.adoc[backlog upkeep] requires independent
  acceptance to be recorded with its evidence. Cold storage is the
  repository's own home for "completed investigations and reviews" and
  "earlier understanding and history"
  (`docs/ai_info/doc_conventions.adoc`, knowledge homes). Relocation
  preserves the record and removes the per-release reading cost.
* *Byte-for-byte.* The relocated text is not edited, summarised or
  re-dated. Only link targets inside it are repaired, and only where the
  move breaks them.
* *One approval, one record.* The `pypi` deployment approval (G8) is stated
  as *the* release authorization; GitHub records who and when *[X]*. This
  removes nothing from the gate list and adds nothing to it; it names which
  of the five Beta-1 authorizations is the recurring one.
* *The chapter also states what R1 and R3 will implement, as planned.* That
  is how E1 specified E2 and E3 (`_open.adoc`'s E1 card: "planned tooling is
  named as planned, not as existing"). Each such statement has a named owner
  that retires it: E1's planned tooling was E2's to make real, and E2's card
  records "chapter 4 updated". <<handover>> lists every statement this slice
  writes as planned together with the slice that retires it, so the chapter
  cannot be left describing something that exists as something intended.

[[recovery]]
=== The corrected recovery table

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|State |Action

|Failed before any upload |Fix on `main`, delete the tag, tag again.
|TestPyPI upload succeeded, a later step failed |Transient failure: re-run
the failed jobs. Content change: the next version. Never delete and re-tag:
TestPyPI refuses a second upload of the version and `publish-pypi` needs
`publish-testpypi`, so a corrected tag cannot pass.
|PyPI upload succeeded, a later job failed |Re-run the failed jobs. Never
re-tag.
|Wrong content on PyPI |Yank; release the next version.
|===

Written into the chapter together with the table, and *not* as a claim about
the pipeline as integrated:

* *Exception, while
  link:FTR-WFC-RELEASE-PIPELINE-ROBUSTNESS.adoc[R3] is only planned:* in both
  "re-run the failed jobs" states this holds for every job *except*
  `release`. If that job already created the GitHub Release, re-running it
  fails -- `release.yml:371-374` calls `gh release create` with no pre-check
  and no `--clobber`. Until R3 integrates, attach the missing assets by hand.
  R3 changes that behaviour and strikes this exception in the same slice; this
  row is one of the four chapter statements R3 owns, all of them listed in
  <<handover>> and in R3's own scope.
* *Already true of the implementation, and therefore stated plainly:* no
  state needs a rebuild, because every publishing job re-runs against the
  artifact `check` built once (`release.yml:180-186`, `:302-312`,
  `:336-346`).

*Not* adopted as the fix for the TestPyPI-spent state: `skip-existing` on the
TestPyPI upload. It would let the rehearsal silently keep the old file, which
defeats G6's reason for being a real upload (MD-1).

== Acceptance criteria

AC-1:: The recovery section is the table of <<recovery>> *together with its
`release`-job exception*. No sentence in the chapter prescribes
delete-and-retag for a state in which TestPyPI has accepted the version, and
no sentence claims that re-running the failed jobs is safe for the `release`
job while R3 is still planned.
AC-2:: Gate G2's wording states its accepted state and no longer says
"once MD-5 is settled feasible".
AC-3:: The chapter describes `python-compat` exactly as the workflow describes
it *at the moment this slice runs*, and announces no change to it. At
`0f50ad2` that is the recorded 2026-11-23 maintainer decision
(`release.yml:188-195`, `ch04:164-168`, `:566`): an informational job that is
not a prerequisite of any publishing job and not one of the gates G1-G8. The
reduced gate section keeps that scoping explicit, so `ch04:243` cannot be
read as covering the compat job. Whether the job becomes a gate is the
maintainer's open decision D6 in
link:beta2_release_streamlining_plan.adoc#d6[the plan] and no slice of the plan
executes it. Should the maintainer have taken *and* executed Option A or
Option B before this slice runs, the chapter describes the files as they then
stand and the Run record names which case applied; for Option A it reproduces
the maintainer's own statement of how the new prerequisite relates to the gate
list and does not invent one, because the G namespace stays P3's and this
slice may not extend it (<<non-goals>>). The slice never anticipates an
undecided or unexecuted D6.
AC-4:: The MD-5 investigation, the hosted-run record of the seven attempts,
the run-#9 primary evidence, the release-candidate artifact verification, the
Q1 result record, the G7 acceptance, the MD-5 acceptance and the superseded
template all exist under
`docs/cold_storage/backlog/pre-0.1.0b1/release-engineering/`, *byte-identical
to their current text* apart from link targets the move breaks, and each is
linked from the chapter.
AC-5:: A per-item before/after statement in the Run record shows, for each of
C1-C7, MD-1 to MD-6 and G1-G8, that its meaning is unchanged.
AC-6:: Strict render -- `asciidoctor --safe-mode=safe -a assembled -o
/dev/null --failure-level=WARN` -- prints nothing for every changed `.adoc`,
including the assembled developer guide and `docs/backlog/_open.adoc`.
AC-7:: `python3 scripts/wfc_doc_catalog.py generate` then `check` reports no
additional unresolved link and no additional Project Memory miss against the

AC-8:: `python -m pytest -q` green;

AC-9:: Every statement the chapter makes about something that does not yet

Checked by walking the table against the written chapter and against

== Risks

R-1 A quietly changed decision:: rewriting a chapter that states a decided
contract. _Mitigation:_ AC-5's per-item before/after statement, and the
Not-authorized line above.
R-2 Relocation read as discarding evidence:: the two maintainer acceptances
are the most sensitive text in the repository's release history.
_Mitigation:_ byte-for-byte move, a link from the chapter, a group
`README.adoc` that says what the group is and why it moved, and no edit to
any quoted statement.
R-3 Broken inbound links:: `_open.adoc`, `docs/README.adoc`, the release
notes, the P3 plan and several warm reviews link into chapter 4's anchors.
_Mitigation:_ AC-7's catalog comparison, which is exactly the check the BL1
backlog-consolidation slice used for the same kind of move (`_open.adoc`'s BL1
card).
