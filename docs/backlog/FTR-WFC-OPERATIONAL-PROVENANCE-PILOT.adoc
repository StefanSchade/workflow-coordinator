== Problem and practical benefit

Project Memory is curated memory of the project's evolution, not an audit trail; its concerns, events and decisions are
deliberately coarser than commits, reviews and Findings (see link:../../project_memory/README.adoc[its README]). What is
missing is a way to move from a decision or concern to the *operational* record beneath it and back: which Commission and
WorkItem produced a commit, which review judged it, and what later became of what that review found.

Two kinds of reader pay for that today:

* A maintainer or agent who finds an old review in cold or warm storage cannot tell, without reading around it, whether its
  findings were later addressed, overtaken, never substantiated or deliberately left, and so either ignores a still-relevant
  point or revives a long-settled one.
* Any future audit or report work (link:../project_memory.adoc[Project Memory orientation] and `DEC-WFC-023`, which names a
  target audit trail with "traceable association between WFC-generated commits and their executions") needs to know how much of
  that association is already extractable from today's artifacts and how much needs judgment. Nobody has measured it.

Practical benefit: (1) a measured, reproducible answer to "what links commits, Commissions, Runs and reviews today", and
(2) a small, honest register showing what an evidence-qualified disposition of historical review findings looks like, with a
stop/continue gate before any wider effort.

== Maintainer intent and 2026-11-17 decisions

The pilot addresses a concrete interpretive risk: independent readers, including
external developers and LLMs, can encounter a serious historical review Finding
and mistake its then-current assessment for a present defect, unaware that later
work addressed it, replaced the mechanism, consciously left it open, or failed
to establish its status. Historical reviews and their original claims remain
visible and unchanged. The desired result is a verifiable historical disposition,
not a high closure rate or a retrospective rewrite of Run Findings.

P1 is one Commission combining mechanical provenance joins and a substantive,
evidence-based historical sample; neither half is optional. Up to twelve
reviews and approximately forty findings are ceilings, not a target or quota.
Select an informative range of genuinely different histories and prioritize
relevance and source quality, not token savings or numerical coverage.

Each annotation identifies its original review and finding, evidence and an
assessed-at commit. Use actual local links to the original review and verify
K1 `query --refs-to` returns the register for each linked review within K1's
coverage. Expose a comprehensible entry in current documentation navigation
explaining how to find the register and that historical findings require later
disposition context before being treated as present defects. The annotation
is an independently inspectable, non-authoritative interpretation; it does
not change a review's historical meaning, any Run Finding or acceptance.

K1 is now accepted; its catalog is an ignored local artifact
`build/wfc/document_catalog.jsonl`, never the former planned committed
`docs/ai_info/` file. Generate explicitly or use the in-memory live projection;
no automatic cache invalidation. K1's header extraction is deliberately
bounded; historical formats, verdicts and finding labels may require specific
independently testable extraction rules, with ambiguity explicitly unlinked.
Coverage must state population, period and repository/governance generations.
The original spreadsheet-handling tooling, subsequent `git filter-repo` carve-out
and rapid development explain legitimate historical gaps; neither a missing
link nor an older convention establishes a present process defect.

The sibling Evidence repository's `project_memory/canonical/reviews.json` and
`findings.json` may be read as evidence and compared, not altered or made a
bulk-backfill destination. Its README explicitly rejects historical backfill.
The approved pilot destination is the single warm working document specified in
D3; no later canonical promotion or external write is authorized.

== Disposition vocabulary and evidence rules

Each curated entry classifies one historical review finding, identified as (review document path, label as written), using
exactly one of:

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
|Disposition |Minimum evidence and meaning
|`addressed`
|A cited later change *and* a check that the specific issue no longer occurs (a test that exercises it, or a source reading at
a stated commit). Later refactoring alone is not enough; without the check the entry is `unknown`.
|`superseded`
|A cited and verified later design, decision or removal made the specific subject moot (for example a later `DEC-WFC-…` or an FTR that replaced the
mechanism). Names the successor and verifies applicability; refactoring alone is not proof.
|`not_substantiated`
|The claim was checked against the code or documents at the stated commit and not reproduced or not found true. Names the check.
|`not_pursued`
|A cited, recorded reason (a decision, backlog note or review response) for consciously leaving it. The rationale is required text; this does not refute the finding.
|`still_relevant`
|A source check at the stated commit shows the condition still holds. Not inferred from absence of later work.
|`unknown`
|Default. No cited evidence. A permitted final state.
|===

Every entry also records: the evidence references (commit, later review, test, source path and symbol at a commit, Project Memory
or decision identifier), an `evidence_strength` of `demonstrated`, `indicated` or `none`, the repository commit at which it was
assessed, who or what assessed it (agent Run/Hop), `maintainer_accepted: false` until the Maintainer says otherwise, and a short
own-words summary of the finding with a pointer into the review. The words "resolved", "closed" and "fixed" are not used.

Guards that are part of the definition, not options:

* Original review documents are untouched (byte-identical; the diff shows no change to any reviewed file).
* The register is an annotation outside WFC Run state: it is not read by the Coordinator, not cited as authority for a Finding,
  route, review disposition or acceptance, and needs no Coordinator, profile or schema change. It states "authority: none".
* An entry about a historical review finding says nothing about whether a Run Finding existed; it never changes a Finding's
  state in any Run (Finding disposition authority stays with the configured phase of the Run).
* No automatic closure: no script writes or updates a disposition; mechanical output only proposes leads.

== In scope

* *Mechanical join (query-time).* One additional read-only subcommand of `scripts/wfc_doc_catalog.py` (default name
  `provenance`), taking a WorkItem identifier and printing: its Commission directory, the commissioning commit, commits whose
  subject carries the identifier and Hop (three forms), documents whose `WorkItem::` or title/identifiers match, Run identifiers
  and candidate SHAs from their headers, and an explicit "unlinked" list. Output is not committed (it depends on Git history).
  A coverage report over all Commission directories: how many have a commissioning commit, ≥1 token commit, ≥1 review document,
  a Run identifier; what the unlinkable categories are.
* *Curated pilot register.* A bounded sample of at most 12 review documents (about 40 findings at most), stratified across at
  least three storage homes and containing at least one review that ended CHANGES REQUIRED and one that accepted with no
  findings (the latter is expected to yield no entries). Candidates from the sampling, to be confirmed by the Worker:
  `docs/cold_storage/backlog/history/FTR-WFC-SCOPE-ESCALATION-CONTINUATION_implementation_review_001.adoc` … `_004`,
  `BUG-WFC-FINDING-DELTA-COMPATIBILITY-SHIM_review_001.adoc`, `FTR-WFC-CORRECTION-CONVERGENCE-ESCAPE_implementation_review_001.adoc`,
  the two Front Door direct/robust implementation reviews under `docs/cold_storage/backlog/pre-0.1.0b1/front-door/`,
  `FTR-WFC-EVIDENCE-FAILURE-DISPOSITION-HARDENING_implementation_review_001.adoc`, and
  `docs/warm_storage/concepts/wfc_beta1_implementation_readiness_review.adoc` (its `C1`…`C7`).
* *Pilot report* with go/no-go: coverage numbers, the disposition distribution, how many entries reached `demonstrated`, the
  effort in Hops, and a recommendation to stop, continue narrowly or widen. The register and report live in one new warm working
  document, `docs/warm_storage/concepts/wfc_review_finding_dispositions_pilot.adoc` — a bounded investigation is exactly what warm
  storage holds — so no canonical Project Memory table is created or altered.

== Out of scope

* Any change to WFC runtime, Coordinator semantics, Run/Finding state, review schemas, profiles, evidence commands or commit-message
  construction; the commit-body feature; making any disposition an input to admission or review.
* A complete finding audit trail, automatic or bulk disposition, extraction of every finding in the repository, full lifecycle
  extraction, a knowledge graph, embeddings, RAG, a retrieval service or MCP — all possible later developments, none committed.
* Editing, annotating or moving any historical review, the Run Ledger, Project History, Project Memory canonical JSON or the
  sibling Evidence repository; any write outside this repository.
* Retiring or relocating warm or cold documents because of a disposition.

== Risks and controls

[cols="2,4",options="header"]
|===
|Risk |Control
|Misleading historical findings: a stale finding is read as a current defect, or a settled one as still open.
|Disposition requires cited evidence; `unknown` is the default; `still_relevant` needs a source check at a stated commit; each entry
carries its assessed-at commit and strength; the register says it is not a deficiency list.
|False closure: refactoring is taken as resolution, or a disposition is read as acceptance.
|`addressed` needs a specific check; no "resolved/closed" vocabulary; `maintainer_accepted: false` by default; no script writes
dispositions; no Coordinator or Run-state path reads the register.
|Source authority: the register competes with review documents or Run state.
|Original reviews byte-identical; register is an annotation with "authority: none"; Finding disposition authority stays with the Run.
|Metadata drift: commit-subject conventions change and extraction silently degrades.
|Query-time extraction from Git and Commission directories, never a committed duplicate; unlinked commits are listed; tests pin the
three known forms.
|Knowledge loss: pilot output replaces reading the review.
|Entries are pointers with a one-sentence summary; reviews stay in place in their homes; nothing is moved or retired.
|Scope creep toward a complete audit trail or a knowledge graph.
|Hard sample cap, one Commission, go/no-go gate before any widening.
|Judgment quality of an agent-written disposition.
|Independent review re-checks a third of non-`unknown` entries and all `addressed`/`still_relevant` evidence; Maintainer acceptance is a
separate, later act.
|Cross-repository reach: the Evidence repository is outside this repository.
|Read-only inspection at most; no write; if unreachable the pilot proceeds on in-repository sources and says so.
|===

== Mechanical versus human judgment

* Mechanical: Commission-directory and commit-token joins, header extraction, candidate-leads lists (later commits on cited paths,
  later documents linking to a review), coverage counts.
* Judgment: choosing the sample, reading each finding, deciding whether a later change addresses *that* issue, supersession,
  adequacy of evidence, the one-sentence summary and the go/no-go recommendation. The independent reviewer re-checks the judgment;
  Maintainer acceptance remains separate.
