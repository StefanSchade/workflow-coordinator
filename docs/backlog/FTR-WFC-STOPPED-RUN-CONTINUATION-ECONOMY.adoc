== Design (2026-11-27, slice O8, `WI-WFC-BETA2-O8-001`)

Written before the code, under decision
link:beta2_operator_efficiency_plan.adoc#d7[D7a] and the Beta rule, for the
questions link:beta2_operator_efficiency_plan.adoc#o8[the slice] asks. Each
decision names the property it preserves and the alternative it rejects.
Scope of the slice is class *(a)* of the problem below -- a budget-stopped
Run -- and nothing else: class (b)'s `prepare-successor` channel, the
rejected Worker-only Finding closure (D7b) and `wfc resume`'s
durable-artifact recovery class are untouched.

Preserved unchanged by every decision below: used Hops are never reset or
refunded (`WFC-AUTO-05`); the independent reviewer's sole authority to open
and resolve a Finding; the trusted-commit boundary; fail-closed admission;
one Run per checkout; the admitted Commission and its digest as the Run's
governing truth; Hop charging (`model.record_hop`) and both budget-stop
sites in the reducer.

=== Where the effective limit lives

*Decision.* `Run.hop_limit` stays the one enforced effective ceiling, and a
recorded extension raises it. The extension itself is a new, append-only
element of the Run record, `Run.budget_extensions`, each element carrying
*who/what authorised it, when, how much, and against which Run state*:

[source,text]
----
BudgetExtension(sequence, granted_at, additional_hops,
                hop_limit_before, hop_limit_after, hop_used_at_grant,
                run_status_at_grant, stop_reason_at_grant,
                current_head_at_grant, authority, authority_sha256)
----

The admitted `WorkItem.max_autonomous_hops`, the compiled Commission files
and the Commission digest are *never rewritten*: the digest is re-verified
unchanged (the same check `wfc resume`'s recovery makes) before anything is
recorded, so the original admission stays verifiable and an extension is
never mistakable for an original authorisation. The originally admitted
ceiling remains reconstructible from the Run alone as
`hop_limit - sum(additional_hops)`.

*Why this preserves `WFC-AUTO-05`.* Nothing on the path reads or writes
`hop_used` or `hops`; the reduction only ever *increases* `hop_limit`. The
persisted record pins `hop_used_at_grant`, and closed-wire validation
refuses any Run whose `hop_used` is below a recorded grant's charge or whose
extension chain does not add up to its current `hop_limit`. "Used Hops are
never refunded" is therefore a persistence invariant, not a convention.

*Alternatives rejected.* (i) *Recompile the Commission with a higher
`max_autonomous_hops`*: it rewrites admitted governing truth and moves the
Commission digest, so the original admission is no longer verifiable and the
extension becomes invisible as an extension. (ii) *A second counter beside
`hop_limit`* (`hop_limit + extension` evaluated at each reader): every
existing reader of `hop_limit` -- `model.record_hop`, both reducer stop
sites, `reducer.resume_after_reconcile`, `slice4`, `operator.run_serial_hops`
and its hard-maximum guard, the recovery predicate, status, report, audit --
would have to be found and changed, and a missed reader is either a silent
budget bypass or a silent stop. Rejected on risk, not on taste. (iii) *A
journal-only raise* that the lifecycle applies for one continuation without
recording it in the Run: the Run, its checkpoint and its archive would then
disagree with how many Hops the Run may actually charge.

=== The command: a new `wfc extend-budget`, not an option of `wfc resume`

*Decision.* One new subcommand:

[source,shell]
----
wfc extend-budget --repository . \
  --additional-hops 3 \
  --authority "Maintainer instruction of 2026-11-27 in this session: ..." \
  [--preview]
----

Both `--additional-hops` and `--authority` are required: no default amount,
no default statement, no implicit "one more Hop".

*Why not `wfc resume --additional-hops`.* `wfc resume`'s whole contract is
that it asks the human for nothing and either recovers mechanically or
refuses by name. Hanging a human-authority mutation on it would make the
habitual bare `wfc resume` and an authority-bearing `wfc resume --...` the
same verb, under a name that promises no authority, and would put a flag
parser in front of the one command an operator reaches for after every
anomaly. A separate verb makes the authority act its own command with its
own name in the journal, its own exit codes and no way of performing it
while reaching for recovery -- and leaves `wfc resume` literally unchanged,
which this slice requires.

*What it asks of the human, and shows.* Before any mutation (and as the
whole of `--preview`) it prints one confirmation block: Run id, WorkItem id,
phase, Hops used of the current ceiling with the note that they stay
charged, the current ceiling, the requested amount, the resulting ceiling,
the per-call and total bounds with this Run's consumption of them, and the
authority statement with its SHA-256. `--preview` takes no lock, writes
nothing and exits 0.

*What it then does.* Records the extension, re-projects the checkpoint, and
drives the *same* Run on through the unchanged `--continue-existing`
admission path (`operator._continue_existing_run`) at the Run's own
`current_head`, exactly as mechanical recovery does, to a further
`classified` record. One command, one outcome; the Run proceeds in its
recorded phase with its Run-local Findings, pinned governing inputs and
charged-Hop history intact.

=== The bounds, and why

Three, all enforced in the pure reduction before any effect:

* *Per call: 1..4 Hops.* Under `robust@v1` one correction cycle is three
  Hops (Worker correction, focused review, renewed broad review); four
  admits that cycle plus one further Worker Hop, which is the entire shape
  friction entries 15 and 19 describe. More in one step is no longer a
  mechanical residue but new work, and new work belongs in a Commission that
  is authored, previewed and reviewed as one.
* *Total per Run: `ceil(admitted / 2)` Hops*, where `admitted` is the
  originally admitted ceiling reconstructed from the Run. An extension
  corrects the operator's own budget estimate; it does not re-commission. A
  Run may never be made more than half again as large as what admission
  actually judged, and it cannot creep there in small steps.
* *Never above the admitted policy ceiling* (`policy.max_autonomous_hops`,
  re-derived from this checkout's cached, digest-verified Commission --
  never from the live `.wfc/config.yaml`, which the invoking agent can edit)
  *nor above `COORDINATOR_HARD_MAX_AUTONOMOUS_HOPS`* (64).

So an extension cannot silently become unlimited: each bound is a function
of facts already frozen at admission, and the two Run-scoped bounds are
recomputed from the Run's own record on every call.

=== Checkpoint, ownership lock and the one-Run-per-checkout lifecycle

The ownership lock is taken *before any mutation* and held across the
continuation launch (passed to the child by file descriptor), exactly as the
recovery path does, so an extension in a checkout whose lifecycle is active
refuses by name and changes nothing. `checkpoint.json` is re-projected from
the extended Run immediately after the Run snapshot, so the published
checkpoint identity (`run_json_sha256`) matches the snapshot it projects;
its `budget` block gains the effective `limit` and the `extended_by` total
beside the pre-existing `used`/`remaining`. The checkout is not re-used and
no new Run is created: one checkout still carries exactly one Run, which is
why an extension is the cheap answer at all.

If the continuation engine resolves at the pre-mutation check and then fails
to spawn, the Run and checkpoint are rolled back to their pre-extension
state and a `budget_extension_rolled_back` record is appended -- the
append-only journal keeps the granted *and* the rolled-back record, so the
attempt stays auditable, and because the extension is no longer in the Run
it also does not consume the total bound. Stated plainly: a rolled-back
extension is an attempt, not a grant.

=== What is refused, by name

Every refusal below changes nothing at all. The first group is the pure
reduction (`reducer.extend_hop_budget`), the second the lifecycle's
live-fact re-verification.

* the Run is not in `awaiting_human_budget`; or its stop reason is not
  `autonomous_hop_budget_exhausted`; or its used Hops are below its own
  ceiling (it is not actually out of budget);
* the Run carries a frozen `semantic_stop` -- that Run needs the human's
  answer and, where eligible, the `prepare-successor` channel, not more
  budget;
* the amount is not a positive integer, exceeds the per-call bound, exceeds
  this Run's remaining total bound, or would exceed the admitted policy
  ceiling or the Coordinator hard maximum;
* the authority statement is empty, is shorter than 40 characters once
  whitespace is collapsed, or is a bare authorisation token (see below);
* this checkout has no lifecycle, or its classification is not
  `awaiting_human_budget`; another lifecycle holds the ownership lock;
  HEAD differs from the Run's recorded end HEAD; the worktree is not clean
  with resolved submodules; the Commission or execution-envelope identity
  changed since authorization; the provider or engine executable is absent
  or changed; the governing profile cannot be re-derived.

An *open blocking Finding does not block an extension* -- that is the whole
point of the slice (friction entry 19 stopped one Broad Review short with
exactly one). The continuation runs the Run's existing, reviewed
correction/review chain, which is the only thing that may dispose of that
Finding. Nothing on this path reads, writes, opens or closes a Finding, so
D7b is untouched by construction, not by promise.

=== Visibility

* `wfc status` prints a `BUDGET EXTENSION` block per recorded grant (amount,
  before/after ceiling, Hops used at the grant, time, authority statement)
  from the checkout's own append-only journal.
* `wfc report` reports `budget_extensions` and renders one `budget ext` line
  per grant; the `awaiting_human_budget` next-action line now names the real
  command instead of `wfc resume`, which never answered a budget stop.
* `audit.json` carries the extensions in the `run` section *structurally* --
  amount, before/after ceiling, Hops used at the grant, time, and the
  authority statement's SHA-256, deliberately *not* its free text. The audit
  projection is default-deny and passes a privacy guard that refuses any
  absolute path or private string; an operator's free-text sentence cannot
  be allowed to decide whether a Run is archivable at all. The verbatim text
  is in the archived raw `run.json`, and the digest ties the two together.
* The archive carries both: the raw `run.json` (verbatim statement) and
  `audit.json` (structural projection). A continuation attempt's archive
  gets the pre-existing continuation ordinal suffix (`-recovery-N`), which
  this slice deliberately does not rename -- renaming it would change the
  archive destinations of the unrelated mechanical-recovery path.

=== What this cannot enforce, stated honestly

Technical prevention of an agent extending its own budget *is impossible at
this CLI*, and is not claimed anywhere. The Level-1 operator is itself an
agent that already passes `--yes` for launches; whoever can run `wfc` can
pass `--additional-hops` and `--authority` too, and no flag, prompt or exit
code can distinguish a human-instructed extension from an invented one.

What actually controls it:

. *The recorded authority statement*, verbatim in the Run, the journal, the
  checkpoint and the archive, and by digest in `audit.json`. A false
  statement is a durable, attributable false claim in the record the
  maintainer reads -- which is a governance control, not a technical one.
. *The skill rule*: an agent never extends a budget without the maintainer's
  explicit instruction in the current session, quotes that instruction as
  the authority statement, and reports the extension in its own report.
. *Visibility* in status, report, audit and the archive, so no extension is
  quiet.
. *The bounds*, which make the worst case of an unauthorised extension
  finite and small (at most four Hops per call and half the admitted ceiling
  in total) instead of open-ended.

*Adopted as the strongest additional control that needs no new
infrastructure:* refuse a degenerate authority statement. `--authority` is
required; its value must be at least 40 characters once whitespace is
collapsed and must not be one of a closed set of bare authorisation tokens
(`y`, `yes`, `--yes`, `ok`, `okay`, `approved`, `authorised`, `authorized`,
`confirmed`, `granted`, `go`, `maintainer`, `maintainer approved`, ...,
compared case-insensitively with surrounding punctuation stripped). This is
sound against the realistic failure mode -- an agent reflexively reusing the
launch ritual's `--yes` spelling as if it were authority -- and it names its
refusal. It is *not* a defence against a deliberately fabricated sentence,
and is not presented as one.

*Evaluated and rejected.* (i) *An interactive TTY confirmation*: the Level-1
operator runs without a TTY (the lifecycle's own `_make_confirm` already
returns `False` there), so it would make the command unusable in the actual
operating model and would need exactly the `--yes` escape hatch we are not
relying on. (ii) *A maintainer-signed authority token*: it needs key
material and distribution -- a new infrastructure component the slice
excludes -- and in a single-maintainer local setup the key would sit on the
machine the agent drives. (iii) *Requiring the statement to be a committed
repository file*: the agent writes repository files too, so it adds ceremony
without adding a boundary.

=== Deliberate non-goals of this slice

* A budget-stopped Run that is *also* a mechanically recoverable
  durable-artifact stop is not covered: `wfc resume` still refuses by name
  (`no autonomous Hop budget remains for a continuation`) and
  `extend-budget` still requires `awaiting_human_budget`. Combining the two
  belongs to link:beta2_operator_efficiency_plan.adoc#o10[O10], which the
  plan already makes depend on this slice's budget semantics.
* No extension of a `reconcile_required`, `awaiting_human`, `suspended`,
  `stopped`, `completed` or `ready` Run; no successor-path change; no
  autonomous, non-recorded extension path of any kind.


== Problem

Two stop shapes cost a full successor Commission, with its own review chain,
for work that is mechanically trivial.

=== (a) An exhausted Hop budget has no in-place raise and no successor path

A Run whose budget runs out reaches `RunStatus.AWAITING_HUMAN_BUDGET`
(`model.py:132`, set at `reducer.py:1968-1981` and `:2046-2056`) with
`semantic_stop` untouched. The productized successor preparation requires
`AWAITING_HUMAN` *and* a non-null frozen semantic stop
(`operator.py:1688`), so a budget stop fails both halves and is categorically
ineligible -- as the manual states
(`operator_manual/ch06_recovery/02_successors.adoc:118-119`). Mechanical
recovery refuses it too, by name, when no budget remains
(`frontdoor/lifecycle.py:887-888`), and the shipped texts correctly record
that there is no in-place raise
(`agent_skill/wfc/references/commission.md:71-73`).

Observed cost (friction entry 19): under `robust@v1`, one Worker correction
plus a Focused and a Broad Review are three Hops, so a 10-Hop ceiling buys
one implementation Hop, one review round and about two correction cycles. A
retire-heavy rework consumed exactly that and stopped one Broad Review before
acceptance, with one open blocking Finding whose whole content was a single
unused import. A successor Commission with its own review chain for one name
is out of proportion: three agent Hops and roughly an hour, at about six
minutes per task-check, for a mechanical residue.

=== (b) An operational stop can be answered only through a heavy channel

The channel that does exist is `prepare-successor --authority-decision`
(`operator.py:324-342`), and it is available for an operational stop: an
escalation of any kind freezes the `SemanticStop` its gate requires
(`reducer.py:1753-1759`). But it needs five newly pinned governing input
paths, a clean successor checkout at an exact baseline and a published
checkpoint identity anchor (`operator.py:1683-1704`) -- heavy for handing a
Worker one failing node id. `wfc resume` is not an alternative and correctly
refused: it covers only the mechanical durable-artifact anomaly class
(`reducer.py:487-500`, `frontdoor/lifecycle.py:871-875`).

The *cheap* half of (b) is not a product gap at all and is not this record's
subject: the channel is simply absent from the shipped operator texts, which
link:FTR-WFC-LEVEL1-OPERATOR-GUIDANCE.adoc[the operator-guidance record]
addresses as text.

== Why this is category B, and what could erode

Three distinct authority boundaries, any one of which makes this more than
ergonomics:

. *A budget raise changes an admitted WorkItem's governing truth.* It
  collides directly with the recorded rule that resume and recovery require
  explicit maintainer authority and that used Hops are never reset
  (link:FTR-WFC-ATOMIC-HOP-RECOVERY.adoc[the atomic-Hop recovery record]'s
  `WFC-AUTO-05`, which that record states remains normative).
. *Worker-only closure of a "purely mechanical" Finding moves Finding
  disposition away from the independent reviewer* -- the safeguard the
  repository's review model rests on.
. *A lighter answer channel re-opens what governing truth a continuation
  admits*, which is the question `prepare-successor`'s pinning requirements
  exist to answer.

The specific erosion to weigh, stated plainly: "a deterministic check failing
with no behaviour change" is not mechanically distinguishable from "a
blocking Finding the Worker would rather close itself". Any mechanism of
class 2 would have to derive that class from the Coordinator's own
observation -- the trusted gate's own result, before and after -- and never
from the agent's claim about its own change. No such derivation is designed
here, and the analysis does not assert that one is available.

== The interim remedy, which prevents the case instead of cheapening its repair

Two measures, both carried by
link:FTR-WFC-LEVEL1-OPERATOR-GUIDANCE.adoc[the operator-guidance record] and
neither needing any mechanism change:

* An evidence command that runs the repository's own gates would have
  surfaced the observed residue at the *first* Hop's task-check, for one
  Worker correction and no reviewer -- see
  link:FTR-WFC-EVIDENCE-COMMAND-SEQUENCE.adoc[the evidence-command record]
  for why the rule, not a contract change, is the Beta-2 remedy.
* Budgeting a retire-heavy rework (removing a mechanism and its tests, plus
  documentation synchronisation) above the usual double of the expected Hop
  count, against the observed convergence figures.

The disposition actually used for the recorded incident is recorded in
friction entry 19 as a deliberate, maintainer-approved exception and is not a
precedent this record generalises.

== What shipped (2026-11-27, `WI-WFC-BETA2-O8-001`)

Class (a) of the problem above, exactly as <<design>> decided it, and
nothing else.

*The command.* `wfc extend-budget --repository . --additional-hops N
--authority "<the maintainer's instruction>" [--preview]`
(`frontdoor/lifecycle.py::extend_budget_lifecycle`, dispatched by
`frontdoor/cli.py`). Both value arguments are required; there is deliberately
no `--yes`. It prints the confirmation block before any mutation (and as the
whole of `--preview`), records the grant, re-projects the checkpoint, and
drives the *same* Run through the unchanged `--continue-existing` admission
path to a further `classified` record, with preservation and the postrun
archive step following exactly as for any other continuation. New
classification `refused_budget_extension` (exit `34`); `--preview` exits `0`
and is deliberately not an `EXIT_CODES` classification, since it is not a Run
outcome.

*The record.* `model.BudgetExtension` and `Run.budget_extensions`
(append-only; the key is absent from an unextended Run's document, so every
Run written before this slice replays byte-identically).
`reducer.extend_hop_budget` is the whole decision: pure, total, and the only
place that raises `hop_limit`. `serialization` validates the chain on the
wire -- contiguous grant sequences, an arithmetic chain ending at the Run's
own `hop_limit`, a digest that matches its own statement, a grant only ever
against an `awaiting_human_budget`/`autonomous_hop_budget_exhausted` state,
and `hop_used` never below a recorded grant's charge. That last one is how
`WFC-AUTO-05` became a persistence invariant rather than a convention.

*The bounds*, enforced in the reduction: `BUDGET_EXTENSION_MAX_PER_CALL = 4`;
in total `ceil(admitted / 2)` with `admitted` reconstructed from the Run; and
never above the admitted policy ceiling (re-derived from the cached,
digest-verified Commission) nor `COORDINATOR_HARD_MAX_AUTONOMOUS_HOPS`.
`BUDGET_EXTENSION_MIN_AUTHORITY_CHARACTERS = 40` plus a closed set of bare
authorisation tokens refuse a gesture in place of a statement.

*Visibility.* `wfc status` prints a `BUDGET EXTENSION <n>:` block per grant
from the checkout journal; `frontdoor/report.py` carries `budget_extensions`
and renders one `budget ext` line per grant, and its
`awaiting_human_budget` next-action line now names this command instead of
`wfc resume`, which never answered a budget stop; `frontdoor/audit.py`
projects the grants structurally with the statement's SHA-256 and not its
free text; the archived raw `run.json` carries the verbatim statement.
`checkpoint_projection`'s `budget` block gained `limit` and `extended_by`.

*One adjacent correction.* `reducer.validate_convergence_route_history`'s
budget-preemption exemption for a v3 strategy-gate Hop was written when a
budget stop was necessarily terminal. It now also accepts the recorded
extension as evidence of the same historical fact (that Hop's route *was*
preempted by an exhausted budget); without it an extended v3 convergence Run
could never be projected again. The consumer programs compile to profile
schema 2, so this shape is not reachable through the Front Door today; the
exemption is corrected rather than left as a latent trap.

*Unchanged, and tested as unchanged.* `wfc resume` (a budget stop still
falls through to its no-op; a mechanically recoverable stop with no Hop left
still refuses by name), `prepare-successor` eligibility, Hop charging, and
the independent reviewer's sole Finding authority -- an open blocking Finding
deliberately does not block an extension, and nothing on the path touches a
Finding (D7b).

*Documentation.* Operator Manual chapters 2, 3, 5, 6 and 10 (the
budget-extension contract itself is
link:../operator_manual/ch06_recovery/01_outcomes.adoc#budget-extension[chapter
6's]), the technical model's runtime and persistence views, and the shipped
Agent Skill sources (`SKILL.md`, `references/commission.md`,
`references/stops-and-evidence.md`) -- in each case *replacing* the statement
that there is no in-place raise, and adding the rule that an agent never
extends a budget on its own. The shipped texts reach an installed consumer
only on a separate Runtime promotion and `wfc skill install`, neither
authorized nor performed here. Independent acceptance and product closure are
the maintainer's to record.

== Open decisions

* Classes (b) and (c) of the problem above are untouched and remain open as
  described: a lighter answer channel for an operational stop, and what
  governing truth a continuation admits. Class (b)'s cheap half stays a text
  matter for link:FTR-WFC-LEVEL1-OPERATOR-GUIDANCE.adoc[the
  operator-guidance record].
* A Run that is *both* budget-exhausted and mechanically recoverable is not
  covered by either mechanism; link:beta2_operator_efficiency_plan.adoc#o10[slice
  O10] owns it, as the plan already states.
* Whether the per-call and total bounds are the right numbers is an empirical
  question this slice cannot answer: they are stated, documented and
  mechanically enforced, and should be revisited against observed use rather
  than argued in advance.
