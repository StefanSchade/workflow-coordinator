=== 2. Blocking Findings

==== F1 — The verification line states a count that the staged manifest
contradicts

*Invariant.* Every path, count and citation written into the plan is verified
against this repository or the staged input (task, PROPORTION AND FORM and
Acceptance).

*Reproduced.* Plan lines 15–20 read "the staged read-only input under
`build/wfc/postbeta-inputs/`, whose 22 `MANIFEST.sha256` digests all verified,
including the two items added for this update (`MD5_FACTS.txt`,
`MD5_flake_repro.py`)". `wc -l MANIFEST.sha256` is 24 and
`sha256sum -c MANIFEST.sha256` prints 24 `OK` lines and nothing else. The
figure 22 is the previous plan's correct figure (`git show
39809b6:…plan_p3_release_engineering.adoc` line 15, and the earlier Run's
H005 record, "all 22 staged digests verified `OK`"): 22 items were staged
before this update, and the two new items make 24. The sentence is therefore
internally contradictory as well as wrong — it claims 22 digests *including*
two additions to a set of 22.

*Why it blocks.* This is the plan's own statement of what was verified, which
the task explicitly required to be updated and which every later reader uses to
judge the plan's evidence base. A wrong count there is not a stylistic matter.

*Feasible correction.* `24`. The digests do all verify; only the number is
stale.

==== F2 — "Exactly four conditional skips exist suite-wide … nothing else is
ever skipped" is false

*Invariant.* MD-5's answer [4] names which tests or external prerequisites
cannot run on a hosted runner, and nothing may be skipped silently (task, MD-5
question 4; "no hidden skips").

*Reproduced.* Plan lines 574–576 (MD-5 answer [1]) state "Exactly four
conditional skips exist suite-wide (three opt-in live-provider, one internal
probe); nothing else is ever skipped", and answer [4] (lines 592–593) repeats
"the four skips are opt-in or internal, not host-related". A grep over `tests/`
for `pytest.skip`, `skipif` and `@pytest.mark.skip` returns seven skip sites,
not four:

* `tests/integration/test_claude_adapter_live_smoke.py:37`, `:100`, `:134` —
  the three opt-in live-provider skips the plan names;
* `tests/unit/test_pytest_temp_storage.py:39` — the internal subprocess probe
  the plan names;
* `tests/unit/test_memory_workbook.py:54` —
  `needs_make = pytest.mark.skipif(shutil.which("make") is None, …)`;
* `tests/unit/test_memory_workbook.py:55` —
  `needs_git = pytest.mark.skipif(shutil.which("git") is None, …)`;
* `tests/architecture/test_historical_governance_parse.py:13` —
  `pytest.skip("historical self-development governance directory is absent")`,
  conditional on `governance/self-development` existing in the checkout
  (`git ls-files governance` is 860 files today, so it does not fire on a full
  checkout).

`tests/conftest.py`, `scripts/wfc_test_inputs.py`, `pyproject.toml` and
`Makefile` add no further skip mechanism, so seven is the complete set.

*Why it blocks.* The three unnamed skips are precisely the class MD-5 exists to
rule out: two are conditional on a host facility (GNU make, Git) and one on
checkout content. They do not fire on the Operator's reference host, which is
why the staged `MD5_FACTS.txt` makes the narrower and accurate claim — "exactly
four tests skip *on the reference host*" (§1, last bullet). The plan widened
that measured statement into a suite-wide guarantee that the code contradicts.
On a hosted runner, in a container image without `make`, or in a shallow or
sparse checkout, a required test would skip green and the plan says that cannot
happen. A gate justified by a false no-skip guarantee is weaker than the plan
claims, and the plan's own rule — "nothing may be silently widened" — is the
one being violated.

*Feasible correction.* Keep the facts file's scoping and name the residue: four
skips fire on the reference host (three opt-in live-provider, one internal
probe); three further conditional skip sites exist
(`test_memory_workbook.py:54`, `:55`, `test_historical_governance_parse.py:13`)
and must be asserted *not* to fire on the runner — `make` and `git` present,
`governance/self-development` checked out. That is one sentence and it turns a
false guarantee into a checkable one, which also strengthens E3's `check` job.

==== F3 — The plan is 613 lines against "about 540 lines at most", and the
growth was not paid for

*Invariant.* "The plan may grow by what the decisions add, but stay an
operating plan of about 540 lines at most; compress existing text to pay for
it" (task, PROPORTION AND FORM; Acceptance: "stays about 540 lines at most").

*Reproduced.* `wc -l` is 613: 13.5 % over the stated maximum, and +31 % against
the 467 lines of the accepted baseline. The Worker's own summary records the
overrun, so this is an acknowledged non-conformance rather than a reviewer's
reading of "about". For calibration, the earlier P3 Run closed at 467 against
"about 450" (3.8 % over) and that pass left it non-blocking; 13.5 % is a
different magnitude, and this task added the explicit instruction to pay for
growth by compression.

*Why it blocks, and what the cost is.* The overrun is not caused by the
mandated additions alone. Every decision is currently stated three times — once
as a section 2 table row, once as a section 3 C-item, once as a section 8
MD-item — in prose that restates rather than cites:

* MD-1 ↔ C7 ↔ the "Package publishing and credentials" row (lines 90–98,
  201–205, 542–548);
* MD-2 ↔ C1 ↔ the "Build backend and version derivation" row (47–53, 167–170,
  549–552);
* MD-4 ↔ C5 ↔ the "Documentation build and version-pinned publishing" row
  (140–149, 189–194, 559–562);
* MD-5 ↔ C6 (195–200, 563–566);
* MD-6 ↔ Q1 "Subject" ↔ the dropped-Q0 section (386–397, 425–433, 607–613),
  where the smoke-test fallback and its escalation clause are written out three
  times in near-identical wording.

That is a real maintenance cost, not a style preference: a later decision change
must be found and kept consistent in three places, and the plan already forces a
reader to reconcile three statements of MD-1 and three of MD-4 to be sure they
agree. Stating each decision once in section 8 and letting section 2 and
section 3 carry the disposition plus an anchor reference removes the cost and
pays most of the overrun. The same holds for the risk list (496–517) and the
rejected-package list (527–537), both of which restate material already carried

*Feasible correction, and its bound.* The structure the task requires —
sections 1–8 and the anchors `c1..c7`, `e0..e3`, `q1`, `g1..g8`, `md1..md6` —
is preserved by this change; no package, gate, disposition, decision or MD-5
answer is dropped to reach the budget, and none may be. I estimate 45–60 lines
recoverable from the duplication named above and a further 10–15 from the
execution-package prose without losing content. A correction that removes the
triple statement and lands at about 540 resolves this Finding; a correction that
reaches the number by deleting mandated substance does not.

*For the maintainer.* Whether "about 540 lines at most" is a soft target or a
hard ceiling is still unsettled — the earlier Run's H002 and H004 passes raised
the same question against "about 450" and it was not answered. I block here on
the unpaid growth and the removable duplication, which hold under either
reading; if the maintainer intends 540 as soft, the duplication finding still
stands on its own and the number does not.

=== 5. Non-blocking observations

NB-1 (misattributed `fetch-depth`):: E2's tag-mode clause says the reachability
check "needs the runner to fetch `main` with full history, already needed for
<<c1>>'s `fetch-depth: 0`". C1 is the *static*-version decision, and section
2's first row says the opposite in so many words: deriving the version from the
tag "is why the reference needs `fetch-depth: 0`". WFC's static version needs no
such depth; the reachability check does. The operational conclusion is right,
the rationale is attached to the wrong decision, and a reader could conclude the
depth comes for free. One clause fixes it, and E3's `check` job would be clearer
for naming the checkout depth and the `main` fetch itself.
NB-2 (an unverified reproducibility claim inside the Q1 argument):: Step 2 of
the release sequence offers Q1 "that commit's build artifact (downloaded, or
rebuilt identically — the build is static-version and reproducible)". A static
version makes the *metadata* stable; it does not make `python -m build` produce
byte-identical archives, which generally embed file timestamps. Nothing in the
repository or the staged input establishes a reproducible-build fact, so
"rebuilt identically" is an unverified claim stated as a verified one, in the
one place where artifact identity is the whole argument. The plan does flag the
substantive question as the single maintainer confirmation, which is why this is
not blocking; the cheap fix is to prefer the downloaded `check` artifact and
mark the rebuild equivalence unverified.
NB-3 (a stale count outside this WorkItem's scope):: `docs/backlog/_open.adoc:393`
still records "link:postbeta_plan_p3_release_engineering.adoc[The plan] (467
lines)". The plan is now 613. The acceptance criterion forbids this WorkItem to
touch any other file, so this is reported rather than corrected — one upkeep
commit after the Run clears it, as the earlier P3 Run's records did for the same
line. It will need the final figure, so it is best done after F3. The same
upkeep commit should catalogue this review record: adding it leaves a known
`wfc_doc_catalog.py` drift, exactly as the earlier P3 Run's two review records
did, and cataloguing it from here would touch a file this WorkItem may not
change.
NB-4 (an inference stated as fact):: Section 2's first row says the reference's
"sdist finder sweeps the tracked tree" without qualification, while the artifact
row correctly labels the same over-inclusion an "*inference*" and
`ARTIFACT_FACTS.txt` marks it "Inference, NOT measured". The qualification
belongs in both places.
NB-5 ("branch run" where only `main` triggers):: MD-5's cheapest-evidence clause
and R-1 speak of "the first hosted branch run", but E3's triggers are `push` to
`main` and tags `v*`, so a push to any other branch starts nothing. ES-3 is
precise ("Push the intended release commit"), so the referent is unambiguous
and the task's own wording is the source; still, "the first hosted run on
`main`" would say what can actually happen.
NB-6 (the container route in answer [2]):: The maintainer's question (2) names a
pinned container explicitly. Answer [2] develops route R-A only and leaves R-B
to a parenthetical label in the escalation rule, so a reader of [2] learns
nothing about why the container is the second choice. `MD5_FACTS.txt` §5 gives
two concrete reasons in one line each — apt is not byte-pinned without a
snapshot mirror, and bwrap in a container needs relaxed seccomp/userns — and one
clause carrying them would answer the question as asked. Compressing elsewhere
(F3) pays for it.

=== 6. Simplicity and maintainability

The plan adds no mechanism that fails to earn its place. Four packages plus one
qualification package, one workflow file, two new standard-library scripts with
one implementation of the artifact rule and two callers, two GitHub
Environments, zero repository secrets, eight blocking gates and no advisory
class, one consolidated external package replacing eight separate human
actions, and no new register, entity or status report. E0 is a genuinely new
package but a prerequisite of a deterministic gate, correctly bounded to one
test file with a conditional one-function source scope. The one structural
excess is the triple statement of every decision, which is F3's subject: it is
pure duplication, not mechanism, and removing it makes the plan both shorter and
safer to change. I found no safeguard added merely to exclude a hypothetical
failure state, and no simpler design that would reach the same authorized
objectives.
