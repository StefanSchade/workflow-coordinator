=== 4. The blocking Finding -- the record's commit attribution is an overclaim

The record states, in the section headed "Verified cause of the local/hosted
discrepancy":

____
"`git log -S` on each file's offending text shows `journal.py`'s `Iterator`
import, `fake_engine.py`'s `json` import, and all 13 `F841` sites trace to
commit `1a2fede` ... -- 15 of the 17 findings."
____

Re-running that command contradicts the sentence it is cited for:

* `git log --oneline -S "prelaunch = L._do_prelaunch"` over the two test files
  lists *three* BP4 commits, not one: `1a2fede` (H001), `ade01b8` (H003) and
  `41bab45` (H006).
* Counting the sites per revision in `test_lifecycle_crash_matrix.py`:
  5 at `1a2fede`, 7 at `ade01b8`, 11 at `41bab45` and 11 at the base `b7e62be`.
  So 2 of the 11 came from H003 and 4 from H006.
* In `test_lifecycle_controller.py` the two F841 sites (then `:385-386`) are
  present at `1a2fede`.

The true figures are therefore *9 of the 17 findings trace to `1a2fede`*
(`Iterator`, `json`, 2 controller sites, 5 crash-matrix sites) and *15 of 17 to
the three BP4 Hops together*; "all 13 F841 sites" and "15 of the 17 findings"
at a single commit are both wrong. The substantive causal conclusion is
unaffected -- all three commits belong to the BP4 lifecycle-controller WorkItem,
and the findings genuinely pre-existed the hosted run -- but the acceptance
criteria require the record to state the *verified* cause "without
overclaiming", and the task required the Worker to "not claim more than you
verified". A verification-attributed statement that the cited command disproves
is the defect, in a durable provenance document whose later readers have no way
to notice it.

Remedy, one sentence in one file already inside the Worker's scope: name the
three BP4 commits (`1a2fede` H001, `ade01b8` H003, `41bab45` H006) and give the
9-of-17 / 15-of-17 split, keeping the existing, correct `c7b84e2` attribution
for the fixture pair. No code change, no new mechanism and no change to any
other claim is needed or wanted.

=== 5. Non-blocking observations

None of these blocks acceptance and none asks for new mechanism.

. *Two per-line suppressions against the acceptance wording "at most one".* The
  acceptance text allows "at most one narrowly scoped commented suppression on
  that import". Ruff reports the F401 at `:16` and the F811 at `:22` -- the
  task's own finding list says so -- and `# noqa` is strictly per-line, so no
  single directive on the import line can clear both. The implemented pair is
  the minimal correct form, and the alternatives I can see each break another
  binding constraint: deleting the import or renaming the parameter breaks
  fixture resolution; a per-file or configuration ignore is explicitly withheld.
  I therefore do not treat the second directive as a violation, and I flag the
  wording for the maintainer rather than waive it: if exactly one directive is
  wanted, that is a decision about the criterion, not about this candidate.
  The record's own "Correction against the task text" paragraph documents the
  deviation, which is the right handling.
. *`ruff` is unpinned in CI* (`ruff>=0.6`), so a future ruff release can
  introduce findings on an unchanged tree. Pre-existing and out of this
  WorkItem's withheld authority; worth the maintainer's attention separately.
. *Pre-existing `# noqa: E402` comments* in `tests/fixtures/bp4/fake_engine.py`
  and `tests/fixtures/bp9/wfc_entry.py` are inert under `select = ["F"]` and
  were correctly left untouched. `RUF100` is not enabled, so they report

  section earns its place and I propose no cuts.
