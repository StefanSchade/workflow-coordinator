==== 3.4 Classifications (AC-6, second clause) and the blocking defect

All 20 classifications named anywhere in the four files are keys of
`frontdoor/lifecycle.py`'s `EXIT_CODES`; none is invented. Two things in the
same four-line paragraph of `references/stops-and-evidence.md` are wrong, and
together they are the blocking Finding F-1 of section 5.

*(a) The lifecycle-consumption generalization.* The file states:

----
* `refused_unauthorized` / `refused_drift` / `refused_prelaunch` /
  `refused_probe` / `refused_identity_changed` / `refused_launch_exhausted` /
  `refused_active_lifecycle` / `refused_journal_root` /
  `refused_no_lifecycle` -- a mechanical refusal before or around launch;
  each still consumes this checkout's one lifecycle.
----

A checkout's one lifecycle exists exactly when its journal holds an
`authorized` record: `_advance` refuses a second `wfc run` with
`refused_active_lifecycle` if and only if `journal.latest(("authorized",))` is
not `None` (`frontdoor/lifecycle.py:742-752`). `_authorize` raises
`refused_unauthorized` when confirmation is withheld, and `refused_drift` on
the re-derivation, *before* it appends `authorize_intent`/`authorized`
(`:271-302`). I reproduced the consequence on a disposable consumer repository
built under `$TMPDIR` (the pattern of `tests/unit/test_lifecycle_controller.py`:
`git init`, a committed `GUIDANCE.md` and `checks/check.sh`, `wfc init
--scope-ceiling src`, a hand-written `simple@v1` request, `XDG_STATE_HOME`
inside the temporary tree), driving `lifecycle.run_lifecycle` three times in
the *same* checkout with an unresolvable provider executable:

----
1) wfc run, confirmation refused (the non-interactive case without --yes):
   classification: refused_unauthorized
   journal: (no journal file at all)
2) wfc run AGAIN in the same checkout, confirmation granted,
   provider executable unresolvable (a refusal after authorization):
   classification: refused_prelaunch
   journal: ['authorize_intent', 'authorized', 'prelaunch_intent']
3) wfc run a third time in the same checkout:
   classification: refused_active_lifecycle
   journal: ['authorize_intent', 'authorized', 'prelaunch_intent']
----

Step 2 is the refutation: after `refused_unauthorized` the checkout is
untouched and the next `wfc run` proceeds past authorization. Only once
`authorized` is recorded does a further `wfc run` get
`refused_active_lifecycle` (step 3). Two tests already in the required suite
assert the same facts from the other side:
`test_no_tty_and_no_yes_refuses_with_no_side_effect` (the state home does not
even exist afterwards) and
`test_digest_drift_between_plan_and_authorization_refuses_before_any_commit`
(`Journal(...).read_all() == ()`).

The sentence is wrong for at least four of the nine it covers:
`refused_unauthorized` and `refused_drift` when raised pre-authorization (both
can also occur later — `refused_drift` again in `_do_commit` at `:511-515`),
`refused_no_lifecycle`, which is `wfc resume` finding *nothing* recorded
(`:745`), and `refused_active_lifecycle`, which reports a lifecycle consumed
earlier rather than consuming one. `refused_prelaunch` is itself on both sides
of the line (`:291` before authorization, `:326-390` after). What is true, and
is what the sources say, is the probe case: the quickstart states it of
`refused_probe` only, and `references/commission.md` repeats it correctly
there.

Why this blocks rather than being an observation: `refused_unauthorized` is
precisely the refusal the FTR's authorization section puts in front of the
agent ("without it and without a terminal the launch is refused"), so it is the
refusal a Level-1 agent is most likely to meet. An agent that believes its
fresh worktree is spent will prepare another one or tell the human the checkout
is burned — and discarding a worktree is on the skill's own always-human list.
It is also an invention: no sentence in the six pages S1 may distil says this
of any refusal but the probe, and the content contract is explicit that where
the skill and the CLI disagree, the skill is wrong.

*(b) The false completeness claim.* The section is headed `## Every
classification, and what it means`, and the FTR gives this file "every
classification and what it means" as its content. Three of the 23 `EXIT_CODES`
keys are unlisted: `launch_failed_no_engine`, `not_launched` and
`lifecycle_active`. `lifecycle_active` is not obscure — chapter 3's STATUS
section documents it by name ("`authorized`/`lifecycle_active`") and
`frontdoor/report.py` carries operator text for all three, so an agent polling
`wfc status` around a launch can see them and will find its "every
classification" list silent. The file is 95 of its 200 permitted lines, so the
remedy costs nothing.

=== 7. Non-blocking observations

O1:: AU-4's third sentence — "an instruction to carry out a task through WFC is
an instruction to launch it once its authority facts are decided, unless the
human reserved the go" — has no counterpart in the skill's rule 4. Its
operative effect survives in rule 2 ("ask once, undecided facts only"), rule 3
("without a further approval turn") and rule 7 (the reserved go), which is why
I do not read AC-7 as violated; restoring the sentence would nonetheless make
the no-extra-turn rule explicit where the FTR puts it. Equally, rule 3 drops
the FTR's enumeration of what "lies within" means (scope equal to or inside
the approved paths, the approved program, a Hop budget not above the approved
one, bindings as approved or as committed) and rule 7 drops "for an
interactive terminal", which is the detail that explains why the handed-over
command needs a terminal at all. All three are compressions of present
statements, not omissions of a rule.

them from `SKILL.md`, which has no fenced block. AC-6 is about the whole text,
