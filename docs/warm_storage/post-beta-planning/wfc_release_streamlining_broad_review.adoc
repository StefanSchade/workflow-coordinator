=== 3. Blocking Finding 2 — the W7 disposition states a contradiction that the files do not contain, and settles a reversal of a recorded maintainer decision

The candidate dispositions W7 as *confirmed / adopt (gate it)* and writes that
adoption into the slice title, the acceptance criteria and the `_open.adoc`
card:

* planning record, W7 row: "`ch04:243`: 'Every gate is release-blocking;
  there is no advisory class.' … so a red `python-compat` publishes anyway …
  One `needs:` line removes *a stated contradiction*".
* plan `:329-330`: "`python-compat` gates nothing while the chapter says every
  gate is release-blocking"; plan `:357`: acceptance "— `publish-testpypi`
  lists `python-compat` in `needs`".
* `FTR-WFC-RELEASE-PIPELINE-ROBUSTNESS.adoc:59-63`, `:154-161`, and its title
  "… And A Real Compatibility Gate"; `_open.adoc` card: "a real compatibility
  gate".
* `FTR-WFC-RELEASE-CONTRACT-STEADY-STATE.adoc:52-58` ("One stated invariant is
  contradicted by the implementation") and AC-3 at `:193-195`.

The repository refutes the premise:

. `ch04:240-275` is the *gate* section. The gates are exactly G1-G8 and
  `python-compat` is not among them (`:267-274`). The sentence at `:243`
  — "Every gate is release-blocking; there is no advisory class. *Each step
  below names the gate(s) guarding it.*" — is scoped to that enumerated list
  and is true of it.
. The chapter already records `python-compat` for what it is, in the same
  chapter: `:164-168` — "`python-compat` (both triggers, `needs: check`, no
  elevated permissions, *not a prerequisite of any publishing job*, matrix
  CPython 3.10, 3.11 and 3.12): *the maintainer's small compatibility job of
  2026-11-23*. `requires-python = ">=3.10"` and the classifiers are
  unchanged" — and `:566` calls it "the informational `python-compat`
  matrix".
. The workflow says the same, attributing it to the same decision:
  `release.yml:188-195` — "Separate, smaller Python-compatibility job
  (*maintainer decision of 2026-11-23*) … It is NOT qualification: no offline
  bundle, no bubblewrap, no provider, no full suite, *no release gate (no job
  waits for it; it reports the compatibility facts for the maintainer)*", and
  `:290` lists "any release gate" under what the job does not cover.
  `_open.adoc:161` likewise records "the informational `python-compat`
  matrix".
. The basis itself does not claim a documentation contradiction. Its W7
  (`wfc_release_process_comparison_fable_2026-11-23.adoc:800-805`) states the
  limitation as "Classifiers advertise 3.10-3.12; a red compat job would not
  stop publication" and offers two changes: "Add `python-compat` to the
  `needs` of `publish-testpypi`, *or remove the classifiers*."

Two consequences, both blocking:

*(a) An unverified statement about current behaviour.* The CONSTRAINTS require
that "every FTR statement about current behavior must be verified against the
files", and the REVIEW FOCUS asks me to verify the claims myself. The claim
"the chapter says every gate is release-blocking while `python-compat` gates
nothing" is not what the chapter says; the chapter's own text at `:164-168`
and `:566` is consistent with the workflow. The candidate's `[D]`/`[I]`
evidence for this row cites `:243` and `release.yml:293-295` but not
`:164-168`, which is the sentence that governs the job.

*(b) A recorded maintainer decision reversed as settled design.* That
`python-compat` gates nothing is not an oversight; it is recorded as the
content of the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-23 in three places
(`ch04:164-168`, `release.yml:188-195`, `_open.adoc:161`). Making it a
prerequisite of `publish-testpypi` changes that decision. The task is
explicit: "Do not decide anything the P3 decisions or the maintainer already
decided; where the Fable concept reverses a recorded decision (W3 …), present
it as an open MAINTAINER DECISION with both options, costs and your
recommendation, not as a settled design." The candidate does this exemplarily
for W3 (plan `<<d1>>`, `FTR-…-CONSUMER-SMOKE-PLACEMENT.adoc:77-81`, the whole
slice held conditional) and not at all for W7, although the basis offers the
same two-option shape and the candidate itself notes the alternative only as
"stays available".

Correction (either is acceptable; the second is the smaller change):

* State W7 accurately — the chapter does not contradict itself; the real
  question is whether the 3.10-3.12 classifiers should be backed by something
  that can stop a publication — and present it as an open maintainer decision
  (gate the job / drop the classifiers / leave both as decided) with a
  recommendation, moving the `needs:` edit out of R3's acceptance into the
  conditional set; or
* keep it in R3 but mark that element explicitly conditional on the
  maintainer reversing the 2026-11-23 informational-job decision, exactly as
  R4 is conditional on D1.

In both cases the derived text must change with it: the planning record's W7
row, plan `:329-330`, `:340-342` and `:357`, the R3 FTR title, `:59-63` and
`:154-161`, the R2 FTR's `:52-58` and AC-3, and the `_open.adoc` card's "a
real compatibility gate".

=== 4. Blocking Finding 3 — the plan and R2 assert that "re-run the failed jobs" is already true of the implementation; R3 shows it is not

The plan, at `:146-148`, after the corrected recovery table:

[quote]
Because publication uses stored bytes, "re-run the failed jobs" is safe in
every state and no state needs a rebuild. *This is already true of the
implementation*; only the documented rule is wrong.

`FTR-WFC-RELEASE-CONTRACT-STEADY-STATE.adoc:178-180` repeats it ("already
true of `release.yml`"), and its AC-1 makes that table the chapter's recovery
section. But `FTR-WFC-RELEASE-PIPELINE-ROBUSTNESS.adoc:38-42` states the
opposite, correctly:

[quote]
`:371` calls `gh release create`, which fails when the release already
exists. After a partial success … the job cannot be re-run, and *the
corrected recovery rule R2 writes ("re-run the failed jobs") would not hold
for this one job*.

I confirmed the behaviour: `release.yml:371-374` calls `gh release create`
with no pre-check and no `--clobber`. So the recovery table's rows "TestPyPI
upload succeeded, a later step failed → re-run the failed jobs" and "PyPI
upload succeeded, a later job failed → re-run the failed jobs" are false for
the `release` job today. Since the plan's own prioritized order is R2 first
and R3 after it — and R2 is the slice the plan recommends if only one is ever
commissioned — R2 as specified would put a recovery instruction into the
owning chapter that does not work for the one job most likely to fail late,
for the whole window until R3 is integrated. What the plan presents as "only
the documented rule is wrong" is, for this job, also an implementation gap.

This is the one hidden coupling between the two slices the plan treats as
independent lanes: R3 depends on R2 for its specification, and R2's
correctness depends on R3 for one row of its table.

Correction (one sentence per document): drop the "already true of the
implementation" claim, and have R2 state the `release`-job caveat the way it
states R1's and R3's other work — as planned ("re-run the failed jobs holds
for every job once R3's idempotent `release` lands; until then a partially
created GitHub Release must be completed by hand"). R3's W1 item already
carries the fix; only the claim and the chapter wording need to acknowledge
the order.
