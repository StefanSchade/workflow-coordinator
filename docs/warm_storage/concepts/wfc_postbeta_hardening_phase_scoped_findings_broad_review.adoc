=== 1. The stated problem reproduces at the base HEAD

At `1e2c9b3` the `findings` item schema was built from
`[item.value for item in FindingState]` for every phase and both delta shapes,
and v3's authoritative `transition` shape, with all four targets, was projected
unconditionally (base-HEAD `structured_result_schema.py:49`, `:62-63` and
`:70-80`).
`reducer.py:1693-1703` meanwhile gives `permitted_operations = ()` to every
phase that is not a gate review, so the projected contract was strictly wider
than the admitted authority for every Worker Hop.

The cost is measured, not presumed. The P1 plan's Run facts
(`docs/backlog/postbeta_plan_p1_adr_and_hardening.adoc:154-183`) name the four
archived stops and their exact reducer messages:

* `RUN-69816e71`, `broad_review`: `gate cannot create Finding F001 as residual`
  -- a *creation* delta from a gate that holds `resolve` only.
* `RUN-fd571798` and `RUN-18a6ae05`, `correct`:
  `agent cannot perform Finding operation resolve`
* `RUN-7ec65677`, `correct`: `agent cannot perform Finding operation residual`

The three `cannot perform ... operation` messages come from the
*existing*-Finding branch (`reducer.py:645-651`), the first from the creation
branch (`reducer.py:590-595`). So one of the four is a creation shape and three
are existing-delta shapes; a narrowing that touched only creation states, or
only v3's explicit transition shape, would not have prevented three of them.

=== 2. Which contract version the narrowing has to reach

`select_result_contract` returns a kind; `model.result_contract_version`
(`model.py:711-729`) turns it into the wire version, and for
`profile.schema_version == 2` that is **1** for all three kinds. Every rendered
consumer profile is `schema_version: 2` -- `commissioning.py:352`, and a scan of
`governance/commissions/*/profile.yaml` gives 104 of 104 at version 2. Contract
**v1** is therefore the version every real Run dispatches, including the four
that stopped, and v1 uses the *combined* existing-Finding shape rather than
v3's separate citation/transition split.

I checked this because the task's "current versions only" rule reads at first
like a reason to leave v1 alone. It is the opposite: v1 is the current
production wire version, v2 is reachable for a convergence-role phase under a
`schema_version: 3` profile, and v3 is reachable for `work-result` /
`review-verdict` under such a profile. All three are produced by the one shared
builder, and `admits_result_contract_version` defines no fourth. Nothing
historical was touched (<<h002-identity>>).

=== 4. The narrowing matches the reducer, and is derived from one fact

=== 8. Non-blocking observations

[[h002-o1]]
*O1 (for the maintainer). The acceptance criterion's literal reading cannot be
met on the v1/v2 shape, and is met in substance.* The criterion asks that for an
empty authority "the generated findings item schema admits no authoritative
state transition". Under v3 that is literally true: the transition shape is
absent. Under v1/v2 one shape carries both meanings -- an existing-Finding delta
with `proposed_state: open` is an evidence-only same-state citation when the
cited Finding is open and the `reopen` operation when it is resolved
(`reducer.py:478-483`) -- and the projection cannot know the cited Finding's
state. I reproduced it: that payload is still schema-valid under an empty
authority, and the reducer still refuses it. Removing the ambiguity would mean
dropping the existing-Finding shape whenever the authority is empty, which would
also remove the same-state citation of an *open* Finding that the Agent
Operating Contract explicitly sanctions. Constraining `new_material_evidence` to
`false` instead would not remove the reopen attempt either, only its justified
form, and would convert an authority stop into a
`reopening requires new material evidence` stop. So the candidate's reading --
no *disposition* state is admitted, and `open` always stays -- is the achievable
one, `reopen` was not among the four measured stops, and the expressible
unauthorized surface is strictly smaller than before rather than unchanged. I
record this as the limit of the criterion rather than as a waiver; the
maintainer may want to restate the criterion in those terms.

*O2. One live capability is lost as the forced flip side of O1.* Because the
v1/v2 `proposed_state` enum governs both the creation state and the
existing-delta target, a phase can no longer cite a Finding that is *already* in
a state its authority does not cover. Concretely, in `robust@v1` a `correct`
phase can no longer attach evidence to an already-resolved Finding, and
`broad_review` / `focused_review` can no longer attach evidence to a residual or
superseded one. Admitting those back would re-admit three of the four measured
stops, so it is not a simpler alternative but a worse one; the AOC sanctions
only the citation of an *open* Finding and tells a correction phase to report
correction evidence in the normal result fields instead; and a residual or
superseded Finding can arise in `robust@v1` only by human disposition, which a
human can repeat. The candidate states this limit in
`structured_result_schema.py:62-72` and in the FTR. Nothing is owed.

*O3. The `finding_authority=None` tri-state defaults in the widening
direction.* `None` means "caller states no authority" and yields the *widest*
surface, on exactly the surface being hardened, so a future second dispatch path
that forgets the argument fails open rather than closed. I checked the cost of
removing the sentinel: `finding_authority` would have to become a required
keyword like `permitted_routes`, which touches about 24 call sites, nearly all
of them in `tests/unit/test_slice4.py` and the Codex conformance suite, and
`()` as the dataclass default would narrow those existing expectations instead.
Given one production dispatch path, covered by
`test_dispatch_carries_the_admitted_phase_finding_authority` plus both
dispatched-artifact tests, the sentinel is the proportionate choice and the two
docstrings state it. Recorded, not owed.

*O4. `codex_adapter.routing_result_openai_schema` and
`projected_result_openai_schema` are unreachable and were left un-narrowed.* No
caller in `src/` or `tests/` reaches either (only the former calls the latter).
The candidate flagged this and left them alone as the smaller change, which is
right for this WorkItem; the divergence would end by deleting dead code, which
is a separate concern.

*O5. A future `FindingState` without an operation raises `KeyError`, not
`StructuredResultSchemaError`.* `_proposed_states` indexes
`_DISPOSED_STATE_OPERATIONS` directly (`structured_result_schema.py:78`). The
comment at `:43-50` says the failure is deliberate, and failing loudly is the
right direction, but it surfaces as an unhandled exception rather than the
module's own error type, which the Codex adapter would otherwise translate. One
line if the maintainer ever wants it; not worth a guard today.

*O6. The Codex test's justification comment overstates O1, and the FTR header is
now stale.* `test_codex_adapter_conformance.py` says of the empty-authority case
that "the only state left is `open`, which needs none" -- true for a creation
delta, not for an existing delta against a disposed Finding. The assertions are
correct; only the comment is loose, and both the product comment and the FTR
state the limit accurately. Separately, and for the maintainer: the FTR's
`Status::` line is unchanged as the task requires, but the adjacent
`Next action::` and `Not authorized here:: Result-schema implementation or a
Run` lines in the same maintainer-owned header now read as stale against the
dated implementation section below them.

*On `ruff format --check`:* it fails on seven of the eight changed files, and it
fails identically at the base HEAD -- the repository's `format-check` target
(`Makefile:22-26`) deliberately covers five unrelated files, so these files have
never been `ruff format` clean. Satisfying the criterion literally would mean
reformatting whole files well outside this scope. `ruff check`, which is the
repository's actual lint gate (`Makefile:19-20`), passes on every changed file.
Not a correction owed; noted so the maintainer can narrow the criterion's
wording.

=== 9. Simplicity assessment of the change itself

123 changed lines in `structured_result_schema.py`, of which the substance is
three small functions and two call-site guards; 18 changed lines of plumbing
across `adapter.py`, `vertical.py` and the two adapters, roughly half of them
comment; no new state, no new
abstraction layer, no parameter threaded through a module that did not already
carry the request. I looked for a smaller reaching change and agree with the
candidate's conclusion that there is none inside the authorized scope:
`select_result_contract` has `finding_authority` in its selection key but
returns only a kind, so using that seam would require `model.py`, which is
withheld. I also checked whether the v1/v2 part could be dropped as
forward-looking -- it cannot; it is where all four measured stops live
(sections 2 and 3). Conversely the v3 part currently applies to
no live profile (104 of 104 at `schema_version: 2`), but it is eight lines in
the same shared builder, it is where the acceptance criterion is literally
demonstrable, and leaving it out would make the builder narrow one contract and
not another for no stated reason. No duplicated responsibility, no redundant
safeguard and no recurring operational cost found.
