== 1. Disposition

Outcome:: `changes_required`
Route:: `correction`

The H005 correction fixes F002's central runtime-classification defect.  The
adapter now classifies only observable process facts, does not promote a
post-start sandbox refusal to nonzero transport, and preserves the fail-closed
provider controls without inventing a tool-execution oracle.  The failure table
and fake-test plan say the same thing consistently
(`design:142-144,164-166,227-241`).

F002 cannot yet be resolved because the corresponding real-provider smoke is
internally impossible as written.  Section 7 still requires _exactly one_
`claude -p` invocation, but that one invocation must exercise the ordinary
successful edit/test path, characterize startup sandbox unavailability, and
characterize a post-start sandboxed-command refusal (`design:245`).  A process
that is unavailable at startup cannot also start, perform the normal task, and
later exhibit a post-start refusal.  Recording the actual exit and wrapper for
both states therefore requires separate process scenarios.  This is a narrow
test-strategy defect, but it blocks an implementable release gate and leaves
F002 open.

The correction should replace the one-invocation claim with the minimum
separate, separately authorized pinned-CLI scenarios needed to observe the
normal successful wrapper/edit/test path, startup unavailability, and a
post-start sandboxed-command refusal.  Startup unavailability and a post-start
state necessarily require different processes; the ordinary success checks may
be separate or combined with the post-start-capable scenario only where the
observations remain unambiguous.  Each failure scenario must record actual
process status and wrapper behavior without presupposing nonzero, and must prove
no unsandboxed retry.  No production, test, governance, schema, Runtime,
Evidence, programme, telemetry, or provider-framework change is needed in this
design Hop.

The inspection and checks recorded here are reviewer-side diagnostics.  They
do not replace Coordinator-owned `task-check` evidence.

== 3. Finding disposition

=== 3.1 F002 -- observable transport facts and feasible qualification

Severity:: *BLOCKING.*
Disposition:: *OPEN; one bounded correction remains.*

Invariant:: The adapter design must classify only observable provider process
and wrapper facts as transport outcomes.  A provider-side tool refusal must
remain fail-closed without being promoted to a guaranteed nonzero exit when the
documented CLI can continue.  The release qualification for those distinct
states must itself be executable and must record rather than assume the process
outcome.

==== 3.1.1 Corrected runtime behavior

The runtime design now satisfies the first part of the invariant:

. Adapter-created schema/settings write failure and local launch failure are
  separated from an observed process outcome (`design:166`).
. Only an observed nonzero status or timeout is classified as failed
  transport; zero proceeds to wrapper decoding and the unchanged WFC semantic
  validator (`design:146-166`).
. A post-start command refusal or sandbox loss can leave Claude running.  The
  adapter does not synthesize nonzero status without a documented, verified
  machine-readable signal (`design:166`).
. The security table preserves `failIfUnavailable` and
  `allowUnsandboxedCommands: false`, but records only the observed
  exit/wrapper/tails and explicitly rejects a provider-independent
  tool-execution oracle (`design:227-231`).
. Fake coverage includes a zero-exit wrapper after a provider-side refusal and
  asserts that the adapter neither manufactures nonzero transport nor requests
  an unsandboxed retry (`design:241`).

The correction also acknowledges that invalid settings can be silently ignored
in print mode and assigns exact-profile compatibility to the pinned-CLI gate,
not to an invented pre-`Popen` observation (`design:166`).  The explicit tool
surface is correctly counted as six (`design:113,241`).

==== 3.1.2 Remaining counterexample

The real smoke is specified as “exactly one pinned `claude -p` invocation” and
requires that invocation to:

* produce the successful structured wrapper and complete an authorized edit
  and sandboxed test command;
* characterize startup sandbox unavailability; and
* characterize a post-start sandboxed-command refusal, with actual exit status
  and wrapper behavior (`design:245`).

These are mutually exclusive process states.  Startup unavailability ends the
scenario before a post-start refusal or successful task can occur.  A fake can
exercise wrapper branches, but it cannot qualify the pinned real CLI/platform
behavior that §7 makes release-gating.  An implementation following the design
literally must either violate the one-invocation bound or omit at least one
required observation.

==== 3.1.3 Required correction

Correct §7, and the §8 acceptance criterion only if needed, as follows:

. Define a small smoke _suite_ with at least separate startup-unavailability and
  post-start-capable pinned-CLI processes.  The normal success checks may be a
  third scenario or may share the latter only when their observations stay
  unambiguous.
. Keep every invocation separately authorized, disposable, non-secret, and
  outside this design Run.  Do not convert the smoke into a production Run.
. For both sandbox-failure scenarios, record actual exit status and wrapper
  behavior and prove no unsandboxed retry; do not prescribe nonzero.
. Preserve the existing fake cases for nonzero-with-valid-JSON,
  timeout-with-partial-JSON, missing/malformed wrapper, absent structured
  output, and exact correlation mismatch.
. Do not add a transcript parser, provider-independent tool oracle, registry,
  telemetry schema, Coordinator policy branch, or fallback.

This is new material evidence on existing Current-Run Finding `F002`, not a new
Finding.
