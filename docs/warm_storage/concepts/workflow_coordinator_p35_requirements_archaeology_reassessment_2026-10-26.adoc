== Commission conclusion and reader's map

*Conclusion shape: escalate a precise unresolved requirements decision to the
maintainer.*  The original Concept contains both a literal every-path target
sentence and an apparently conflicting ``could never'' boundary sentence.
`f31a423` adds ordered, opener-relative scope; it is not the first literal
universal quantifier.  The incident establishes missing disposition
opportunity and review_001 establishes strict ordering, but neither resolves
that textual conflict or supplies a safe/unsafe-branch incident.  The
maintainer must choose universal no-dead-end admission or the defined
existential availability predicate in section E.  Existing reducer completion
protection remains a separate stateful backstop.

* Section A (<<archaeology>>) separates `965d14d`'s first literal every-path
  wording from `f31a423`'s ordering/opener-relative rewrite and adjudicates
  the original section's conflicting boundary language.
* Section B (<<fidelity>>) tests the candidate requirement against the actual
  S3-002 profile and worked branching, carrying, loop, and selectability
  cases.
* Section C (<<placement>>) locates a static topology check at the parsed
  profile / operator-preflight composition boundary, if the maintainer adopts

* Section D (<<previous>>) independently re-evaluates the four deferred

This is a recommendation for the still-pending P3.5 decision only.  It does

== A. Historical archaeology [[archaeology]]

=== Documentary chain

The initial Concept was introduced at `37cf58c` and P3.5 was added/refined at
`965d14d` (Git commit metadata and diffs).  At `965d14d`, Concept §11 stated:
``if a reachable workflow state can carry an open blocking Finding into a gate
whose successful route requires disposition of that Finding, some phase on
every path to that gate must possess`` the needed operation.  That is both
conditioned on a particular gate needing disposition and does not say that the
authority must occur after the opening invocation.  Crucially, it is already a
literal universal quantifier: ``some phase on *every path* to that gate''.
Thus `965d14d`, not `f31a423`, is the earliest located written all-path
wording.  Its trigger is nevertheless conditional on a gate whose successful
route requires disposition, unlike the current opener-relative formulation.

The same `965d14d` §11 immediately states the boundary that P3.5 ``only
rejects graphs that could never have reached a legal completion regardless of
any Run's actual history'' (lines 429--439).  This is direct
existential-looking language: a graph with one legal repair continuation has
not *never* been able to reach legal completion.  The document does not say
how to reconcile that boundary with the preceding every-path sentence.  This
is a genuine internal tension in the original written record, not evidence
that either reading can simply be called its sole intent.

The observed BUG is likewise narrower than a safe/unsafe branching rule.  Its
factual history followed `review -> correct -> rereview -> accept -> complete`:
S3-002 opened three Findings, correction had no review authority,
and `rereview` had `finding_authority: []` and could not close them
(BUG, ``Observed behavior (S3-002 factual trigger)'').  The historical-evidence
paragraph says the same empty tuple was latent in S1/S2/S3-001 because no
Run-native blocker had previously been created.  The actual cited YAML is *not
linear*: both `review` and `rereview` are gate/blocking-only phases with empty
authority and both declare `accept: complete` plus `correct: goto correct`
(`governance/self-development/...-s3-002-profile.yaml`, phases `review`,
`correct`, and `rereview`).  Neither the BUG nor that YAML is the
distinguishing safe/unsafe branch; it is direct evidence for absence of any
later legal opportunity after the exercised opening state, not direct evidence
for rejecting a topology solely because one alternative continuation lacks a
disposer.

The decisive *scope and ordering* change is `f31a423`, ``docs(wfc): correct P3.5
conformance criteria''.  Its full commit message attributes the Section-11
change to review_001 finding 4.2, and its diff replaces the conditional
`965d14d` property with: for every `P_open`, and *for every forward path* that
may reach a completion/accept gate while the Finding remains open, a phase
*strictly reachable after* `P_open` on that same path must dispose it.  This
changes the reference point to each opener, requires a phase strictly after
it, and applies to every forward checkpoint-reaching path from it.  It
therefore broadens/rewrites the conditional gate formulation into the current
opener-relative ordered property; it does *not* introduce the first literal
all-path quantifier.  It was accepted as part of the corrected Concept by that
development act; later design acceptance is acceptance of the then-current
Concept, not independent original-requirement evidence.

Review_001 §4.2 supplies the stated justification.  Its counterexample is
linear: `independent_review [resolve] -> correction [none] -> rereview [none]
-> accept -> complete`.  The old membership language counted the opener's
`resolve`, even though its invocation had already executed.  The review's
smallest recommended correction says ``for every forward path'' and ``strictly
reachable after''.  The counterexample demonstrably requires the latter
ordering repair.  It does *not* distinguish a profile with no later disposer
at all from a branch where a later disposer is legally selectable on another
route: it has only one continuation.  Therefore the documentary evidence
establishes why `f31a423` used the broader words, but does not establish that
the broader quantifier was necessary to cure §4.2's false negative.  A
condition-preserving correction could instead have required a strictly later,
legally reachable disposition opportunity before a completion attempt that
would carry the blocker.

The P3.5 proposal began at `fa308c3`.  Its §4.4/§4.5 algorithm already
expressed universal checkpoint-path rejection (backward `danger` propagation)
and §4.7 claimed every statically enumerable forward path passes a later
disposer.  This is a restatement/implementation of the post-`f31a423` Concept,
not an earlier source of it.  `6075417` corrected the proposal's same-Hop
clean-checkpoint hole: it added an origin-checkpoint arm so a disposer on an
opener cannot cure a blocker newly opened by that same Hop.  `8fc44ee` refined
version-specific opener identification and diagnostics; `6807d56` changed the
v2 proxy from empty-disposition to the reviewed/blocking-only opener set and
thereby recorded the 43/0 outcome.  These diffs change precision, origin
handling, and compatibility breadth, not the Concept's already-universal
quantifier.  Design review 003 accepted the corrected design; that is a
decision accepting the design's fidelity to the current Concept, not evidence
that the earlier escalation was intended.

The prior reassessment and review are useful documentary inputs only.  Its
`P35SIR-001` correction accurately says the *accepted* rule rejects if any
checkpoint-reaching path lacks a later disposer
(`...structural_invariant_reassessment...adoc`, §1 and H003 correction
record).  It did not investigate whether `f31a423`'s change from the original
conditional text was warranted, so it cannot decide that prior question.

=== Interpretation

Two conclusions follow without relying on a recollection of intent: (1) the
original written requirement contained a conditional every-path sentence *and*
a conflicting existential-looking boundary; (2) `f31a423` first supplied the
current opener-relative strict-after scope, not the first universal wording.
The historical review supplied a sound *order* defect, but the record contains
no incident or stated product requirement involving a safe and unsafe branch
that resolves the original tension.  The archaeology therefore cannot prove
either that universal rejection was an unintended escalation or that it was
the uniquely intended original requirement.

== B. Requirements fidelity [[fidelity]]

=== What the engine and the incident require

Finding creation and disposition are different facts.  `_finding_operation`
maps creation of `OPEN` to no disposition operation, while transitions from
open to resolved/residual/superseded require named operations
(`src/workflow_coordinator/reducer.py:470-487`).  `_may_dispose` permits one
only to human authority or a gate whose current phase grants it
(`reducer.py:489-497`).  A later phase with `resolve`, `residual`, or
`supersede` is therefore an opportunity, not proof that an agent will use it.
The S3-002 path has no such later legal opportunity, so both the candidate
existential rule and the accepted universal rule correctly reject it.

The following minimal cases make the difference explicit.  `O` can create an
open blocking Finding; `D` has a suitable disposition operation; `C` has a
complete route.  Edges are routes actually declared by the profile.

[source,text]
----
1. Incident/order: O -> C                         (reject: no later D)
2. Review_001:     O[resolve] -> C                (reject: same invocation
                                                    is not later)
3. Branch:          O -> D -> C
                       \-> C
4. Loop:            O -> work -> O -> D -> C      (the second O is later)
----

Case 3 is the legitimate counterexample the candidate hypotheses leave open.
Read per phase as hypotheses 2--3 say, `D` is legally reachable from `O`, so
the existential rule accepts; the universal rule rejects the `O -> C` branch.
That result is not a proof that universal rejection is required: in the
actual reducer, an agent that opens the blocker and selects `O -> C` reaches
`_apply_route`'s complete guard, which stops for
`completion_blocked_by_open_blocking_findings` (`reducer.py:1145-1174`).  It
does not complete incorrectly, and the reducer does not choose `D` for the
agent.  An agent that needs disposition can instead select the already legal
`D` branch.  The history has no case 3: the BUG and review_001 §4.2 first
present a one-continuation topology, not this branching shape.

That does leave a real operational risk: an invocation can select the bad
branch and consume work before the fail-closed stop.  It is a reason a
maintainer might knowingly adopt universal no-dead-end admission, but it is
not the recorded original requirement and it is not completion safety.  It
must be stated as that additional policy, rather than smuggled in by a
stronger theorem.

The candidate formulation needs more precision than graph membership.  Here
is the complete alternative static predicate, stated so its difference from
the universal predicate is testable.  For each *origin configuration* `(P,s)`
that the profile declares or conservatively identifies as relevant, let `s` be
either `new` (P may create an open blocking Finding during this invocation) or
`carried` (an open blocking Finding may enter P and remain until its result is
applied).  There must exist an invocation node `D` reachable by a sequence of
actually declared, agent-selectable `goto` routes such that D grants an
operation that the reducer may use to dispose the relevant open blocking
state.  For `new`, the sequence has positive length--the opening invocation
cannot dispose the identity it is about to allocate.  For `carried`, it may
have length zero: the current invocation P itself is eligible because the
reducer applies its Finding delta before applying P's selected route
(`reduce_result`, `reducer.py:1152-1174).  `resolve`, `residual`, or
`supersede` may qualify only when legal for that state; `reopen` alone cannot.

The quantifier is existential over those route sequences, not universal over
each individual completion-reaching path.  An unreachable orphan D, an
`await_human` outcome, or a route unavailable to the phase is no witness.  A
cycle may be a witness: a return to O after one or more transitions is a later
invocation for `new`; it does not demand a further disposer after a carried
Finding has actually been disposed.  Thus case 1 and case 2 reject; the
S3-002 topology rejects because no D exists on either declared continuation;
case 3 admits because `O -> D` is a witness even though `O -> C` remains a
mis-selectable, fail-closed route; and case 4 admits through its later O/D
invocation.  This predicate intentionally does not predict route selection,
promise recovery, or turn a disposition-capable phase into a mandatory choice.

=== Result

The independent fidelity result is not a selection between the predicates.
The existential predicate above meets the actual BUG and review_001's order
defect; the universal predicate additionally rejects case 3's mis-selectable
dead-end.  The original Concept supplies evidence pointing in both directions,
and the record contains no branch incident to decide between them.  Section E
therefore escalates that exact choice rather than treating a stronger theorem
as an unstated requirement.

== C. Architectural placement [[placement]]

If adopted, this is still a static composition property of a parsed
`WorkflowProfile`: phases, routes, review descriptors, and
`finding_authority`.  `_review` validates local closed operations and
gate/advisory coherence (`serialization.py:548-599`); `_route` validates a
route's own shape (`serialization.py:601-628`).  Neither is the right place
to decide cross-phase reachability.  `operator._preflight` already parses the
pinned profile and performs cross-document, pre-Run checks
(`operator.py:281-367`), matching Concept §11's ``where this belongs''
paragraph.  A pure parsed-profile validator called from that preflight is the
least new boundary; placing a helper module elsewhere does not change this
semantic location.

This recommendation separates where validation runs from what it validates.
The validator reports topology; the invocation chooses a requested route; and
`reduce_result` applies the actual state transition.  The reducer must retain
its stateful permission and completion checks, not invent a `D` route or
disposition.  No Commission-specific or YAML-parse-time policy is needed to
make that separation work.

== D. Reevaluate the previous reassessment [[previous]]

The prior reassessment's core observation is independently verified: the
completion guard makes the harm from an actual undisposed blocker a stopped
Run rather than incorrect completion (`reducer.py:1145-1174`).  But the
candidate existential rule changes its cost analysis materially: a profile
with an actual selectable repair route is not rejected just because another
route lacks one.

. *1. Creation declarations and runtime enforcement.*  Explicit declarations
  can improve static precision, especially for legacy creators, but they are
  not required to express the narrowed availability rule where current profile
  facts already identify a relevant opener.  Runtime enforcement is a separate
  semantic change, not justified merely by revising a static requirement.
  Defer unless a later bounded design establishes its independent value.
. *2. Clean-GOTO guard.*  The existing `COMPLETE` guard is verified.  A new
  clean-GOTO guard would prevent carrying a blocker down a selected edge; that
  is a stronger execution constraint than the existential availability
  requirement and is not evidenced by the linear BUG.  Defer as a separate
  policy decision.
. *3. Static validation/preflight.*  A narrow validator remains potentially
  valuable: it can reject S3-002's no-opportunity topology before cost is
  incurred.  Its preflight integration belongs at the boundary in section C.
  Whether Level 0 needs it depends on the maintainer's tolerance for a
  fail-closed stopped Run, but the 43/0 result cannot by itself establish that
  a narrowed validator is disproportionate.
. *4. Legacy admission/migration.*  The prior design's migration burden is a
  consequence of its reviewed-opener/all-path predicate, not an invariant fact
  of all legacy YAML.  Preserve parse/replay/fixture history independently
  from new-launch admission, as `f31a423` and Concept §§18--19 already
  distinguish.  Do not require corpus-wide migration before identifying which
  currently used profiles actually lack any legal later repair route.

The reported 43 rejections / 0 acceptances is a historical snapshot of the
accepted universal design, not a count for this alternative predicate.  It is
reproducible from `6807d56`: `git ls-tree -r --name-only 6807d56
governance/self-development | rg -- '-profile\\.yaml$'` yields 43 profiles,
and design §6.1 says each is rejected by the v2 reviewed-`blocking_only` /
local-`COMPLETE` proxy.  The current tree has 45 such profiles: the two added
after that snapshot are this Commission's
`p35-requirements-archaeology-reassessment-001-profile.yaml` and the prior
reassessment's `p35-structural-invariant-reassessment-001-profile.yaml`
(`git diff --name-status 6807d56..HEAD -- governance/self-development`).  The
43/0 result must therefore neither be silently re-described as a current
launch census nor extrapolated to the two later profiles.

Repository evidence supports these concrete compatibility categories.  Eight
correction-convergence lineages (S1-001 through S3-002) are mandatory
regression examples whose production-preflight assertions the Concept/design
already names as expected to flip under the accepted rule.  Eleven named
additional files--the design, design-revision, compatibility-shim,
immutable-echo, P1, and scope-escalation fixtures listed in accepted-design
§6.1--already fail the separate production evidence-admission predicate and
are historical-inspection fixtures.  These nineteen are not evidence that a
newly launch-admissible bundle would become rejected; their parse, replay, and
fixture roles remain separately preservable (design §6.3).  This is direct
repository classification, not an inference from the 43 count.

For the remaining 24 profiles from the `6807d56` snapshot, and for the two
post-snapshot P3.5 Commission profiles, the repository establishes their
presence but supplies no launch ledger or operational-use marker from which to
claim current launch demand.  The records examined do establish one concrete
new-launch-relevant category: these two P3.5 profiles were added to commission
the two pending reassessments, not as historical fixtures.  Their migration
need is nevertheless *not established*: it depends on which predicate the
maintainer selects and, under the existential alternative, on evaluating their
actual selectable disposition witnesses.  The same is true for the 24
unclassified profiles.  Thus no profile is shown by present evidence to
genuinely require migration under the recommended unresolved decision; the
proper residual is ``usage and predicate-dependent, unresolved'', not
``migrate all'' or ``migrate none''.  S3-002 remains a directly verified
no-opportunity instance, but does not classify the remainder.

== E. Recommendation [[recommendation]]

Escalate this precise requirements decision to the maintainer: does the
original Concept's conditional ``every path to that gate'' sentence control,
making universal no-dead-end admission the intended P3.5 policy; or does its
immediately following ``could never have reached a legal completion'' boundary
control, making the following existential availability predicate the intended
one?  The written record does not resolve their conflict, and neither the BUG
nor review_001 supplies the missing safe/unsafe branch case.  This is not a
request to change the accepted design absent that decision.

If the maintainer selects the existential alternative, adopt the static
predicate in section B verbatim: for every relevant `(P,new)` origin there is
a legally selectable disposition invocation reachable after P, and for every
relevant `(P,carried)` origin there is one reachable at or after P; the
operation must be state-legal.  It rejects orphan and S3-002 no-opportunity
topologies and admits case 3 because `O -> D` is a witness, while leaving the
direct `O -> C` misselection to the existing completion guard.  It has no
path-relative ``before completion'' clause and does not substitute routes.

If the maintainer instead selects universal admission, state explicitly that
the additional policy is ``no legally selectable forward continuation may
strand an open blocker before completion'', accepting its branch/corpus
consequences.  That policy is coherent and compatible with `965d14d`'s target
sentence, but archaeology cannot establish it as the uniquely intended
reading.  Either choice retains review_001's strict-after correction for a
newly created Finding and permits a current carried-Finding disposer as
defined in section B.
