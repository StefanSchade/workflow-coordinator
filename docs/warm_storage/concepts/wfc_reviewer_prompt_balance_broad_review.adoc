== The prompt text

`reviewer.txt` is purely additive (20 insertions, zero deletions, 21 -> 41
lines), so every surviving statement is kept byte-for-byte: the opening
mandate, the rule that "`A reproduced violation of an explicit acceptance
criterion is a blocking Finding. Do not downgrade it because the presumed risk
is low, the behavior already exists, or correction would be costly.`", the
maintainer-escalation sentence, and the whole offline/exploratory-evidence
paragraph.

The addition is two paragraphs in the prompt's existing plain style and covers
the maintainer's instruction in substance: simplicity and maintainability
alongside correctness and compliance; the active search for a materially
simpler route to the *same authorized* objectives and guarantees, with the
named smells (unnecessary abstractions, redundant safeguards, duplicated
responsibilities, avoidable state, recurring operational or maintenance
burden); the explicit cost-class split (high one-time implementation, token and
review effort is acceptable when it buys a better lasting result, while lasting
complexity, additional failure states and operational friction need critical
assessment); the two guiding thoughts that a safeguard is not good merely
because it excludes further hypothetical failure states and that a working
strong invariant such as a simplifying immutability is not given up without
reason; the evidential bar for a blocking maintainability Finding (a concrete
cost or defect *and* a feasible simpler alternative) with "`stylistic
preference does not block`"; and "`Do not introduce new requirements of your
own.`"

Checked for the three failure modes the task names:

* *Waiver.* The criterion-appropriateness sentence cannot be read as a waiver.
  It routes the question to "`the maintainer as above`" -- the escalation route
  the prompt already named three lines earlier -- or to a non-blocking
  observation, and then says in the same sentence that "`it never waives a
  reproduced violation of that criterion and never justifies adding further
  mechanism to satisfy it.`" The surviving no-downgrade sentence stands
  immediately above it, unqualified. The tension the task asked to resolve is
  therefore resolved inside the prompt, not left to the reader.
* *Subjective blocking.* The blocking bar for the new goal is evidential and
  narrower than the general one, and it is scoped to maintainability Findings,
  so it does not weaken the criterion rule for other Findings.
* *New mechanism.* No new route, phase, Finding class or artifact kind is
  named; both outlets (maintainer escalation, non-blocking observation in the
  review artifact) already exist, the second one literally in
  `.wfc/governance/repository-policy.md`.

No contradiction with the repository policy was found. The new text reinforces
"`Keep the change proportionate and locally understandable`" and leaves the
test rules ("`a behavior change needs a test that fails without it`", "`Never
weaken or delete a test to make a change pass`"), the scope and Git rules, and
the Finding-authority rule untouched; a reviewer enforcing those existing,
phase-bound rules is not "`introducing new requirements of your own`". The
vocabulary shift between "`explicit`" and "`authorized`" acceptance criterion
matches the operating contract's own wording and names the same object.
41 lines with one blank line between five short paragraphs still reads at a
glance.

== Non-blocking observations

. *The developer guide's reading attribution is loose.*
  `docs/developer_guide/ch02_code_navigation.adoc` says the role texts are
  "`the single live source: `prompt.py` and `commissioning.py` read only those
  bytes`" and that the frozen copies are "`frozen historical copies that
  nothing reads`". Both claims are right where they matter and slightly
  overstated in mechanism. `prompt.py` reads one package resource,
  `resources/agent_operating_contract_v6.txt`, not `resources/roles/*.txt`; at
  Hop time the role text reaches the prompt as a `PromptComponent` that
  `operator.py` loads from the Commission's own materialized
  `components/<id>.txt` at the pinned head (`operator.py:489-501`), which
  `commissioning.py` had rendered from the digest-checked package resource.
  And `operator.py` would read the frozen copies if a components manifest named
  them -- `governance/self-development/components.yaml` still does, and the
  hand-authored schema-v2 profiles beside it still exist -- so the accurate
  statement is the technical model's narrower "`no compile path reads`" them.
  Not blocking: the operative instruction the paragraph exists to give (edit
  the package resource, update the pin; editing the frozen copy has no effect)
  is correct, and no acceptance criterion is violated. Worth tightening
  whenever that paragraph is next touched.
. *Already committed Commissions no longer re-verify against the new bytes.*
  `frontdoor.verify.verify_commission` re-compiles a Commission from the
  package resources and compares *every* compiled byte against the committed
  copy (`verify.py:74-82`). Every committed
  `.wfc/governance/*/components/reviewer.txt`, including this Run's own
  `wfc-reviewer-prompt-balance-001`, holds the old reviewer bytes and the old
  digest in its `commission-manifest.json`, so re-verifying any existing
  Commission at a head containing this change fails with `compiled projection
  equivalence mismatch`. This is the inherent consequence of changing a
  digest-pinned role resource -- the documentation the candidate adds says so
  ("`a new implementation identity`") -- and not a defect of the candidate; a
  Runtime promotion and a fresh Commission are withheld authority for this
  WorkItem. Recorded so the maintainer sequences the promotion and
  re-commissioning deliberately rather than discovering the mismatch in the
  next Run.
