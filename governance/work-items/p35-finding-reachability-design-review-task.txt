INDEPENDENT_REVIEW (phase `independent_review`, first phase of this Run)
======================================================================

Independently and adversarially attempt to falsify the design, with fresh
context. At minimum:

A. GRAPH ALGORITHM (design §4). Verify the `disposer`, `checkpoint`, and
   `creates_blocking` predicates and the `danger` backward-reachability
   fixpoint are each correctly specified and that the decision rule (§4.5)
   correctly implements "strictly reachable after the opening phase, not at
   or before it." Attempt to construct a minimal counterexample profile (as
   few phases as needed, expressed the way §7's own worked examples are) that
   the algorithm as specified would either (a) incorrectly ACCEPT -- a real
   dead end the algorithm misses -- or (b) incorrectly REJECT -- a safe
   profile the algorithm over-flags. Pay particular attention to: cycles
   where the only disposer is reached by revisiting the same phase (§4.4's
   own cited exception); guarded routes
   (`require_no_open_blocking_findings`) and whether §4.6's claimed
   checkpoint-but-not-edge asymmetry is actually sound; a `supersede`
   disposition whose successor Finding is itself left open (§4.7, example 7)
   -- confirm the design is correct that this is genuinely undecidable
   statically, not merely asserted as such; and the `AWAIT_HUMAN`/
   `SUSPEND_FOR_PREREQUISITE` vacuous-acceptance boundary (§4.7, example 8) --
   confirm this is a deliberate, correctly-scoped boundary and not a gap the
   design mischaracterizes.
B. AUTHORITY SEMANTICS (design §3, §5.3). Verify the claimed independence of
   `may_open_findings` (Finding-creation capability) from
   `review.finding_authority` (Finding-disposition capability) against
   current reducer/serialization behavior (§2 of the design cites exact line
   ranges -- check they are accurate). Verify the no-safe-default rationale
   for requiring a new schema version (§3.1.1) is sound: would EITHER a
   default-true OR a default-false actually misrepresent authorial intent
   for some currently-accepted profile, as claimed? Verify §5.3's proposed
   runtime enforcement (rejecting an unauthorized Finding-creation attempt)
   is genuinely fail-closed and matches the existing mechanical-preemption
   convention it claims to mirror.
C. COMPATIBILITY AND MIGRATION (design §6, §10 item 1). The design's own §10
   flags, as unconfirmed, that no schema-v2/v3 profile OUTSIDE the eight
   named lineages would be collaterally rejected by the proposed
   `creates_blocking(phase) := phase.review is not None` proxy. Actually
   check this claim against a representative set of currently-accepted
   self-development profiles (not only the eight named ones) by reasoning
   through each profile's phase graph under the proxy and the decision rule.
   Report concretely whether the claim holds, and if any profile outside the
   eight would newly fail, name it precisely.
D. SCOPE (design §1.3, §10). Confirm the design does not reopen P1-P3
   (`WORK_RESULT`/`REVIEW_VERDICT` admission, result-contract transport,
   provider adapters), does not introduce a Finding-kind taxonomy, does not
   become a general workflow DSL/liveness/termination checker, does not
   decide hierarchical escalation or nested Runs, and does not close
   BUG-WFC-SELF-DEVELOPMENT-GATE-FINDING-AUTHORITY. Confirm the design
   accurately treats the non-normative hierarchical-escalation hypothesis
   (`hierarchical_escalation_and_reconciliation_hypothesis_2026-10-17.adoc`)
   as granting it no authority to broaden P3.5.
E. OPEN QUESTIONS (design §10). For each of the six listed items, assess
   whether leaving it open (rather than resolving it now) is appropriate for
   a design document at this stage, or whether any of them is actually a
   precondition for the design to be soundly implementable at all.
