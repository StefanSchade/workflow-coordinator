MAINTAINER'S CANDIDATE REQUIREMENTS -- HYPOTHESES TO TEST, NOT CONCLUSIONS
======================================================================

Treat the following as hypotheses to test against evidence, not as
requirements to enforce or as a predetermined replacement for the accepted
design:

 1. The subject is static connectivity, reachability, and disposition
    authority in the configured workflow profile. The validator does not
    predict the route chosen during a concrete Run.
 2. Every phase that can be entered carrying an open blocking Finding, or
    can itself open one, must have at least one legally reachable
    disposition opportunity if an open Finding may remain upon leaving that
    phase.
 3. Alternative routes without a disposition opportunity are not inherently
    forbidden if a legally reachable repair route exists and the workflow
    can select it when necessary.

Do not reinterpret these statements as universal recovery, termination, or
successful LLM-behavior guarantees. Dynamic reducer completion safety
(`_has_open_blocker`/`_apply_route`, reducer.py) is a separate
responsibility and must not be used as the main argument for or against
this static structural requirement -- it may be cited as context, as the
prior reassessment did, but the central question here is requirements
fidelity, not runtime safety.

Do not add new requirements merely because they would make a stronger
formal theorem possible. Actively look for legitimate requirements these
three hypotheses might overlook -- do not treat them as exhaustive.

======================================================================
RESPONSIBILITY BOUNDARY -- PRESERVE UNLESS HISTORY AUTHORIZES OTHERWISE
======================================================================

Preserve the existing division of responsibility unless historical
evidence establishes an explicitly authorized reason to change it:

 * The workflow profile defines available phases, transitions, and
   authorities.
 * The agent invocation selects its requested route based on the actual
   outcome, including any Findings.
 * The reducer validates and executes the requested state transition. It
   does not invent an alternative route, synthesize a Finding disposition,
   or repair an inconsistent agent decision.
 * The proposed P3.5 static validator checks whether a legal disposition
   path is available in the configured topology. It does not select that
   path on behalf of the agent.

A phase that may open a blocking Finding may legitimately have both a
successful route to completion and a separate correction/disposition
route. The existence of the successful route is not, by itself, evidence
of a structural defect. If an agent opens a blocking Finding but requests a
route inconsistent with that state, distinguish this execution-level
inconsistency from the structural absence of any legal repair path.

Do not introduce universal-path requirements, mandatory route
substitution, reducer-side repair, or guaranteed autonomous recovery merely
to make the workflow appear fail-proof. However, treat these boundaries as
maintainer design intent to investigate, not as proof that the existential
invariant (hypothesis 2-3 above) is sufficient. Actively search the
original incident and historical requirements for a concrete counterexample
that would justify a stronger constraint. If one exists, document it and
state the additional requirement explicitly -- do not silently incorporate
it into P3.5.
