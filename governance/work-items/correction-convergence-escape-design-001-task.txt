WHY THIS DESIGN RUN IS BEING COMMISSIONED:

The existing bounded correction topology has exposed two distinct
convergence problems, detailed in the BUG above:

(1) Requirement carryover/identity loss -- Runs 001-003 show broad known
blocking obligations remaining open across repeated correction/review
cycles while narrower concrete findings got fixed, with the open obligation
frequently renamed/renumbered at each reviewing Hop.

(2) Failure of repeated local correction to close a broader invariant --
Run 004 deliberately introduced stable correction-requirement identifiers
`R1A`, `R1B`, `R2`, and `R3` plus explicit per-identifier completion/
carry-forward discipline. `R1B`, `R2`, and `R3` remained explicitly addressed
under their stable identities through H002, H004, and H006 and did not
silently disappear and later re-emerge under new names -- positive evidence
on problem (1). `R1A` remained explicitly the SAME open carried requirement
throughout: successive worker corrections closed the concrete R1A
counterexample raised by the immediately preceding reviewer, and successive
fresh reviewers nevertheless found progressively deeper counterexamples to
the same broad R1A invariant. Run 004 exhausted its 6/6 autonomous Hop
budget at H006 with R1A still open.

Problem (2) is this design's actual target. It is a generic convergence
problem about the correction/review strategy itself, not a defect in R1A's
specific content.

HARD CONSTRAINT -- preserve this throughout:

The design must NOT assume that the root cause of R1A is already known, and
must NOT solve R1A itself. R1A is evidence and a future dogfood case for the

FTR DESIGN QUESTIONS -- the design artifact must produce a concrete,
implementation-ready answer to each of the following, not merely acknowledge
it:

A. Domain-neutral boundary: define clearly what belongs to generic WFC Core;
   workflow profile/workflow strategy; domain-specific work semantics;
   project-specific policy/evidence/conventions; task-specific payload/
   acceptance requirements; and future Level-1 orchestration. The design
   must preserve WFC as domain-neutral -- software-development vocabulary
   such as "refactor", cyclomatic complexity, Python, or tests must not
   become generic Coordinator semantics. The mechanism must plausibly apply
   to other domains such as curriculum construction or business-analysis
   artifacts without changing generic Coordinator mechanics.
B. Structured correction obligations: design a machine-visible
   representation for stable blocking correction obligations. Explicitly
   evaluate whether this should be an extension of existing WFC Finding
   state, a distinct BlockingRequirement/CorrectionCoverage-style model, or
   another bounded representation -- and do not create a shadow semantic
   status model accidentally. Resolve ownership explicitly: who may
   introduce, claim coverage, carry forward, close, or supersede an
   obligation; what evidence is attached; how identity survives correction/
   review cycles. Worker completion claims must not themselves close
   reviewer-owned blockers.
C. Convergence facts and goalpost: define what mechanically observable
   state means that the normal correction/review strategy has reached its
   bounded convergence goalpost. Do not merely hard-code "three failures
   means root cause analysis". A bounded numeric threshold may be part of
   the design if justified, but it must not substitute for semantic
   acceptance or become a fake correctness metric. Separate clearly:
   autonomous Hop budget; semantic acceptance; correction-obligation
   lifecycle; and convergence-strategy goalpost.
D. Deterministic escape routing: define how the Coordinator can move from
   normal correction/review into a profile-declared convergence-escape
   path, preserving existing routing precedence and fail-closed authority.
   Profiles must remain finite declarative data. Do NOT put arbitrary
   executable conditions, code, computed graphs, or user-defined program
   logic into YAML. The Core may expose trusted convergence facts; the
   profile may declare finite routes/topology. Make the Core/profile split
   explicit.
E. Diagnosis phase: define the semantics and authority of a convergence-
   diagnosis phase. It should normally be read-only with respect to the
   subject artifact unless the design demonstrates why otherwise. It may
   analyze WHY normal correction is not converging. Potential generic
   classes may include concepts such as requirement ambiguity, problem/
   solution-model mismatch, decomposition mismatch, evidence gap,
   capability gap, authority/scope gap, regression chain, or unknown --
   these examples are not mandatory schema, and software-specific generic
   categories such as "bad refactor" must be avoided. State whether
   diagnosis classification is structured, prose, or both, and which parts
   are trusted versus agent-attributed.
F. Strategy revision/reframe: define what follows diagnosis. The mechanism
   should support a path conceptually like: normal revision -> evaluation ->
   convergence goalpost reached -> diagnosis -> strategy revision/reframe ->
   independent evaluation -> return to the original still-open requirement.
   The original requirement must not disappear merely because diagnosis/
   reframing occurs. A diagnosis or worker claim must not grant semantic
   acceptance.
G. Authority and scope: no convergence mechanism may autonomously widen
   authority. If diagnosis concludes that solving the problem requires
   broader mutation scope, new trust authority, a public/API decision, a
   project governance decision, profile redesign not already authorized, or
   other undelegated semantic authority, the existing appropriate stop/
   escalation boundary (see FTR-WFC-EXECUTION-TRUST-BOUNDARIES.adoc) must
   remain authoritative. Do not turn "root-cause mode" into an authority
   bypass.
H. Relationship to existing simple profiles: do NOT require replacing the
   existing simple bounded review loop. Design for coexistence: the simple
   iterative profile remains useful; a later convergence-aware profile may
   be added separately. Make clear whether profile choice is external
   commissioning/Level-1 semantics rather than runtime profile mutation --
   a profile remains pinned for a Run.
I. Persistence/wire/replay/compatibility: specify any required changes to
   Run, Hop, structured result schema, checkpoint projection, JSON
   persistence, prompt/context projection, replay semantics, and migration/
   backward compatibility. If no change is needed for one of these, say so
   explicitly. Historical Runs must remain truthfully readable and must not
   gain fabricated convergence state.
J. Evidence and acceptance: define focused deterministic tests sufficient
   to prove the mechanism. At minimum consider tests for: stable requirement
   identity across correction/rereview; worker coverage claims not closing
   reviewer-owned blockers; normal correction route before goalpost;
   deterministic convergence escape when goalpost is reached; no escape
   when the relevant requirement was actually closed; multiple simultaneous
   requirements with only one non-converging; newly discovered blockers
   versus carried blockers; no authority widening; profile-pinned finite
   topology; interruption/replay/persistence where applicable; ordinary
   simple profiles remaining behaviorally unchanged; and a domain-neutral
   fixture with no software vocabulary. Include a later real dogfood
   acceptance step using the still-open R1A case, but do NOT fix R1A in
   this design Run.
K. Implementation slicing: produce small implementation slices. Prefer
   something resembling: (1) structured correction-obligation state; (2)
   convergence facts/goalpost; (3) profile/routing support; (4) diagnosis/
   reframe support; (5) R1A dogfood; (6) later non-software-domain
   falsification if still useful. This is guidance, not a mandatory
   predetermined solution -- change the slicing if the architecture
   warrants it. Do not build Level 1 as part of this FTR.

STABLE BLOCKING-REQUIREMENT IDENTITY DISCIPLINE FOR THIS RUN (task-level
only -- this is NOT implementation of the future structured
BlockingRequirement mechanism design question B asks for; it is a bounded,
local prompt/task discipline for this Run's own ordinary review/correct/
rereview cycle, analogous to the stable-ID experiment Run 004 used for
R1A/R1B/R2/R3):

  design-review requirement a stable identifier `D001`, `D002`, `D003`, ...,
  each identifying the underlying design requirement/invariant the reviewer
  is blocking on, not merely the single concrete example that most recently
  demonstrated it. The review artifact must explicitly list every open
  blocking `Dxxx` identifier. Do not create a WFC Current-Run Finding merely
  to carry one of these ordinary design-review blockers; the existing rule
  of an empty `findings` delta on a `correct` route still applies.
- Before `correct` requests `rereview`, it must provide explicit coverage,
  in its own structured Hop summary and/or the design artifact, for every
  `Dxxx` identifier that was still open in the latest review history. For
  each such ID record at minimum: the identifier; a disposition of
  `addressed`, `blocked`, or `escalation`; what design change was made (or
  why none could be made); and a concrete artifact section/evidence
  reference. A worker's own `addressed` claim does NOT close the
  requirement -- only the independent reviewer disposes it.
- Each fresh `rereview` must explicitly disposition every `Dxxx` identifier
  that was open going into that Hop, under the SAME identifier: closed/
  addressed by the corrected design, still open, or (only where genuinely
  necessary) superseded by a clearly identified successor requirement. A
  newly discovered blocker gets a new `Dxxx` identifier; an existing
  identifier must never be silently renamed or dropped. Only the
  independent reviewer may authoritatively close a `Dxxx` requirement.

  that merely mentions every `Dxxx` identifier is not thereby accepted. The
  independent reviewer's substantive judgment of sufficiency remains the
  sole acceptance authority, exactly as in every other WFC review. This
  discipline is local to this commissioned Run's own task prose; it must
  not be described, in the design artifact or anywhere else, as
  already-implemented generic WFC functionality.
